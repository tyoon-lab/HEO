# HEO Manuscript Literature Review

**Date:** 2026-09-18

## Purpose

This file is the literature source map for the HEO manuscript. It is intended to support the Introduction, structural interpretation, electrochemical discussion, and reviewer defense. The manuscript remains synthesis-centered; electrochemical analysis is used to explain how Mg incorporation and ball milling change accessible conversion capacity, polarization, and relaxation.

The literature review is organized by the scientific questions that the manuscript must answer rather than by journal or characterization method.

---

## 1. Field-level background: why HEO anodes matter

### 1.1 Entropy-stabilized oxides

**Rost et al., Nature Communications 2015, 6, 8485.**  
DOI: 10.1038/ncomms9485

- Foundational entropy-stabilized oxide paper.
- Establishes configurational entropy as a route to stabilize multication single-phase oxides.
- Use in the Introduction only for the origin of the HEO concept; do not imply that every property of the present electrode is uniquely caused by configurational entropy.

### 1.2 HEOs for reversible energy storage

**Sarkar et al., Nature Communications 2018, 9, 3400.**  
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-018-05774-5

- Early demonstration of HEOs for reversible electrochemical energy storage.
- Useful for the broader battery motivation and the idea that multiple cations can combine electrochemically active and stabilizing roles.

### 1.3 Current reviews

**Zou et al., Chemical Communications 2023, 59, 13535-13550.**  
DOI: 10.1039/D3CC04225A

**Ran et al., Advanced Science 2024.**  
DOI: 10.1002/advs.202401034

**Journal of Power Sources review, 2024, 620, 235259.**  
DOI: 10.1016/j.jpowsour.2024.235259

**Journal of Energy Storage review, 2026, 146, 120192.**  
DOI: 10.1016/j.est.2025.120192

- Use these reviews to establish the current field-level consensus: HEO anodes are attractive because composition, lattice distortion, phase stability, morphology, and inactive/active cation combinations can all change conversion behavior.
- The recent reviews also emphasize that the lithium-storage mechanism remains incompletely resolved. This supports a manuscript that focuses on synthesis-dependent reaction pathways rather than a simple performance ranking.

## 2. The direct material family: spinel (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4

### 2.1 Baseline spinel electrochemistry

**Wang et al., Journal of Alloys and Compounds 2020, 844, 156158.**  
Title: *Spinel-structured high entropy oxide (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4 as anode towards superior lithium storage performance*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.jallcom.2020.156158

- Establishes (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4 as a spinel HEO anode.
- Appropriate background reference for the basic spinel structure and reversible lithium-storage behavior.

### 2.2 Same composition with HEO vs ball-milled comparator and conventional GITT

**Xiao et al., Nano Energy 2022, 95, 106962.**  
Title: *High-entropy oxides as advanced anode materials for long-life lithium-ion batteries*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.nanoen.2022.106962

Key relevance:

- Directly studies (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4 and a ball-milled comparator.
- Reports conventional GITT-derived Li-ion diffusivity and interprets HEO/BM differences largely through compact transport parameters.
- This is the closest literature benchmark to the present electrochemistry.
- The present manuscript should not position this work as "wrong". Instead, it extends the interpretation by preserving current-off polarization magnitude and relaxation time as separate observables.

Important positioning sentence for our manuscript:

> Conventional GITT diffusivity remains useful as a familiar comparative descriptor, but the present current-off analysis preserves synthesis-dependent differences in polarization amplitude and relaxation time that are compressed into a single apparent diffusivity.

## 3. Structural evolution during lithiation: the strongest basis for assigning the GITT hump

### 3.1 Atomic-scale lithiation/delithiation of spinel HEO

**Huang et al., Chemical Engineering Journal 2021, 420, 129838.**  
Title: *Atomic-scale investigation of Lithiation/Delithiation mechanism in High-entropy spinel oxide with superior electrochemical performance*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.cej.2021.129838

- Direct atomic-scale evidence that the multication spinel does not behave as a simple invariant host during lithiation.
- Useful to support discussion of redox/structural reconstruction and conversion-type storage.

### 3.2 Reversible spinel-to-rock-salt transformation in Mg-containing spinel HEO

**Zheng et al., Processes 2022, 10, 49.**  
Title: *A Spinel (FeNiCrMnMgAl)3O4 High Entropy Oxide as a Cycling Stable Anode Material for Li-Ion Batteries*  
DOI: 10.3390/pr10010049

Key points:

- In-situ XRD and ex-situ TEM reveal reversible spinel-to-rock-salt phase evolution.
- High-energy ball milling can induce a similar spinel-to-rock-salt structural change.
- Strong literature support for treating ball milling as a structural perturbation rather than only particle-size reduction.

### 3.3 Direct phase-transition pathway for the same five-cation spinel family

**Jin et al., Materials Today Chemistry 2025, article 102949.**  
Title: *Unraveling phase transition pathway of spinel (FeCoCrNiMn)3O4 high-entropy oxide anodes for long-life Li-ion batteries*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.mtchem.2025.102949

Most important result for the present manuscript:

`spinel -> mixed spinel/rock-salt -> rock-salt`

- Established by in-situ XRD and ex-situ TEM.
- This is the strongest direct literature basis for assigning the pronounced low-voltage late-stage GITT polarization feature primarily to the spinel-to-rock-salt/conversion transition.
- The present work does not independently measure the phase fraction during GITT, so wording should be assertive but bounded:

> The late-stage polarization hump is assigned primarily to the spinel-to-rock-salt/conversion phase transformation based on its characteristic voltage range and the phase-evolution pathway established for compositionally related spinel HEO anodes.

## 4. Mg: what is established and how it should be used in this paper

### 4.1 Classical spectator effect in HEO conversion anodes

**Qiu et al., Journal of Alloys and Compounds 2019, 777, 767-774.**  
Title: *A high entropy oxide (Mg0.2Co0.2Ni0.2Cu0.2Zn0.2O) with superior lithium storage performance*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.jallcom.2018.11.049

- Electrochemically inactive MgO formed during conversion is described as a spectator phase.
- MgO suppresses active nanograin agglomeration and buffers conversion-induced structural change.
- Important caveat: this is a rock-salt HEO, not the present spinel composition. Use it as mechanistic precedent, not direct proof.

### 4.2 Operando Mg-content study

**Wang et al., Materials Chemistry and Physics 2021, 274, 125105.**  
Title: *Operando synchrotron transmission X-ray microscopy study on (Mg, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn)O high-entropy oxide anodes for lithium-ion batteries*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.matchemphys.2021.125105

- Explicitly varies Mg content: Mg-containing M1/M0.8 vs Mg-free M0.
- Operando transmission X-ray microscopy shows that Mg contributes to structural retention and mitigates pulverization/crack growth.
- Mg-free material has higher specific capacity but poorer structural integrity.
- This trade-off closely matches the present observation that Mg lowers accessible capacity while stabilizing the electrochemical response.

### 4.3 Spinel studies containing Mg as a structural component

**Duan et al., Ceramics International 2021.**  
Title: *New spinel high-entropy oxides (FeCoNiCrMnXLi)3O4 (X = Cu, Mg, Zn) as the anode material for lithium-ion batteries*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.ceramint.2021.08.098 [verify final pagination/DOI metadata before bibliography export]

- Discusses inactive Mg-derived species and the spectator effect in multication spinel-type HEOs.
- Useful supporting precedent, but final bibliographic metadata should be verified before submission.

**Ceramics International 2026.**  
Title: *Spinel high-entropy oxides (FeNiCrMnZnX)3O4 (X = Al, Mg) as anode materials for high-performance lithium-ion batteries*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.ceramint.2026.07.231

- Directly compares Al- and Mg-containing Co-free spinel HEOs.
- Confirms that Mg substitution changes both capacity and kinetic response rather than acting as a neutral additive.
- New literature; useful for Discussion if final paper timing permits.

### 4.4 Preferred Mg interpretation in the present manuscript

Observed in our data:

- Mg lowers first-cycle accessible capacity.
- Mg strongly suppresses current-off polarization amplitude and the late-stage relaxation hump.
- Mg does **not** shorten t63/t90; characteristic relaxation is comparable or slower.

Therefore the preferred interpretation is not "Mg accelerates Li diffusion." The literature-supported picture is:

`Mg incorporation -> structural stabilization / spectator-like inactive component -> reduced extent of conversion-type structural reconstruction -> lower transition-associated polarization + lower accessible conversion capacity`

This mechanism should be stated first because it simultaneously explains the capacity decrease, hump suppression, and absence of faster relaxation.

Alternative interpretations and why they are less complete:

- **Faster Li diffusion:** inconsistent with the absence of shorter relaxation times.
- **Lower ohmic/electronic resistance alone:** can reduce the fast current-off jump but cannot naturally explain selective suppression of the late-stage hump and lower conversion capacity.
- **Reduced surface area/accessibility:** Mg-HEO actually has a larger BET area than HEO in the present data, so simple loss of geometric surface area is not a sufficient explanation.
- **SEI-only origin:** may affect the early first-lithiation response but does not explain the state-specific late-stage feature centered in the known conversion/phase-evolution voltage range.

## 5. Ball milling: what is established and how it should be interpreted

### 5.1 Ball milling can directly alter spinel/rock-salt balance

Zheng et al. (Processes 2022, DOI 10.3390/pr10010049) used high-energy ball milling to induce a structural change resembling the electrochemically generated spinel-to-rock-salt transformation.

Implication:

- Ball milling should not be described only as particle-size reduction.
- It can alter disorder, local strain, structural heterogeneity, and proximity to conversion-type phase evolution.

### 5.2 Particle fragmentation increases conversion reversibility but also interfacial burden

**Zhai et al., Journal of Power Sources 2024, 603, 234418.**  
Title: *Insight of the evolution of structure and energy storage mechanism of (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4 spinel high entropy oxide in life-cycle span as lithium-ion battery anode*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.jpowsour.2024.234418

- Divides long-term cycling into activation/upgradation/degradation regimes.
- Continuous particle fragmentation increases conversion reversibility and generates additional interfacial capacity.
- Excessive refinement later creates large electrolyte-derived interfacial layers and degradation.
- High-energy ball milling was used as an experimental perturbation supporting the role of fragmentation.

This paper is highly relevant to the present BM result:

- BM increases BET area from 3.94 to 18.159 m2 g-1 (~4.61x) in Mg-free HEO.
- BM increases first-cycle accessible capacity.
- The additional capacity is therefore plausibly linked to greater electrochemical utilization and conversion/interfacial participation rather than a universal decrease in kinetic resistance.

### 5.3 Present BM mechanism

Observed in our GITT analysis:

- large early first-lithiation current-off polarization after BM;
- fast polarization becomes similar to HEO at intermediate capacity;
- late-stage transition-associated hump becomes lower but broader;
- capacity-weighted excess hump area decreases somewhat rather than simply conserving a fixed peak area;
- overall relaxation becomes slower / more broadly distributed;
- low-rate accessible capacity increases.

Preferred interpretation:

`ball milling -> larger surface area + structural disorder / heterogeneous local environments -> transformation spread over a wider capacity interval -> reduced local peak transformation polarization + larger low-rate electrochemical utilization -> larger slow residual relaxation and weaker preservation of the capacity advantage as rate increases`

Do not write "ball milling improves diffusion." The present data do not support that simplification.

## 6. Why phase transformation can create additional overpotential

### 6.1 General phase-transition electrochemistry

**Meethong et al., Annual Review of Materials Research 2010, 40, 501-529.**  
DOI: 10.1146/annurev-matsci-070909-104435

- Reviews thermodynamics and kinetics of electrochemically driven phase transitions.
- Particle size, defects, doping, nucleation, phase-boundary motion, strain, and metastability can all alter the required overpotential and hysteresis.

**Kao et al., Chemistry of Materials 2010, 22, 5845-5855.**  
Title: *Overpotential-Dependent Phase Transformation Pathways in Lithium Iron Phosphate Battery Electrodes*  
DOI: 10.1021/cm101698b

- Directly shows that applied overpotential changes the phase-transition pathway.
- Useful general precedent for associating a localized voltage-polarization feature with a phase-transformation region.

**Komayko et al., Journal of Power Sources 2024, 624, 235589.**  
Title: *Unraveling the contribution of nucleation to the intercalation energy barrier for phase-transforming Li-ion battery materials*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.jpowsour.2024.235589

- Quantifies nucleation as a material-level contribution to overpotential in phase-transforming electrodes.
- Supports the statement that a phase-transition region can introduce polarization beyond simple solid-state diffusion.

### 6.2 How to use this literature without overclaiming

The manuscript should not claim that GITT uniquely measures a nucleation barrier or phase-boundary velocity. Instead:

- HEO literature establishes the relevant structural transformation.
- general battery literature establishes that nucleation/phase-boundary/strain processes contribute to overpotential.
- the present GITT data identify a state-localized excess relaxation feature in the same voltage region.

Therefore the feature can be **assigned primarily** to the phase transformation while keeping its microscopic contributions unresolved.

## 7. GITT and current-interruption literature

### 7.1 Conventional GITT limitations

**Jia et al., Journal of Power Sources 2022, 543, 231843.**  
Title: *Re-understanding the galvanostatic intermittent titration technique: Pitfalls in evaluation of diffusion coefficients and rational suggestions*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.jpowsour.2022.231843

- Summarizes assumptions behind GITT-derived diffusion coefficients: geometry, Fickian transport, invariant structure, appropriate time window, etc.
- Directly relevant because conversion/phase-evolving HEO violates the simplest invariant-host picture.

### 7.2 Early current-interruption analysis

**Chien et al., Nature Communications 2023, 14, 2289.**  
Title: *Rapid determination of solid-state diffusion coefficients in Li-based batteries via intermittent current interruption method*  
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-37989-6

- Uses the current-off intercept and early E vs sqrt(t) response.
- Supports extrapolating the first 3-30 s of the present rest transient to t -> 0 rather than calling the first sampled point a pure IR drop.
- In the present HEO manuscript, use the terms `apparent instantaneous current-off resistance/jump` and `early diffusion-like coefficient/slope`; do not assign the sqrt(t) slope uniquely to intrinsic bulk diffusion.

### 7.3 Recent model-comparison literature

**Journal of Energy Storage 2025, 132, 117628.**  
Title: *Evaluating the determination of solid-phase diffusion and reaction-rate constant for Li-ion batteries*  
DOI: 10.1016/j.est.2025.117628

- Compares analytical and physics-based extraction of Ds and k0.
- Relevant internal reference for why pulse/rest fitting can be sensitive to model structure and fitting window.
- Do not center the HEO manuscript on this modeling problem; it belongs more naturally in the separate GITT/EKF methods program.

## 8. Literature-derived manuscript differentiation

A large fraction of HEO anode papers use GITT mainly to plot an apparent diffusion coefficient and then describe larger D as faster kinetics. The present manuscript should not attack this convention. Its distinct contribution is more specific:

1. **Two independent synthesis variables are separated in a 2 x 2 matrix:** Mg-free/Mg-containing and pristine/ball-milled.
2. **Accessible capacity, polarization amplitude, and relaxation time are treated as different observables.**
3. **Mg and BM move these quantities in different directions**, which makes a single scalar diffusivity inadequate as the mechanistic explanation.
4. **The state-resolved late-stage GITT feature is connected to an independently established spinel-to-rock-salt/conversion pathway.**
5. The electrochemical analysis therefore identifies different physical roles for Mg and ball milling rather than simply ranking samples by rate performance.

Recommended high-level manuscript message:

> Mg incorporation and ball milling regulate the same conversion-type HEO through different physical routes: Mg suppresses the extent of late-stage phase transformation, whereas ball milling broadens the transformation over a wider state interval while increasing electrochemical utilization and slow residual relaxation.
