## What has actually been established
- Four real GITT spreadsheets, each with C1–C3 full lithiation/delithiation, gave ~1,020 completely rested pulses across 24 halfcycles. Each condition one cell; later cycles repeat history, not cell replicates. Protocol nominal current 100 mA/g, 600 s pulse/3600 s rest, 16.67 mAh/g per full pulse. Whole-halfcycle Q includes terminal partial pulse; first-region Q does not.
- C1 lithiation R1–R4 t63 boundaries fixed: HEO 6/25/55; BM 9/38/60; Mg 7/24/42; BM-Mg 9/35/46. BM R2 gain (+166.7/+150) and R3 loss (−133.3/−116.7); Mg R3 loss (−200/−183.3), all nominal mAh/g. Both shrink R3 but BM mainly shifts its **start later** and Mg mainly shifts its **end earlier**; boundaries do not show abrupt full-rest shape discontinuities.
- C1 lithiation uniquely complex and has +~342–425 mAh/g vs mean C2/C3; first delithiation charge/voltage resembles later delithiation more than first lithiation; later C2/C3 normalized rest curves largely recur, including `|ΔErest|` hump. Structural reconstruction is a working interpretation, not phase identification.
- BM and Mg total C2/C3 lithiation and delithiation effects retain opposite signs. C2/C3 total charge is near multiplicatively factorizable: `Q_BM-Mg≈Q_BM Q_Mg/Q_HEO`; C3 lith 550.0 predicted vs 549.6 observed, delith 533.0 vs 543.5 mAh/g (three contemporaneous reference measurements, not an independent future forecast).
- **Key novelty candidate:** BM increases total reversible charge but also changes C2/C3 **delithiation** normalized potential-charge distribution and increases slow effective voltage relaxation; Mg's reversible potential/charge change is more parsimoniously explained by capacity factor+potential shift. C2-calibrated 2-potential population model predicts held-out C3 delithiation better than shift-only for BM: RMSE BM/noMg 0.0144→0.0087; BM/withMg 0.0097→0.0042 (fraction of charge). Positive reweighting (~β=0.051/0.044) robust in 12/12 chosen window settings; this is a **model-equivalent weight, NOT a phase fraction**. Matched 2-param affine potential warp does not similarly improve these BM delithiation tests. **Lithiation model preference differs**; a universal 2-population picture across both current directions is not supported.
- C2-derived kinetic offset improves C3 delithiation effective t63 prediction BM/noMg 84.5→23.9s RMSE, BM/withMg 52.0→13.4s; normalized first-300s recovery decreases (roughly 3.5/2.4 percentage points). BM has more accessible charge AND longer effective rest relaxation, which is NOT proof of slower elementary charge transfer.
- Mg specific 0.9V amplitude residual ~−12–14mV after global amplitude-gain only is largely absorbed by potential-shift allowance (~1–3mV left); do NOT assert selective Mg blocked-redox phase. Hump-based later BM “~70% after hump” is boundary-sensitive, not a mechanistic quantitative assignment.
- Near-multiplicative global capacity does NOT imply independent dynamic response: BM effective delith t63 gain ~+81s without Mg vs +50s with Mg. Mg stoichiometry/site and loading need source verification.

## Current scientific thesis to test
**The electrochemical effects of BM and Mg cannot be understood from whole first-lithiation charge or a single t63 region alone.** BM appears to broaden/reweight accessible reversible conversion-related response populations, including longer-rest-time contributions on subsequent delithiation; Mg changes total accessed reversible inventory and voltage landscape; their kinetic interaction persists despite almost independent total Q. This is a *falsifiable hypothesis*, distinct from the directly observed GITT and restricted-family model facts. No unique phase or chemical mechanism established. A GITT-to-SOC phase assignment needs state-specific spectroscopy/diffraction and realistic cell/stoichiometry calibration.
