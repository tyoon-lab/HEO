# HEO Figure 6 experimental-history lock — 2026-10-03

## Decision

The preferred Main Figure 6 is now a **fully experimental cycle-history robustness test** of the polarization–timescale distinction.

The former Q-based four-step microkinetic model is no longer the preferred Main Figure 6.

---

## Why

The first-cycle material comparison already shows that conversion-region recoverable polarization and (t_{63}) do not move along one universal fast–slow coordinate.

Cycle-resolved raw GITT provides a stronger test because it asks whether the same non-equivalence appears when **the same material evolves with electrochemical history**.

It does.

Most clearly, Mg-HEO from cycle 1 to cycle 3 shows:

- (Delta E_{mathrm{pol,60}}): 126.4 -> 153.7 mV (+21.6%)
- (t_{63}): 10.53 -> 9.53 min (-9.5%)

Thus polarization becomes larger while relaxation becomes faster.

BM-Mg-HEO shows:

- polarization essentially unchanged (+1.9%);
- (t_{63}) shorter by 15.6%.

These observations cannot be represented by a single scalar quantity in which “larger polarization = slower relaxation”.

## Main claim supported by Figure 6

> **The non-equivalence of polarization magnitude and relaxation timescale persists with electrochemical history and can even appear as opposite-direction changes within the same material.**

This is stronger and cleaner than using a model to prove mathematical possibility.

## Model status

The former R2/R3 capacity-based model:
- remains an audit trail / optional SI calculation;
- is not required for the central claim;
- should not be used to assign ball milling or Mg effects microscopically.

A new model should only be introduced later if it adds explanatory value beyond the experimental result.
