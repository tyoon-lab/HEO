## Issues to review

1. Ball milling and Mg incorporation are separate processing and composition variables; prior literature documents effects but does not establish universal opposing first-lithiation capacity signs.
2. Four samples make **two matched comparisons for each modification**: pristine/BM and Mg/BM-Mg for milling; pristine/Mg and BM/BM-Mg for Mg.
3. Avoid repeating P3 method justification in P4. Briefly state experimental delineation of four regions, then emphasize region-specific charge results.
4. 'Conversion onset/progression' (R2) and 'later conversion' (R3) are literature-informed interpretations, not direct phase determinations.
5. First-lithiation GITT charge and continuous GCD capacity are distinct quantities; directional trends agree in current data.
6. The original 'targeting specific conversion processes' should not imply each region is a pure chemical reaction.
7. References [1] Xiao Nano Energy 2022 and [27] Minouei JPS 2022 refer to other controlled routes/substitutions; they motivate study design but are not the PI's exact matched experiment.

## Suggested new P4

Ball milling and Mg incorporation were selected to examine processing and compositional modifications of spinel HEOs, respectively. Previous studies have shown that milling-based processing and Mg substitution can affect their electrochemical behavior [1,27]. Ball milling can alter particle dimensions, local disorder, and interfacial accessibility, whereas Mg incorporation changes the cation composition and local chemical environment [4,17–20]. First-lithiation GITT was applied to pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO), allowing two matched comparisons for each modification. Ball milling increases total first-lithiation charge in both comparisons, whereas Mg incorporation decreases it. The GITT response identifies four electrochemical regions, with the largest positive charge increment from ball milling in the region associated with conversion onset and progression and the largest negative increment from Mg incorporation in the region associated with later conversion. These differences locate the major capacity changes along lithiation and clarify how processing and composition influence the conversion response.
