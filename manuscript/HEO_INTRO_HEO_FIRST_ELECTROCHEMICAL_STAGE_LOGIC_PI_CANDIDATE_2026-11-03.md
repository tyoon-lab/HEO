## Full English prose

# 1. Introduction

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) combine multiple transition-metal cations within a common oxide lattice and have attracted interest as high-capacity conversion-type anodes. Although their lithium storage can be summarized by an overall oxide-to-metal/Li₂O conversion reaction, state-resolved studies reveal a more intricate evolution during lithiation. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy has shown that the constituent metal cations in a spinel HEO are reduced over different, partly overlapping ranges of lithiation capacity [7]. Ex situ X-ray absorption and diffraction studies have further identified Li uptake, cation migration, and intermediate oxide reconstruction before and during metal-forming conversion [23]. Stepwise reduction via intermediate oxides has also been observed in conventional spinel oxides, including Co₃O₄ [27]. These findings show that the processes contributing to lithium storage change as lithiation proceeds, even though several reactions may occur simultaneously.

Understanding this progression is important for controlling lithium storage by modifying HEO materials. Previous studies have shown that particle size, constituent-cation substitution, and Li₂O incorporation can change the capacity and conversion behavior of spinel HEOs [9,20,24]. However, a change in total capacity does not show which part of the lithiation reaction is affected. A modification may influence charge storage over a wide range of lithiation or act predominantly within a particular region. Distinguishing these possibilities would clarify which reactions are promoted or restricted by processing and composition, and would provide a more systematic basis for materials design.

The next challenge is to identify such differences in electrochemical measurements. Different reduction and structural transformation processes can affect both the voltage response and its relaxation after current interruption. However, substantial lithiation charge can be accommodated within a broad conversion plateau with little change in voltage, while overlapping reactions may produce poorly separated differential-capacity features. Electrochemical studies of Co₃O₄ have assigned distinct cyclic-voltammetry peaks to successive reduction steps, although those peaks can merge depending on the material [28]. For NiO, changes in GITT-derived apparent transport and exchange-current parameters, combined with voltammetric analysis, have been used to distinguish electrochemical regions with different dominant reactions [26]. Compared with state-resolved structural studies, the use of electrochemical signatures to define comparable conversion regions remains limited, particularly when assessing how materials modifications redistribute lithiation charge. The post-pulse voltage relaxation measured by GITT provides additional information beyond the voltage profile. Rather than extracting an apparent diffusion coefficient from a response that also includes interfacial and structural changes [11–13], the measured relaxation amplitude and characteristic recovery time can be examined alongside the rest-end voltage to identify changes in the electrochemical response as lithiation proceeds.

Here, pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO) are compared using first-lithiation GITT. Changes in the rest-end voltage, relaxation amplitude, and characteristic recovery time distinguish four electrochemical regimes, which are interpreted using the conversion processes established in prior structural and spectroscopic studies. The characteristic sequence remains recognizable after material modification, but the charge contributions of the individual regions change substantially. Ball milling produces its largest positive regional GITT-charge increment during conversion onset and progression, whereas Mg incorporation causes its largest negative increment during later conversion. These distinct responses identify where processing and composition affect lithium storage, establishing a direct electrochemical basis for targeting specific parts of the conversion reaction in HEO anodes.

### Physics brief
Potential E_eq = −(1/F)(∂G/∂n_Li) approximately, using appropriate sign/reference convention; in a two-phase coexistence interval the chemical potential can change little and E plateau; a broad plateau can also reflect multiple overlapping conversion reactions, heterogeneity and finite-rate polarization. The finite-window recovery ΔErest = |E(3600s) − E(3s)| and t63 carry kinetic + thermodynamic weighting from E(x), spatial redistribution, SEI, changing reaction interfaces and structural dynamics. Their diagnostic role is *trend/landmark*, not an elementary mechanism rate or a unique phase identity.
