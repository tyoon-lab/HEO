## Approved abstract (verbatim)

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) have attracted considerable interest as high-capacity anodes for lithium-ion batteries. Improving their electrochemical performance has motivated studies of particle refinement and cation substitution, which modify oxide morphology and cation chemistry, respectively. Understanding the effects of these modifications requires examining the lithiation response, where transition-metal reduction and structural reorganization overlap. Here, galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) is used to compare the effects of ball milling and Mg incorporation on the conversion response of spinel HEOs. The evolution of voltage-relaxation descriptors, including recovery amplitude and characteristic relaxation time, together with quasi-open-circuit voltage (QOCV), reveals four electrochemical regions (R1–R4) across lithiation. Ball milling increases initial lithiation capacity in both Mg-free and Mg-containing electrodes, with its largest positive change in nominal regional GITT charge occurring in R2 (conversion onset and progression, +133–167 mAh g⁻¹), accompanied by a decrease in R3 (later conversion, −150 to −167 mAh g⁻¹). Mg incorporation decreases capacity in both unmilled and milled electrodes, with its largest regional charge loss in R3 (−267 to −283 mAh g⁻¹). The greater BET surface area after milling is consistent with increased accessibility of oxide domains, whereas the Mg-associated R3 loss may reflect fewer reducible transition-metal centers or more persistent Mg-containing oxide environments. These findings show that processing and composition produce distinct, partially compensating charge changes across lithiation. Such region-resolved analysis helps identify how material modifications affect the evolving electrochemical response and provides guidance for the design of conversion-type electrodes.
