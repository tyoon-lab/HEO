## PI-accepted Abstract (exact current wording)

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) have attracted considerable interest as high-capacity anodes for lithium-ion batteries. Various strategies have been explored to improve their electrochemical performance by tailoring composition, structure, and morphology. Previous studies have evaluated modification-induced changes in overall capacity and investigated lithiation pathways using structural and spectroscopic techniques. However, transition-metal reduction, structural transformation, metal/Li₂O nanocomposite formation, and possible interfacial storage overlap during lithiation, obscuring which portions of the reaction account for the observed capacity changes. To address this question, this study uses galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) to divide lithiation into four electrochemical regions (R1–R4) based on changes in the characteristic voltage-relaxation time and quantify the charge changes in each region. Ball milling and Mg incorporation are examined as morphological and compositional modifications, respectively. Ball milling produces its largest charge gain in R2, associated with oxide reconstruction and early transition-metal reduction, whereas Mg incorporation produces its largest charge loss in R3, associated with further reduction of intermediate oxides and metal/Li₂O product formation. These results identify the electrochemical regions responsible for modification-induced capacity gains and losses, providing a basis for selecting material modifications in the development of conversion-type anodes.

**Abstract-specific decisions:** Broaden opening beyond only ball milling/Mg; remove repeated motivation and long "while" juxtaposition; affirm useful spectroscopic work rather than describing it as only qualitative; distinguish regional GITT charge from uniquely quantified chemical species; embed physical R2/R3 meanings in the measured result sentence; no numerical charge ranges in Abstract. PI explicitly **kept "responsible for"** to mean the major contributing *electrochemical regions* of net capacity changes (R2 positive BM, R3 negative Mg), **not** proof those bins are underlying elementary kinetic causes. The final materials-development implication is approved. Do not replace it with generic "reveals how" text or "conceal opposing gains" conclusion unless PI reopens editing.

## PI-accepted Introduction — entire four paragraphs (exact current wording)

### Paragraph 1 — Literature evidence → overlapping processes → electrochemical hypothesis

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) have attracted considerable interest as high-capacity conversion anodes for lithium-ion batteries. Although their overall lithium storage is commonly described as the conversion of metal oxides into metal/Li₂O nanocomposites, the presence of multiple transition-metal cations introduces distinct and potentially overlapping reduction pathways. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy of a Co-free spinel HEO has shown that Fe and Mn pass through intermediate oxide states before reaching metallic form, whereas Ni and Cu follow different, more direct reduction pathways [4]. Ex situ structural and spectroscopic studies of another spinel HEO identified initial Li⁺ insertion, cation migration, and spinel-to-rock-salt structural transformations during lithiation [5]. In situ TEM observations of Fe₃O₄ further showed that insertion and conversion can proceed at different rates and overlap, producing coexisting intermediate oxide and metal/Li₂O domains within individual particles [6]. These findings indicate that multiple reduction and structural-transformation processes contribute to lithiation, often over overlapping intervals. Their relative contributions may change as lithiation progresses, allowing particular processes to become more prominent at different stages and potentially produce distinguishable electrochemical responses.

### Paragraph 2 — Material modifications, evaluation limits, narrow research gap

Distinguishing electrochemical responses across lithiation may help determine how material modifications affect different stages of the conversion process. Particle refinement, changes in cation composition, and Li₂O doping have been explored to improve the electrochemical performance of HEO anodes [7–9]. Previous studies have assessed the resulting changes in overall capacity and examined oxidation-state and phase evolution using structural and spectroscopic techniques. However, changes in overall capacity can conceal competing charge gains and losses across different stages of lithiation. Despite advances in understanding conversion reaction pathways, few studies have directly quantified modification-induced charge changes across electrochemically distinguishable lithiation regions.

### Paragraph 3 — Thermodynamics and kinetics → GITT observable roles

Changes in the relative contributions of these processes can influence both the thermodynamic and kinetic behavior of the electrode during lithiation. The thermodynamic characteristics of lithiation influence the potential-dependent features observed in galvanostatic voltage curves and differential-capacity profiles, but overlapping reactions at similar potentials can obscure their individual contributions. Differences in the kinetics of the dominant processes may provide an additional means of distinguishing reaction regions from their voltage-relaxation responses. Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) records these relaxation responses at successive states of lithiation. The characteristic relaxation time (t₆₃) and voltage-recovery amplitude (ΔErest) describe the timescale and magnitude of the response, respectively, while the end-of-rest quasi-open-circuit voltage (QOCV) records the potential at the end of each rest period. Changes in t₆₃ can therefore be used to define electrochemical regions, with ΔErest and QOCV further characterizing the voltage response within each region.

### Paragraph 4 — Four materials, experimental regions, literature inference, key result

Ball milling and Mg incorporation were selected to examine how changes in particle morphology and cation composition affect the lithiation behavior of spinel HEOs. GITT measurements were performed on pristine HEO (HEO), ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-incorporated HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-incorporated HEO (BM-Mg-HEO), allowing matched comparisons of both modifications. Based on changes in the characteristic voltage-relaxation time, lithiation was divided into four electrochemical regions (R1–R4). The dominant reaction process in each region was then inferred from previous mechanistic studies of spinel conversion oxides. Comparison of the regional charge revealed that ball milling produced its largest increase in R2, whereas Mg incorporation caused its largest decrease in R3.

## Decision trajectory and scientific meaning — keep for future editing

1. **P1 introductory definition:** Removed the opening "multiple cations within a single-phase lattice and have attracted interest" coupling: multication structure does not itself establish the cause of high capacity. Instead introduce interest first, then describe conversion complexity. Current single-phase claims about the *specific four powders* still require actual diffraction.
2. **P1 literature:** Cite the Co-free HEO XAS as composition-specific evidence ([4], includes Cu); separate other HEO Li uptake/spinel–rocksalt evolution ([5]) from Fe₃O₄ in-situ TEM phase coexistence ([6]). Do not use a vague "alongside" sentence that blurs these experiments. Direct literature observations are not direct demonstration of current samples' R1–R4 chemistry.
3. **P1 hypothesis:** Describe overlapping processes whose contributions may evolve. Rejected a transition that singles out "element-dependent reduction pathways and different insertion/conversion kinetics" as though these alone are what the present t₆₃ segmentation resolves. The hypothesis is *relative prominence of conversion processes* → distinguishable electrochemical responses. "Stages of lithiation" is appropriate as a conceptual progression; experimentally operational bins are called "electrochemical regions."
4. **P2 opening:** Rejected "This possibility raises a central question" followed by examples with no stated question. Accepted the direct bridge "Distinguishing electrochemical responses..." and **kept** the original second sentence "Particle refinement, changes in cation composition, and Li₂O doping ...", without "For example" or other cosmetic connectors.
5. **P2 gap:** Net capacity can conceal opposite signed regional changes. Spectroscopy/structural characterization provides valuable chemical-state information, sometimes quantitative; the unaddressed specific link is *modification-induced charge gains and losses among electrochemically identifiable regions*. Removed redundant "obscuring which stages" tail. Rejected "redistribute charge" language if it implies transfer from one chemical reaction to another. "Few studies..." is specifically scoped to this comparison, **not a claim that no GITT staging or operando spectroscopy precedent exists**; bibliography check before submission.
6. **P3 physics:** The PI explicitly restored **thermodynamic and kinetic behavior**, instead of equating equilibrium with a galvanostatic voltage plot or kinetics solely with current-interruption response. The potential-dependent features of voltage/dQdV have thermodynamic contributions but also polarization; the GITT relaxation has multiple coupled physical contributions. The PI rejected an expanded charge-transfer/ion-transport/phase-boundary checklist as overly explanatory for Introduction.
7. **P3 concise bridge:** Approved the sentence "Differences in the kinetics of the dominant processes may provide an additional means of distinguishing reaction regions from their voltage-relaxation responses." This conveys kinetics associated with relatively dominant reaction processes without claiming transport kinetics alone. In P3 t₆₃ controls *region definitions*; ΔErest/QOCV supplement interpretation, not boundary placement.
8. **P4 naming:** Define HEO, BM-HEO, Mg-HEO, BM-Mg-HEO when first introduced; use **Mg-incorporated** because cation site and specific substitution are not independently established.
9. **P4 mechanism claim:** The PI rejected "Each region was assigned as a dominant reaction process" (incorrect meaning: region itself equals process). "Each region was assigned a dominant reaction process" is grammatical, but the PI approved the clearer **"The dominant reaction process in each region was then inferred from previous mechanistic studies of spinel conversion oxides."** The process is an **inference**, not a proven chemically exclusive assignment.
10. **P4 ordering:** Materials → GITT → four t₆₃ regions → literature-inferred dominant process in each → BM largest R2 increase and Mg largest R3 decrease. Removed repetitive closing claims about the benefit of regional analysis.
