## Scientific mechanism correction

1. The **net oxide + Li reduction -> metal/Li2O reaction** is not one instantaneous homogeneous event; metal/Li2O product formation can **begin during R2** as oxide destabilization/reorganization proceeds, then **continue through R3**. R2/R3 identify comparatively prominent processes/charge-accommodation signatures, not mutually exclusive phases, nonoverlapping elementary conversion steps or a first-appearance/completion timestamp for metal/Li2O.
2. **Why the long charge interval?** (a) different transition-metal cations have differing, possibly overlapping, reduction domains; (b) spinel-to-intermediate-oxide reconstruction and growth of converted metallic/Li2O-derived regions can occur in spatially heterogeneous domains and reaction fronts. These processes need not track one boundary or one reduction potential. A **broad charge/capacity interval** can appear as a relatively flat voltage plateau; do not incorrectly claim broad *voltage* interval.
3. This interpretation is **literature guided**, rather than direct phase-resolution by GITT; the GITT primary descriptors are finite-window voltage recovery amplitude `ΔErest`, operational `t63` and a 60-min rest-end voltage; neither `t63` nor `ΔErest` determines metal-specific reduction, oxide phase fraction, and yield of Li2O.
4. Existing stage-specific response mechanism remains unchanged: BM largest positive signed nominal GITT region capacity in **R2**, potentially facilitated by gas-accessible surface-area increase; Mg largest negative in **R3**, leading hypothesis persistent Mg-containing oxide environment limiting later accessible conversion under cutoff, potentially accompanied by reducible-cation depletion or shifted reaction energetics. Original first-lithiation GITT values and warning against comparing with GCD retained.
5. Updated the Intro first-paragraph bridge, §2.3 mechanistic paragraph (most substantive), §2.4/§2.5 nuance, §2.6 synthesis, Figure6 caption and Conclusion. Figure4 caption already states “plausible dominant processes” and remains compatible.
