## 1. PI corrections / controlling principles

1. **GITT four-regime identification is mechanistically meaningful.** The conceptual objective is to associate coupled changes in pulse/rest potential (and polarization/relaxation), rest-end voltage, relaxation magnitude `ΔE_rest`, and characteristic `t63` with changes in the **dominant electrochemical process** as conversion advances. Replacing this with a purely phenomenological/four-arbitrary-windows story weakens the paper. Each regime may involve overlapping processes; the data plus prior conversion literature warrant a **dominant-process interpretation**, although the GITT observables alone do not constitute direct element-specific spectroscopy or a one-to-one assignment of four discrete crystalline phases.
2. Distinguish **measured relaxation magnitude** from thermodynamic overpotential. `ΔE_rest = |E(3600 s after interruption) − E(3 s after interruption)|` is a finite-window relaxation measure; `t63` is the time to 63.2% of that same measured recovery; 60-min voltage is a standardized rest-end coordinate rather than a demonstrated equilibrium potential. Any future `η` or `overpotential` analysis needs an explicit definition/reference, matched current and electrode states.
3. **L1 / L3 / L4 electrochemical landmarks, physical interpretation:** R1 early Li uptake; R2 onset/progression of transition-metal reduction and parent-oxide destabilization/reconstruction; R3 further reduction and evolution toward metal/Li2O-derived products; R4 post-plateau deep lithiation. The L3 minimum in `t63` and `ΔE_rest` near a differential-capacity feature is a physically informative change of relaxation/polarization balance, not a direct spectral identification of one product. Alternative or simultaneous pathways remain possible.
4. **Conversion does not occur exclusively during lithiation.** During lithiation (the forward conversion/reduction direction), the oxide can form reduced species and metal/Li2O-derived states. During delithiation, reoxidation/**reconversion** can take place, often with hysteresis and incomplete restoration of the starting oxide; the reverse need not retrace the forward sequence. A safer introductory phrase is **“Spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes exhibit complex conversion–reconversion reactions during electrochemical cycling.”** This is a *working opening*, not a PI-approved Abstract replacement. The current segmented GITT analysis has its strongest numerical support from first-lithiation pulses. Raw traces reportedly cover three cycles, but additional-cycle stage conclusions remain pending inspection; omit “during the first lithiation” from the evolving Abstract if the final scope is still open, while preserving precise first-lithiation descriptions in Methods and Results.
5. **Ball milling / Mg comparison is a research question before it is a result.** Present the familiar opposing overall lithiation-capacity changes, ask which stages are modified, then give the region-resolved answer. Prefer “Ball milling and Mg incorporation produce opposing changes in lithiation capacity, motivating examination of the reaction stages affected by each modification.” The formulation “are known to” additionally implies a literature-proven generality and should be used only with specific supporting references. Grammatical subject is plural: “are known,” not “is known.”
6. Use **“associated with”** for physical interpretation; repeated “broadly associated with” is unnecessary. In this paper, “associated with” represents evidence-linked mechanistic interpretation, not a guarantee of direct phase identification.
7. Avoid superfluous “within the same multistep conversion sequence”; primary novelty is the **location of the capacity gain/loss and a physically plausible reason for that location**. Intro/Abstract changes beyond the provisional opening remain subject to further PI approval.

### Section 2.3 — insert after definitions of L1, L3, L4 and before physical region assignments

The joint evolution of the rest-end voltage, relaxation magnitude, and characteristic relaxation time provides a basis for associating the four regions with changes in the dominant electrochemical processes during conversion. L1 separates the early lithiation response from the subsequent conversion-associated behavior. The minima in ΔErest and t63 near L3, together with the nearby differential-capacity feature, indicate a substantial change in the balance of polarization, relaxation, and charge accommodation within the conversion pathway. L4 marks the end of the extended rest-end voltage plateau, indicating a transition to the post-plateau lithiation response. In this context, R2 is associated with the onset and progression of transition-metal reduction, oxide destabilization, and structural reconstruction, whereas R3 is associated with further reduction and evolution toward metal/Li₂O-derived products. These assignments describe the processes expected to become most prominent in each electrochemically defined region; overlapping reactions and heterogeneous intermediate states may contribute across adjacent regions. The electrical signatures support stage-dominant mechanistic interpretation, while identifying an individual intermediate, metal species, or Li₂O yield requires complementary chemical/structural evidence.

### Section 2.4 — replace the final mechanistic-interpretation paragraph after presenting the BM regional-capacity and t63 results

The preferential expansion of R2 following ball milling is consistent with greater accessibility of oxide regions participating in the onset and progression of conversion. Particle refinement, structural disorder, and increased interfacial access are plausible milling-induced changes that may promote early reduction and reorganization of the parent oxide, increasing the amount of charge accommodated before the later conversion-dominated region. The accompanying lengthening of t63 over R2–R4 indicates that this greater accessible capacity coexists with more persistent post-interruption voltage relaxation. The combined response is consistent with continued equilibration of heterogeneous reconstructed environments and Li/charge redistribution after the applied pulse. Thus, the extent of conversion accessed during the pulse and the timescale of subsequent voltage equilibration are distinct properties. The substantial decrease in R3 and increase in R4 further show that milling redistributes charge across the conversion sequence rather than affecting R2 in isolation. Confirmation of particle refinement, defect/disorder changes, and accessible surface area requires the corresponding measured structural and morphological evidence.

### Section 2.5 — replace the final mechanistic-interpretation paragraph after presenting the Mg regional-capacity and t63 results

The pronounced reduction of R3 following Mg incorporation points to a preferential change in the accessible extent or energetics of later conversion. A lower inventory of reducible transition-metal cations could contribute to the capacity reduction if Mg substitutes for an electroactive species. Changes in Mg-dependent local coordination and oxide stability may additionally affect the progression from partially reconstructed oxide states toward more extensively reduced metal/Li₂O-derived products. The concentration of the capacity decrease in R3, compared with the substantially smaller change in R2, suggests a stage-dependent effect beyond approximately uniform capacity dilution. The relatively modest change in the median t63 of the remaining R3 response further separates the loss of accessible late-conversion capacity from the observed relaxation timescale. These mechanisms remain alternative explanations pending independent confirmation of Mg content, coordination/site occupancy, oxide structural response, and chemical conversion products; GITT alone establishes the location and relaxation characteristics of the dominant change.

### Section 2.6 — insert before its concluding paragraph

A physically coherent distinction emerges between the two modifications. Milling may preferentially expand conversion participation during the oxide-destabilization and reconstruction regime by changing the accessibility and heterogeneity of reactive oxide domains. Mg incorporation may instead limit or redistribute the further conversion of reconstructed oxide-derived states through changes in redox-active cation inventory or the energetics of subsequent reduction. The pronounced R2 gain after milling and R3 loss after Mg incorporation are directly supported by the regional GITT charge comparison, whereas the proposed microscopic origins remain testable explanations. Changes in relaxation magnitude and t63 complement the charge partition by showing that accessibility, polarization, and post-pulse equilibration may evolve differently. This link from a measured stage-selective capacity effect to a constrained physical explanation is the appropriate basis for the manuscript's closing conclusion.
