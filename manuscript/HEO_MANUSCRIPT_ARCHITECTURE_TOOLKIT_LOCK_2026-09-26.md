# HEO Manuscript Architecture — Toolkit-Aligned Story Lock

**Date:** 2026-09-26  
**Toolkit basis:** Observable-contradiction driven paper origin; observation → quantification → independent constraint → robustness/history → mechanistic-consistency existence proof.

## Scientific event that creates the paper

Ball milling increases accessible conversion capacity but slows the conversion-associated effective current-off kinetics.

This is the primary contradiction. Mg incorporation is not a coequal contradiction; it is a complementary perturbation showing that accessible conversion, relaxation amplitude, and relaxation timescale do not move as one scalar kinetic coordinate.

## Locked central statements

**Experimental statement**

> Ball milling reveals that higher accessible capacity can coexist with slower conversion-associated kinetics, while conventional GITT analysis gives the opposite kinetic ranking.

**Mechanistic statement**

> Microkinetic analysis shows that this behavior can arise naturally from the multistep character of conversion.

## Evidence hierarchy

### Figures 1–2 — Define perturbations
Question: Are HEO, BM-HEO, Mg-HEO, and BM-Mg-HEO materially distinct before electrochemical interpretation?

Role: structural/compositional/morphological context only.

### Figure 3 — Establish accessible reaction extent
Question: How do BM and Mg change the amount of conversion accessed?

Answer:
- BM increases accessible capacity.
- Mg suppresses accessible capacity.

No kinetic rate is inferred from capacity.

### Figure 4 — Establish the contradiction and the conventional-GITT failure
Question 1: Does higher BM capacity correspond to faster relaxation?

Answer: No. BM increases capacity while increasing $t_{63}$.

Question 2: Does conventional $D_{\mathrm{app}}$ preserve the same kinetic ordering?

Answer: No. $D_{\mathrm{app,BM}}/D_{\mathrm{app,HEO}}>1$ at 37/37 matched points, whereas the direct relaxation-rate ratio is <1 at 35/37 points.

This is the paper's decisive Figure.

### Figure 5 — Localize the phenomenon
Question: Is the anomalous relaxation actually associated with conversion?

Answer: The GITT excess and cathodic $dQ/dV$ peaks coincide within 32 mV for all four materials.

Claim boundary: conversion-associated, not uniquely assigned to nucleation, phase-boundary motion, oxygen migration, or one product-forming step.

### Figure 6 — Test history dependence
Question: Is the mismatch merely a first-cycle artifact?

Answer: No. The response evolves with cycle history, while BM retains higher accessible reaction extent together with longer $t_{63}$.

Role: robustness/history constraint, not a new headline.

### Figure 7 — Show physical possibility
Question: Can higher accessible capacity and slower relaxation arise within a physically coupled conversion network?

Answer: Yes. The homogeneous control recovers faster → capacity up + relaxation faster; an illustrative multistep heterogeneous case gives capacity up + relaxation slower.

Role: existence proof / mechanistic consistency only.

## General implication

Supported:
> Conversion kinetics cannot, in general, be ranked by apparent GITT diffusivity alone.

Not supported:
- GITT is invalid for conversion electrodes.
- Li diffusion is absent.
- $t_{63}$ is the forward conversion rate.
- ball milling uniquely slows a specific microscopic reconstruction step.
- the illustrative microkinetic population is a measured phase fraction.

## Interpretation boundary frozen after EIS review

The manuscript-level phrase **conversion-associated kinetics** is supported because the excess GITT current-off response is localized independently to the conversion region by cathodic (dQ/dV).

Operationally, however, the measured descriptor is (t_{63}), an effective current-off relaxation timescale. The manuscript must not equate (t_{63}) with one microscopic forward conversion rate constant, one diffusion time, or one unique bulk phase-boundary velocity.

Cycling EIS is excluded from the evidence hierarchy. The available spectra have state-matching/outlier and process-overlap limitations and are not required to establish the central claim.
