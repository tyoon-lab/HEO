## Current manuscript status

The electrochemical and mechanistic story from Figures 3–6 has been organized around the following sequence:

- Figure 3: conventional electrochemistry — accessible capacity, cycling/rate response, and first-cycle conversion evolution;
- Figure 4: GITT — relaxation magnitude and relaxation time are separated;
- Figure 5: the GITT excess response is localized to the first-cycle conversion region; ball milling redistributes accessible conversion whereas Mg incorporation suppresses/delays it;
- Figure 6: reduced spatial modeling is used only as a qualitative consistency test for the experimentally inferred ball-milling and Mg roles.

Figures 1–2 and the corresponding characterization text remain provisional pending the final Yoo-group dataset.

## Interpretation boundaries already adopted

- Ball milling is described primarily as increasing accessibility and redistributing conversion over a broader range of local reaction environments.
- Mg incorporation is described as reducing/delaying accessible conversion, consistent with stabilization of oxide-derived states.
- GITT relaxation is not assigned to one unique microscopic conversion step.
- The reduced model is not used to extract unique material constants or experimental phase fractions.
