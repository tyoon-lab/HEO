## Title

**GITT Relaxation Fingerprints Resolve Material-Specific Perturbations of Conversion Lithiation in Spinel High-Entropy Oxide Anodes**

## Abstract

Spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes undergo multistep conversion during lithiation, involving progressive cation reduction, oxide destabilization and reconstruction, and the formation of reduced metal/Li2O-derived states. The GITT relaxation response during first lithiation resolves this process into four electrochemically distinct regimes: early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation. This stage-resolved response provides a basis for determining how material modifications alter the conversion sequence underlying the overall capacity. Ball milling and Mg incorporation produce opposite capacity changes, but the GITT analysis shows that they act on different reaction stages: the capacity increase induced by ball milling arises predominantly from expansion of the conversion-onset/progression regime associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction, whereas the capacity loss caused by Mg incorporation is concentrated in the subsequent late-conversion regime associated with evolution toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived states. These results show that macroscopic capacity changes can originate from selective expansion or suppression of specific parts of a multistep conversion sequence. By locating the reaction stage responsible for a performance change, GITT relaxation provides an electrochemical basis for connecting materials processing and composition directly to the conversion processes that should be promoted or suppressed.

## Keywords

high-entropy oxide; conversion anode; GITT; voltage relaxation; reaction fingerprint; ball milling; Mg incorporation

# 1. Introduction

Spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes undergo multistep conversion during lithiation. Operando and ex situ studies have established a progression involving cation reduction, destabilization and reconstruction of the parent oxide, and formation of reduced or metallic species together with Li2O. [3,6–10] Because these processes overlap over a broad voltage range, structural and chemical characterization has been central to identifying how the conversion proceeds at the microscopic level.

For materials development, however, a different question is equally important: which part of this multistep sequence is responsible for a measured change in electrochemical performance? Ball milling commonly increases the accessible lithiation capacity of spinel HEO anodes, whereas Mg incorporation can decrease conversion accessibility and capacity. A capacity increase or loss alone does not show whether the modification changes early lithiation, promotes the onset and progression of oxide reconstruction, or limits the subsequent late-conversion response. Resolving the stage origin of these macroscopic changes would connect familiar materials effects directly to the part of the conversion chemistry that is altered.

GITT offers a state-resolved electrochemical response throughout lithiation. Conventional analysis often compresses each transient into an apparent diffusion coefficient, although such a quantity becomes difficult to interpret when transport is coupled to phase transformation, reaction kinetics, finite-size effects, and state-dependent thermodynamics. [11–15] The relaxation response itself contains complementary information: its magnitude and characteristic time evolve with lithiation state and can therefore distinguish electrochemically different regions of a reconstructive reaction without assigning each transient to a single microscopic rate process.

Ball milling and Mg incorporation provide a useful pair of material perturbations because their macroscopic capacity effects are opposite while their structural origins are different. Ball milling changes particle size, disorder, surface area, and interfacial structure and is known to modify electrochemical utilization in conversion-type HEO electrodes. [1,4,17] Mg incorporation has been associated in related systems with lower conversion accessibility and greater persistence of oxide-derived structures. [3,18–20] Comparing these modifications therefore enables a direct test of whether opposite capacity changes arise from opposite changes in the same reaction stage or from selective effects on different parts of the conversion sequence.

Here, pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO) are compared through first-lithiation GITT relaxation. Four electrochemically distinct regimes are resolved and assigned, with support from the relaxed voltage response and established conversion chemistry, to early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation. The capacity increase induced by ball milling is traced predominantly to expansion of the conversion-onset/progression regime associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction, whereas the capacity loss caused by Mg incorporation is localized mainly to the subsequent late-conversion regime associated with evolution toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived states. The comparison links macroscopic capacity changes to the specific parts of the conversion sequence altered by processing and composition.

## 2.2. Opposite capacity changes motivate a stage-resolved analysis of conversion

The conventional electrochemical response first establishes the macroscopic effect of the two material modifications. The first-cycle lithiation/delithiation capacities are 901.25/609.12 mAh g-1 for HEO, 1056.10/782.08 mAh g-1 for BM-HEO, 731.15/458.91 mAh g-1 for Mg-HEO, and 944.07/580.83 mAh g-1 for BM-Mg-HEO. Ball milling therefore increases the accessible lithiation capacity in both the Mg-free and Mg-containing materials, whereas Mg incorporation decreases it relative to the corresponding Mg-free composition.

These opposite capacity changes provide the materials-level observation, but they do not identify their reaction-stage origin. For the HEO/BM-HEO pair, the additional capacity introduced by ball milling is distributed over lithiation rather than confined to a single narrow voltage interval. Likewise, the lower capacity of the Mg-containing material does not by itself identify which part of the multistep conversion sequence has become less accessible. Voltage profiles therefore establish how much capacity changes, but not where within the conversion chemistry that change originates.

The following analysis uses the state dependence of GITT relaxation to resolve that missing information. Electrochemically distinct regions are first defined from the measured relaxation and relaxed-voltage behavior and are then assigned broad physical meanings using the established sequence of HEO conversion.

## 2.3. GITT relaxation fingerprints resolve four lithiation regimes

The first-lithiation GITT response of pristine HEO evolves through a reproducible sequence of relaxation features. Each GITT step consists of a 10 min galvanostatic pulse followed by a 60 min open-circuit rest. The characteristic time t63 is defined as the time required to complete 63.2% of the measured voltage relaxation between 3 s and 60 min after current interruption, while Delta Erest quantifies the corresponding relaxation magnitude. Together with the 60 min rest-end voltage, these quantities describe how the nonequilibrium response changes as conversion proceeds.

Three major landmarks organize the response into four regions. L1 marks the transition out of the initial lithiation response. L3 lies near the pronounced central minimum in t63, accompanied by a minimum in Delta Erest and a nearby maximum in the differential capacity derived from the relaxed voltage profile. L4 marks the end of the broad relaxed-voltage plateau and the transition to the steeper low-voltage branch. These landmarks define R1–R4; smaller extrema within a region are treated as internal features rather than additional reaction boundaries.

The physical interpretation follows the electrochemical segmentation. R1 corresponds broadly to early lithiation before the main conversion response. R2 spans conversion onset and progression, where oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction become prominent. R3 covers the subsequent late-conversion response, during which reconstructed states continue to evolve toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived products. R4 begins after the broad relaxed-voltage plateau and represents post-plateau deep lithiation. These assignments describe broad reaction regimes and do not imply four discrete elementary microscopic steps.

The resulting sequence provides a common electrochemical reaction coordinate for comparing the material modifications: early lithiation -> conversion onset/reconstruction -> late conversion toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived states -> post-plateau deep lithiation.

## 2.4. Ball milling capacity gain originates predominantly from conversion onset and reconstruction

Ball milling increases the total first-lithiation capacity, but the stage-resolved analysis shows that this gain is not distributed uniformly across the conversion sequence. The dominant increase occurs in R2, the conversion-onset/progression regime associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction. Secondary increases occur in R1 and R4, whereas the relative contribution of R3 decreases markedly. The familiar capacity enhancement produced by milling is therefore traced primarily to greater accessibility of the conversion-onset/reconstruction part of the reaction rather than to a uniform amplification of all four regimes.

The relaxation timescale provides additional information about the state created in each regime. t63 decreases in R1 but increases throughout R2–R4 after ball milling. The central minimum of pristine HEO becomes a broader shallow trough, and relaxation remains more persistent after conversion onset. Thus, the stage that contributes most strongly to the capacity gain becomes more accessible even though the subsequent nonequilibrium response does not relax more rapidly.

These changes are consistent with the microstructural perturbation produced by milling. Increased disorder, surface area, and interfacial diversity can broaden the population of accessible environments in which oxide destabilization and reconstruction proceed. The stage-resolved result narrows this general materials picture by locating the dominant electrochemical consequence of milling in the conversion-onset/progression regime. The structural interpretation remains deliberately broad because the GITT response does not identify a unique local reconstruction pathway.

## 2.5. Mg-induced capacity loss originates predominantly from late conversion

Mg incorporation lowers the total first-lithiation capacity, and the stage-resolved response localizes most of this loss to R3. R1 and R4 change little and R2 is only moderately affected. The macroscopic capacity decrease caused by Mg incorporation therefore arises predominantly from reduced access to the late-conversion regime associated with continued evolution of reconstructed states toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived products.

The remaining R3 response shows only a modest change in relaxation timescale. The median t63 changes weakly between HEO and Mg-HEO, whereas Delta Erest decreases and the relaxed voltage response shifts. Mg therefore acts primarily by limiting how much of the late-conversion reaction is accessed rather than by uniformly slowing or accelerating the entire conversion sequence.

This behavior is consistent with prior reports in which Mg-containing HEOs show reduced conversion accessibility or greater persistence of oxide-derived structures. [3,18–20] The present analysis adds a stage-resolved electrochemical result: the dominant capacity loss is localized to late conversion. The data do not establish a unique Mg site or a single bond-level stabilization mechanism, so the interpretation is limited to this experimentally resolved change in reaction accessibility.

## 2.6. Stage-resolved GITT links macroscopic capacity changes to their reaction origin

The Mg-containing pair provides a second test of the ball-milling response. Applying ball milling to Mg-HEO again redistributes accessible reaction away from R3, with the strongest gain occurring in the earlier conversion-onset/progression part of the sequence. Although the detailed fractions depend on the Mg-containing baseline, the dominant effect of milling remains an expansion of the reaction region associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction.

Conversely, Mg incorporation suppresses R3 in both the unmilled and ball-milled comparisons. The repeated late-conversion loss shows that the dominant Mg effect is not tied to a single particle morphology. Ball milling and Mg incorporation therefore generate opposite capacity changes through selective effects on different parts of the same multistep conversion sequence, rather than through a simple faster-versus-slower response of the whole reaction.

This distinction changes how the capacity differences are interpreted. The capacity gain produced by ball milling is dominated by expansion of conversion onset and structural reconstruction, whereas the capacity loss produced by Mg incorporation is dominated by suppression of subsequent late conversion. Accessible reaction extent is therefore a stage-specific materials response: the same overall conversion chemistry can be redistributed internally by processing or composition.

The resulting picture extends the established structural description of HEO conversion into a stage-resolved electrochemical map of materials response. GITT relaxation does not replace structural or chemical characterization and does not uniquely identify microscopic intermediates. Its value is to locate which part of an established multistep reaction is responsible for a macroscopic performance change, thereby connecting materials modification to the reaction process that is actually promoted or suppressed.

# 3. Conclusion

Spinel HEO lithiation was resolved into four electrochemically distinct regimes using the state-dependent GITT relaxation response and relaxed voltage profile. Supported by established conversion chemistry, these regimes correspond broadly to early lithiation, conversion onset with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction, subsequent late conversion toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived states, and post-plateau deep lithiation. This stage-resolved view identifies the reaction origin of the opposite capacity changes produced by the two material modifications. The capacity increase induced by ball milling arises predominantly from expansion of the conversion-onset/progression regime, whereas the capacity loss caused by Mg incorporation is concentrated in the subsequent late-conversion regime. These results show that macroscopic capacity changes can reflect selective expansion or suppression of specific parts of a multistep conversion sequence rather than a uniform change in the whole reaction. By linking a performance change to the reaction stage that produces it, GITT relaxation provides an electrochemical basis for deciding which conversion processes are being promoted or suppressed by materials processing and composition.
