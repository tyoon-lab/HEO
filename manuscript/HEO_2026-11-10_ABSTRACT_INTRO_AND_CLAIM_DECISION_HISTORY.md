## What the author is trying to achieve

The PI's desired *causal argument* is more important than minimizing word count:
1. Spinel HEO is a high-capacity anode. Particle refinement and cation substitution are established **interventions** affecting morphology and cation chemistry.
2. The question is **how modification influences the underlying lithium-storage mechanisms**, not merely whether total capacity changes.
3. Conversion lithiation includes overlapping transition-metal reductions, lattice rearrangements, heterogeneous metal/Li₂O product formation, and possible interfacial storage. Overlap prevents clean voltage-only **attribution of the added/lost charge among different reactions/intervals**.
4. Conventional total capacity provides the **net** outcome. Structural/operando spectroscopic methods *can be quantitative for oxidation state and local structure*, but a structural state sequence is not automatically the same as a signed GITT regional-charge comparison.
5. **Therefore** use pulse-resolved GITT **voltage relaxation** and t63-defined R1–R4 *electrochemical regions*, then compare their nominal charge in matched BM ± / Mg ± samples.
6. Interpreting regions via published HEO/conversion pathways gives **plausible candidate predominant processes**, clarifies where an intervention impacts accessible charge and motivates further structure/kinetic hypotheses. The electrochemical method does not directly image or quantify individual metal redox extents.
7. The main result expressed without abstract numbers at user request: milling's **largest regional gain R2** (additional gain R4, decrease R3); Mg's **largest regional loss R3** (not exclusively R3). Total signs differ, yet the R3 contribution is reduced in both intervention comparisons.
8. Materials-design implication: interpret *where* stored charge changes with chemistry/processing; do not claim complete microscopic elucidation of the mechanism.

**PI-established preferred first two sentences (preserve unless PI changes):**

> Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) have attracted considerable interest as high-capacity anodes for lithium-ion batteries. Improving their electrochemical performance has motivated studies of particle refinement and cation substitution, which modify oxide morphology and cation chemistry, respectively.

### Exact late PI-authored candidate — supplied in conversation before last Word review

This is **a candidate, not a confirmed final Word amendment**. It was authored by PI and deserves priority over assistant-rewritten text. Preserve verbatim here:

> Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) have attracted considerable interest as high-capacity anodes for lithium-ion batteries. Improving their electrochemical performance has motivated studies of particle refinement and cation substitution, which modify oxide morphology and cation chemistry, respectively. However, how these modifications affect the underlying lithium storage mechanism remains poorly understood. Because lithiation involves multiple overlapping processes—including transition-metal reduction, structural transformation, metal/Li₂O nanocomposite formation, and possible interfacial storage—quantifying the charge associated with each process is difficult, obscuring which processes are promoted or suppressed by material modification. Consequently, modification effects have been evaluated mainly through total capacity while relying on spectroscopic tools for qualitative and localized mechanistic insights, hindering systematic materials design. To overcome this limitation, this study proposes a galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) approach that partitions lithiation into four electrochemical regions (R1–R4) governed by dominant reactions, and quantifies how ball milling and Mg incorporation affect each region. Assigning the dominant reaction processes in each region based on prior mechanistic studies reveals which conversion stages respond most sensitively to each modification: ball milling primarily expands oxide reconstruction and early transition-metal reduction (R2), whereas Mg incorporation selectively suppresses continued conversion in R3 (further reduction of intermediate oxides and the formation of metal/Li₂O products). Interpreting these regional capacity shifts clarifies how each modification alters the conversion mechanism and guides the design of conversion-type electrodes.

## Important distinctions and precedent constraints

The term **dominant reaction** is allowed as an interpretation with relevant published mechanistic evidence. The PI explicitly rejected a purely phenomenological presentation. **But avoid equating an operational t63 boundary with proven purely segregated phases or sites.** The NiO GITT precedent [Ng et al., DOI 10.1039/D0TA09683K] did use GITT-derived SOC-region characterization, so this study cannot claim it invented all GITT staging. The novelty is using **t63-only operational landmarks and signed region charge comparisons across matched BM/Mg HEO materials with literature-grounded interpretation**.

- Primary HEO mechanism: Luo et al., Adv. Sci. 2022 DOI 10.1002/advs.202201219 (different Co-free CrMnFeNiCu spinel, contains Cu), Triolo et al., Small 2023 DOI 10.1002/smll.202304585 (Zn-containing HEO), Li et al., Angew. Chem. 2025 DOI 10.1002/anie.202518569 (size-dependent spinel intermediate, not BM in this study), Ma et al. Energy Adv. 2023 DOI 10.1039/D3YA00326D (Mg-related spinel composition but different).
- R2 mechanistic candidates: oxide transformation/rocksalt intermediates; some early TM redox/metal-Li2O onset.
- R3 candidates: further reduction of intermediates, metal/Li2O development and heterogeneous delayed re-equilibration. Pristine R3 hump 10–60 min observation is compatible but not a direct phase identification.
- R4: deepest low-potential accessed charge can involve residual conversion as well as possible interface/parasitic storage. A label “deep lithiation” cannot carry mechanistic explanatory burden.
