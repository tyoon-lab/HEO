## Proposed full revised Introduction

# 1. Introduction

Conversion-type transition-metal oxide anodes store lithium by reduction of the parent oxide toward metal and Li₂O products, but lithiation does not proceed as a uniform reaction. In situ electron microscopy studies of spinel Fe₃O₄ and Co₃O₄ have revealed initial Li uptake, intermediate structural transformations, and subsequent conversion with spatially coexisting phases [22,27]. Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) introduce additional complexity because multiple transition-metal cations undergo reduction over overlapping ranges of lithiation. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy has resolved metal-dependent changes across the voltage–capacity profile of spinel HEOs [7], while ex situ absorption and diffraction analyses have identified Li uptake, cation migration, and intermediate oxide reconstruction during lithiation [23]. Together, these observations show that different reaction processes become prominent along a continuous voltage–capacity profile, even when the processes themselves overlap.

Understanding this progression creates opportunities to control lithium storage through materials modification. Compositional changes, constituent-cation substitution, and particle-size control have been shown to alter the capacity and conversion behavior of spinel HEOs [9,20,24]. In particular, size-dependent structural investigations have demonstrated that reducing particle dimensions can improve the completeness of conversion, linking a materials variable to a specific reaction outcome [9]. However, a change in total capacity does not reveal whether a modification primarily affects early Li uptake, the onset of conversion, or later reduction. Identifying where the capacity change occurs would enable processing and composition to be compared according to the reaction processes they promote or restrict, providing a more systematic basis for materials design.

Structural and spectroscopic studies establish the chemistry of lithiation, whereas electrochemical measurements offer a means of following the corresponding response across successive reaction states. Voltage and differential-capacity profiles can reveal characteristic reaction intervals, although overlapping reduction and reconstruction in multication oxides may obscure their boundaries. Region-dependent electrochemical analysis has been used to localize capacity loss in Si anodes [25]. In conversion oxides, GITT voltage profiles have distinguished insertion and conversion responses in Fe₃O₄ and spinel HEOs [22,9], while GITT-derived apparent diffusivity and exchange-current trends have been used to identify four electrochemical regions in NiO, supported by further electrochemical and physical characterization [26]. These precedents establish that different reaction regimes can have recognizable electrochemical signatures. Less developed is their use to quantify which parts of the lithiation pathway account for capacity changes induced by processing and composition. The amplitude and characteristic time of GITT voltage relaxation, considered together with the rest-end voltage, provide complementary descriptors for making this comparison.

Here, pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO) are compared using first-lithiation GITT. Characteristic changes in relaxation amplitude, recovery time, and rest-end voltage distinguish four electrochemical regimes associated with early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, later conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation. The sequence of recognizable responses persists after modification, whereas the regional charge contributions change markedly. Ball milling produces its largest positive regional GITT-charge increment during conversion onset and progression, while Mg incorporation causes its largest negative increment during later conversion. This contrast identifies different electrochemical origins for their macroscopic capacity changes and demonstrates how region-resolved measurements can connect processing and composition to the reaction processes targeted in conversion-type anode design.

## Internal science caution
The word "prominent" means comparatively prominent not exclusive chemical reactions. R1–R4 are defined as electrochemical trend zones, not proven phase assignments. The NiO paper's four regions are determined from GITT *derived* parameters but chemical SEI/conversion assignments involve extra methods and model arguments. Claim of sparse work is a qualitative judgment from targeted primary-paper search, not exhaustive systematic bibliometrics. Comparisons of electrochemical region-specific nominal GITT charge are not proof of changed chemical conversion yields.
