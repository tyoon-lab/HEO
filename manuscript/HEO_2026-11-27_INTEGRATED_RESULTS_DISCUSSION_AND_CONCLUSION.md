# Integrated Results and Discussion and Conclusion — 2026-11-27

## 2.3. Electrochemical regions identified from first-lithiation voltage relaxation

Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) was used to characterize the first lithiation of the four HEO electrodes. Each 600 s current pulse at a nominal 100 mA g⁻¹ was followed by a 3600 s open-circuit rest (Figure 4). Three experimental descriptors were evaluated: the characteristic relaxation time, t₆₃, defined as the elapsed time from the 3 s reference to the first crossing of 63.212% of the signed 3–3600 s voltage recovery; the magnitude of that finite-window recovery, ΔErest = |Erest(3600 s) − Erest(3 s)|; and the potential at the end of the rest period, denoted QOCV. These quantities characterize different aspects of the voltage response. In particular, t₆₃ is an effective relaxation time of the measured electrode response rather than a directly determined diffusion coefficient or elementary reaction-rate constant [10–12].

The t₆₃ profiles exhibit three identifiable landmarks, L1, L3, and L4, that divide first lithiation into four operational electrochemical regions, R1–R4 (Figure 4). L1 follows the initial decline in t₆₃, whereas L3 corresponds to the central minimum preceding its sustained increase. L4 is identified from a late local maximum in HEO and BM-HEO and from a change in the late slope for Mg-HEO and BM-Mg-HEO. The cumulative pulse indices at L1/L3/L4 are 6/25/55 for HEO, 9/38/60 for BM-HEO, 7/24/42 for Mg-HEO, and 9/35/46 for BM-Mg-HEO. The boundaries describe changes along the continuous experimental relaxation trajectory; they are used for consistent charge accounting rather than as directly identified phase boundaries.

Previous operando X-ray absorption and structural studies provide a physical context for these electrochemical regions. Luo et al. reported different reduction and reoxidation pathways among transition-metal centers in a Co-free spinel HEO, including intermediate reduction states for Mn and Fe [4]. Triolo et al. described Li insertion, cation migration, and partially reversible conversion in a Zn-containing spinel HEO [5]. In situ observations of Fe₃O₄ further show that insertion and conversion can overlap within individual oxide particles [6]. Li et al. and Jin et al. identify oxygen-migration constraints and spinel/rock-salt-related phase evolution in spinel HEOs [7,25]. On this basis, R1 is associated primarily with early Li uptake and structural rearrangement, R2 with oxide destabilization/reconstruction and early transition-metal reduction, R3 with further reduction of intermediate oxide environments and development of metal/Li₂O reaction products, and R4 with late residual reduction and possible interfacial contributions at deep lithiation. These assignments identify plausible prominent process classes within overlapping reactions; the GITT boundaries themselves are defined by the measured t₆₃ trajectories (Figure S12).

The relaxation characteristics vary across the four materials. During the initial part of R1, t₆₃ decreases from comparatively long values in the unmilled samples, whereas the milled samples exhibit shorter initial relaxation times and a different early trajectory (Figure 4). A second distinction appears in the later part of first lithiation. Pristine HEO exhibits a pronounced ΔErest increase from approximately 145 mV near the R3 onset to 189 mV near pulse 50. Mg-HEO exhibits a smaller increase (approximately 109–118 mV), and both ball-milled samples exhibit declining ΔErest within their respective R3 intervals, even as t₆₃ increases (Figure 4). The different evolution of the relaxation time and amplitude indicates that these descriptors capture complementary aspects of the voltage response.

The additional recovery in pristine HEO is concentrated at longer observation times (Figure S4). Between pulses 26 and 50, ΔErest increases by 44.2 mV. Recovery from 10 to 60 min contributes +37.5 mV to this change, whereas recovery from 1 to 10 min contributes +16.5 mV and the initial 3–60 s contribution decreases by 9.8 mV. The growth of the late-rest contribution is consistent with increasingly prominent slow polarization or redistribution processes in this part of first lithiation. Transport gradients, evolving intermediate oxides, and phase-boundary rearrangement represent possible contributions in light of previous studies of conversion electrodes [6,20–24]. The voltage response alone does not isolate their relative magnitudes.

## 2.4. Region-resolved effects of ball milling and Mg incorporation

Integrating the nominal pulse charge within each t₆₃-defined interval identifies the electrochemical regions associated with modification-induced capacity changes (Figure 5; Tables S1 and S2). Each complete pulse contributes 16.67 mAh g⁻¹ on the nominal current basis. In pristine HEO, R2 and R3 account for 316.7 and 500.0 mAh g⁻¹, respectively. Ball milling increases the R2 contribution to 483.3 mAh g⁻¹ (+166.7 mAh g⁻¹) and decreases the R3 contribution to 366.7 mAh g⁻¹ (−133.3 mAh g⁻¹). The R1 and R4 contributions increase by 50.0 and 100.0 mAh g⁻¹, respectively, giving a net gain of 183.3 mAh g⁻¹ over the complete-pulse first-lithiation GITT sequence.

A similar direction of change is observed when ball milling is applied to Mg-HEO (Figure 5b). R2 increases from 283.3 to 433.3 mAh g⁻¹ (+150.0 mAh g⁻¹), R1 increases by 33.3 mAh g⁻¹, and R4 increases by 66.7 mAh g⁻¹. Meanwhile, R3 decreases from 300.0 to 183.3 mAh g⁻¹ (−116.7 mAh g⁻¹). The largest positive contribution of ball milling is therefore located in R2 in both compositional backgrounds, while the R3 contribution decreases in both cases.

Mg incorporation produces a different regional contrast (Figure 5c). In the unmilled pair, the R3 contribution decreases from 500.0 to 300.0 mAh g⁻¹ (−200.0 mAh g⁻¹); in the milled pair, it decreases from 366.7 to 183.3 mAh g⁻¹ (−183.3 mAh g⁻¹). These are the largest regional losses in both comparisons. Smaller negative contributions are observed in R2 (−33.3 and −50.0 mAh g⁻¹) and R4 (−66.7 and −100.0 mAh g⁻¹), whereas R1 changes by +16.7 and 0.0 mAh g⁻¹, respectively.

The reduced R3 contribution produced by both modifications arises from different movements of its operational boundaries. Ball milling shifts the R2/R3 boundary L3 to later pulse indices by 13 pulses without Mg and 11 pulses with Mg. In contrast, Mg incorporation shifts the R3/R4 boundary L4 to earlier pulse indices by 13 pulses in the unmilled pair and 14 pulses in the milled pair. Accordingly, the shared decrease in R3 charge is accompanied by different changes in the overall t₆₃ trajectory. Because the region limits vary with the intervention, a regional charge difference represents an electrochemically defined interval contrast, including boundary movement, rather than an independently isolated reaction-specific charge.

The observed signs are insensitive to small changes in the region boundaries. Independently shifting each of the three landmarks by ±1 pulse in each matched pair produces 729 boundary combinations. The R2 gain and R3 loss associated with ball milling persist across all combinations, as does the Mg-associated R3 loss (Table S3). R3 remains the largest Mg-related loss region in all unmilled comparisons and in 702 of 729 milled comparisons, with additional ties. This sensitivity analysis tests the stability of the operational charge contrasts; it does not represent independent cell-to-cell uncertainty.

## 2.5. Modification-dependent electrochemical responses in subsequent cycles

The complete GITT sequences also contain three successive lithiation/delithiation cycles in each of the four cells. They therefore permit comparison of the first lithiation with the subsequent reversible responses under the same pulse/rest protocol (Figure 6a,b; Table S7). The first lithiation delivers a substantially greater nominal GITT charge than the second and third lithiation for all materials. HEO delivers 1094.2 mAh g⁻¹ in C1 lithiation, compared with 715.0 and 710.5 mAh g⁻¹ in C2 and C3. The corresponding values are 1278.5, 860.9, and 844.4 mAh g⁻¹ for BM-HEO; 814.3, 476.1, and 462.8 mAh g⁻¹ for Mg-HEO; and 948.2, 577.6, and 549.6 mAh g⁻¹ for BM-Mg-HEO. These whole-half-cycle totals include the terminal partial pulse and therefore differ from the complete-pulse regional totals in Figure 5. Each material was measured in one cell, so C1–C3 represent successive histories rather than independent cell replicates. The first-lithiation-specific charge and more complex t₆₃ trajectory are consistent with substantial changes in the initial electrode state, including possible oxide reconstruction and irreversible interfacial processes [4–7,25,27].

The subsequent electrochemical responses become more similar, although their charge–voltage and relaxation characteristics continue to evolve. In particular, the first delithiation already exhibits a broad potential–charge response resembling those of C2 and C3, whereas the more complex first-lithiation t₆₃ trajectory is replaced by comparatively recurring C2/C3 lithiation responses. The rest-voltage recovery-amplitude feature remains observable in later lithiation even though the first-lithiation t₆₃ pattern is substantially altered (Figures S7 and S8). Thus, a similar voltage-response feature can persist while the contribution of individual relaxation processes changes across cycles. This behavior is compatible with repeated redox reactions in an electrode whose structural and interfacial state changes during initial lithiation, as indicated by structural and spectroscopic studies of other spinel and high-entropy conversion oxides [4–7,25,27].

The effects of ball milling and Mg incorporation on accessible charge persist in both later lithiation and delithiation. In C3 lithiation, BM-HEO delivers 844.4 mAh g⁻¹ compared with 710.5 mAh g⁻¹ for HEO; BM-Mg-HEO delivers 549.6 mAh g⁻¹ compared with 462.8 mAh g⁻¹ for Mg-HEO. Mg-containing compositions retain lower charge than their Mg-free counterparts. For the third delithiation, the values are 742.9, 827.6, 478.5, and 543.5 mAh g⁻¹ for HEO, BM-HEO, Mg-HEO, and BM-Mg-HEO, respectively. The effects on integrated charge are approximately multiplicative across the two modifications, whereas their voltage-dependent and time-dependent responses differ.

Comparison of normalized cumulative charge against finite-rest QOCV reveals a persistent ball-milling-dependent redistribution of delithiation charge from a relatively higher potential interval (approximately 1.40–1.70 V) toward a lower interval (approximately 1.05–1.40 V) in both Mg backgrounds (Figure 6c; Figure S9; Table S8). In a conditional C2→C3 contrast-transfer test using the measured C3 reference-material curve, a C2-calibrated potential-shift-only model leaves larger C3 delithiation distribution residuals than a minimal potential-dependent redistribution term (Figure S10; Table S9). This is not an autonomous forecast of C3 voltage or cycle life. This model term represents a change in the electrochemical charge–potential distribution, rather than a measured fraction of distinct chemical phases. The lithiation direction exhibits a different response and does not support imposing the same two-population description on both directions.

The temporal voltage response also changes with ball milling. In C3 delithiation, potential-matched t₆₃ increases by approximately 82 s in the Mg-free comparison and 52 s in the Mg-containing comparison using the 3–3600 s definition (Figure 6d; Table S10); the alternative 3–3500 s window gives approximately 81 and 50 s, respectively. In a separate analysis of the same 10–3500 s raw rest waveforms, the fraction of the total rest recovery attained by 300 s is lower by approximately 3.1 and 2.0 percentage points, respectively, relative to the corresponding unmilled reference. C2-calibrated response-shape changes are observed again in C3 in a conditional shape test using each target pulse's measured rest endpoints (Figure S11; Table S11). However, an effective time-axis transformation and an empirical nonlinear voltage-response transformation provide nearly equivalent descriptions of the C3 relaxation shapes. The measurements therefore demonstrate a persistent modification-dependent voltage-relaxation response while leaving the underlying kinetic and thermodynamic contributions nonunique. This distinction is important because the larger reversible charge of the milled electrodes coexists with longer effective voltage relaxation.

## 2.6. Physical interpretation and implications for materials modification

The first-lithiation region contrasts and the later-cycle responses provide complementary measures of the effects of morphology and composition. Multication spinel conversion oxides exhibit overlapping insertion, cation rearrangement, intermediate-oxide reduction, and metal/Li₂O formation rather than a single spatially or temporally uniform reaction [4–6]. Structural studies of spinel HEOs show that oxygen migration and the development of spinel/rock-salt-related states influence the reversibility of these transformations [7,25]. These findings support interpreting the present R1–R4 regions as intervals in which the relative contributions of coupled processes evolve, with the dominant process classes inferred from literature rather than uniquely identified from GITT (Figure S12).

For ball milling, the BET surface area increases by 4.6-fold in the Mg-free pair and 2.6-fold in the Mg-containing pair (Figure 2). Together with the larger R2 charge and delayed L3 landmark (Figure 5), this observation is consistent with altered access to reactive oxide domains during the initial reconstruction and reduction sequence. Prior work has shown that changes in particle dimensions can influence oxygen migration and conversion completeness in spinel HEOs [7]. This supplies a plausible mechanistic basis for an accessibility-related contribution to the ball-milling effect. The SEM/BET data measure aggregate morphology and gas-accessible area, while the GITT analysis measures electrode-scale voltage and charge; the results do not uniquely distinguish primary-particle transport length, electrical contact, wetting, surface reactions, or structural phase fractions. The persistent change in the later delithiation charge distribution and rest response is consistent with modification of the accessible reversible states produced during and following initial lithiation.

Mg incorporation is associated with a pronounced reduction of the R3 charge and a lower reversible charge in C2/C3. Literature on Mg-containing HEOs supports at least two contributions: substitution by a largely redox-inactive cation can change the inventory of reducible transition-metal centers, and Mg can participate in stabilization of residual oxide environments or suppression of structural deformation [15,19]. These functions have been demonstrated in different rock-salt and spinel compositions and are plausible explanations for the observed response of Mg-HEO. In the present study, the XPS Mg 1s signal confirms Mg in the near-surface sampling volume, whereas lattice-site occupancy and the relative contributions of active-metal dilution and oxide stabilization have not been independently determined. The Mg-related change can therefore be stated as an observed reduction in the electrochemically accessed charge, with literature-guided possibilities for its structural origin.

The prominent R3 recovery-amplitude feature in pristine HEO indicates a particular enhancement of late-time voltage relaxation during first lithiation. Its attenuation following ball milling or Mg incorporation may arise from different combinations of voltage-dependent thermodynamic response, transport gradients, reaction heterogeneity, and structural relaxation. The later-cycle data strengthen the distinction between these observables: the milled electrodes gain reversible charge while exhibiting a longer effective rest-relaxation time during delithiation. This coexistence is consistent with redistribution among accessible electrochemical states, but it does not identify a slower elementary charge-transfer reaction. Accordingly, material improvements should be evaluated in terms of the location of accessed charge, the potential distribution of reversible charge, and the voltage-relaxation response, rather than equating a single relaxation metric with conversion rate.

The proposed comparison is useful for studying modification effects without assuming that each GITT region corresponds to a discrete chemical phase. Earlier GITT work on a NiO conversion anode investigated competing conversion and SEI formation, establishing relevant methodological precedent [26]. The contribution of the present comparison lies in matched, region-resolved modification contrasts and their subsequent reversible-response signatures. First-lithiation R1–R4 charge contrasts identify where the measured lithiation trajectory changes, whereas later-cycle charge–potential and relaxation comparisons reveal whether the effects persist in reversible operation. In this four-material set, ball milling produces the largest first-lithiation charge gain in R2 and a distinct later delithiation response, whereas Mg incorporation produces the largest charge loss in R3 and reduces the overall reversible charge. Such combined observables provide practical criteria for comparing morphological and compositional modifications of conversion-type electrodes. The precise structural pathway remains composition- and history-dependent, as established by previous mechanistic studies [4–7,15,19,25].

## 3. Conclusion

The first lithiation of four spinel high-entropy oxide anodes is divided into four electrochemical regions (R1–R4) using changes in the characteristic GITT voltage-relaxation time. Region-resolved nominal charge comparisons show that ball milling produces its largest positive contribution in R2 (+150.0 to +166.7 mAh g⁻¹), while Mg incorporation produces its largest negative contribution in R3 (−183.3 to −200.0 mAh g⁻¹). Both modifications reduce the R3 contribution, but ball milling primarily moves its starting boundary to later lithiation and Mg incorporation moves its ending boundary earlier. The first lithiation exhibits larger charge and more complex relaxation than subsequent cycles. During C2/C3, the two modifications retain opposite effects on accessible reversible charge, while ball milling additionally alters the delithiation charge–potential distribution and effective voltage relaxation. Previous mechanistic studies of spinel conversion oxides support interpretations involving overlapping oxide reconstruction, transition-metal reduction, and evolving metal/Li₂O-related states. The combined regional charge and reversible-response analysis offers a quantitative basis for evaluating where morphological and compositional modifications influence the electrochemical behavior of conversion-type anodes.

## GITT method synchronization

GITT measurements over three sequential lithiation/delithiation cycles were conducted by applying successive constant-current pulses of nominal 100 mA g⁻¹ for 600 s (corresponding to 16.67 mAh g⁻¹ per pulse), each followed by a 3600 s open-circuit rest period to the respective cutoff voltages. Voltage transients were recorded at ~1 s intervals for the Mg-free cells and ~3 s intervals for the Mg-containing cells; to ensure rigorous comparability across all four materials, relaxation metrics were evaluated identically over the common post-interruption window of 3 to 3600 s.

For each GITT relaxation step, the finite-window recovery amplitude was defined as ΔErest = |E_rest(3600 s) − E_rest(3 s)|, and the characteristic relaxation time (t₆₃) was determined as the elapsed time from the 3 s reference to the first crossing of 1 − e⁻¹ (63.212%) of the signed 3–3600 s voltage recovery, with the crossing time linearly interpolated between adjacent timestamps. The potential at the end of the 3600 s rest was recorded as the quasi-open-circuit voltage (QOCV).

Operational electrochemical regions (R1–R4) were partitioned solely from operational landmarks in the t₆₃ profile: L1 marks the early post-transient minimum preceding a renewed rise, L3 marks the central minimum preceding the sustained mid-to-late increase, and L4 marks the late local maximum (in HEO and BM-HEO) or late slope inflection (in Mg-HEO and BM-Mg-HEO). The resulting cumulative pulse indices (L1/L3/L4) are 6/25/55 for HEO, 9/38/60 for BM-HEO, 7/24/42 for Mg-HEO, and 9/35/46 for BM-Mg-HEO, partitioning complete lithiation pulses into R1 (pulses 1 to L1), R2 (L1 + 1 to L3), R3 (L3 + 1 to L4), and R4 (L4 + 1 to the final complete pulse). Boundary-perturbation sensitivity across all 729 (±1 pulse) combinations is documented in Table S3.

Regional nominal GITT charge contributions were computed by multiplying the number of complete pulses in each region by 16.6667 mAh g⁻¹ per pulse across the 65, 76, 48, and 56 complete first-lithiation pulses of HEO, BM-HEO, Mg-HEO, and BM-Mg-HEO, respectively (excluding partial cutoff-truncated final pulses; Tables S1–S3). Whole-half-cycle nominal charges include all current-on durations, including terminal partial pulses; fully rested-pulse profiles require a complete 600 s pulse followed by a complete 3600 s rest. The C2-to-C3 charge-distribution and waveform analyses use the definitions and separate finite time windows in Sections S8–S10.
