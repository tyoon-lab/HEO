**Working title (provisional):** GITT Relaxation Fingerprints Resolve Material-Specific Perturbations of Conversion Lithiation in Spinel High-Entropy Oxide Anodes

## Abstract

Spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes undergo multistep conversion during lithiation. Yet the capacity changes produced by material modifications do not reveal which parts of this reaction sequence are altered. The galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) relaxation response during first lithiation resolves four electrochemically distinct regimes: early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation.

Ball milling and Mg incorporation produce opposite capacity changes, but their dominant effects occur in different parts of the conversion sequence. Ball milling increases capacity primarily through expansion of the conversion-onset/progression regime, broadly associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction. In contrast, Mg incorporation reduces capacity mainly by suppressing the subsequent late-conversion regime, associated with further evolution toward metal/Li₂O conversion products.

These findings demonstrate that macroscopic capacity changes can originate from selective expansion or suppression of distinct regions within the same multistep conversion reaction. Such stage specificity shifts the focus from how much capacity is gained or lost to where within the conversion sequence that change occurs, offering a more targeted perspective on the design of conversion-type anodes.

**Keywords:** high-entropy oxide; conversion anode; GITT; voltage relaxation; reaction-stage origin; ball milling; Mg incorporation

# 1. Introduction

Spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes undergo multistep conversion during lithiation. Operando and ex situ studies have shown that this process involves overlapping cation reduction, destabilization and reconstruction of the parent oxide, and the formation of metallic species and Li₂O. [3,6–10] Although these studies have clarified the structural and chemical evolution during lithiation, assigning changes in overall capacity to specific parts of the conversion sequence remains challenging.

Ball milling and Mg incorporation illustrate this ambiguity. Ball milling can increase the accessible lithiation capacity of spinel HEO anodes, whereas Mg incorporation can reduce conversion accessibility. [1,3,4,17–20] Their opposite effects on overall capacity, however, leave unresolved whether they modify the same stage of conversion in opposite directions or affect different parts of the reaction sequence. Identifying the dominant stage responsible for each change would connect materials processing and composition to the underlying conversion processes.

Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) provides a means of tracking the electrochemical response throughout lithiation through successive current pulses and subsequent open-circuit relaxation. In conversion electrodes, the relaxation response can reflect coupled contributions from Li redistribution, interfacial processes, and evolving reaction states, complicating its interpretation in terms of a single apparent diffusion coefficient. [11–15] Examining the relaxation magnitude and characteristic timescale together with the rest-end voltage can instead distinguish electrochemically different regions along the lithiation sequence without assigning individual features to unique microscopic transformations.

In this study, pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO) are compared using first-lithiation GITT relaxation. The response distinguishes four electrochemically distinct regimes—early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation—whose broad physical interpretation is guided by the relaxed-voltage behavior and established conversion chemistry. The capacity gain induced by ball milling arises predominantly from expansion of the conversion-onset/progression regime associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction, whereas the capacity loss induced by Mg incorporation is concentrated in the subsequent late-conversion regime associated with further evolution toward metal/Li₂O conversion products. These contrasting responses reveal that material modifications can alter accessible capacity through selective changes in different parts of the same multistep conversion sequence.

## 2.2. Opposite capacity changes motivate a stage-resolved analysis of conversion

The first-cycle voltage profiles reveal contrasting capacity changes induced by ball milling and Mg incorporation (Figure 3). The initial lithiation/delithiation capacities are 901.25/609.12 mAh g⁻¹ for HEO, 1056.10/782.08 mAh g⁻¹ for BM-HEO, 731.15/458.91 mAh g⁻¹ for Mg-HEO, and 944.07/580.83 mAh g⁻¹ for BM-Mg-HEO. Ball milling increases first-lithiation capacity in both composition pairs, whereas Mg incorporation decreases it in both the pristine and ball-milled materials.

These contrasting responses establish the macroscopic effects of the two modifications but do not reveal their origins within the multistep conversion sequence. The additional capacity produced by ball milling extends over a substantial portion of lithiation rather than appearing as a single isolated feature. Likewise, the reduced capacity following Mg incorporation does not identify whether conversion onset or subsequent reaction progression is primarily affected. Determining which parts of the conversion sequence account for these changes therefore requires an electrochemical description that resolves the lithiation response beyond its total capacity.

## 2.3. GITT relaxation fingerprints resolve four lithiation regimes

The first-lithiation GITT response exhibits pronounced changes in voltage relaxation as lithiation progresses (Figure 4). Each GITT step consists of a 10 min galvanostatic pulse followed by a 60 min open-circuit rest. The relaxation magnitude, ΔErest, is defined as the absolute voltage change between 3 s and 60 min after current interruption, while t63 denotes the time required to complete 63.2% of this measured relaxation. Together with the 60 min rest-end voltage, these descriptors track changes in the electrochemical response across the lithiation sequence.

Three prominent landmarks divide the response into four regions. L1 marks the transition from the initial lithiation response to the subsequent conversion-associated region. L3 corresponds to a pronounced minimum in t63, accompanied by a minimum in ΔErest and a nearby maximum in differential capacity derived from the rest-end voltage profile. L4 marks the end of the broad rest-end voltage plateau and the transition to a steeper low-voltage branch. These landmarks define R1–R4, while smaller extrema are retained as features within the corresponding regions rather than additional boundaries.

The voltage and relaxation features provide complementary information about the evolving conversion response. The broad rest-end voltage plateau indicates that substantial charge is accommodated with relatively little change in voltage, consistent with conversion-related transformations extending over a wide lithiation interval. The relaxation response can additionally reflect Li redistribution, interfacial processes, and the evolution of heterogeneous reaction states. Consequently, changes in ΔErest and t63 need not follow the voltage slope alone. In particular, the central minima near L3 indicate a change in the measured relaxation response but do not, by themselves, establish the completion of a specific structural transformation or a uniquely faster microscopic reaction.

The physical interpretation of the four regions is guided by this electrochemical segmentation and the established multistep conversion chemistry of spinel oxides. R1 represents early lithiation preceding the main conversion response. R2 covers conversion onset and progression, where progressive cation reduction, oxide destabilization, and structural reconstruction are expected to become prominent. R3 encompasses subsequent late conversion, consistent with continued reduction and evolution toward metal/Li₂O conversion products. R4 represents deep lithiation beyond the broad conversion-associated plateau. These processes may overlap, and the four regions should therefore be understood as electrochemically distinguishable parts of an evolving conversion sequence rather than four individually identified microscopic reactions.

This stage-resolved description provides a common basis for determining where ball milling and Mg incorporation alter the accessible lithiation response.

## 2.4. Ball-milling capacity gain originates predominantly from conversion onset and reconstruction

The stage-resolved GITT analysis identifies where the additional lithiation capacity introduced by ball milling is accommodated (Figure 5). In pristine HEO, R2 accounts for approximately 29% of the first-lithiation GITT capacity, increasing to approximately 38% in BM-HEO. The protocol-derived regional comparison identifies R2 as the largest positive capacity change among the four regions. Smaller increases also occur in R1 and R4, whereas the relative contribution of R3 decreases from approximately 48% to 29%. The capacity enhancement induced by ball milling is therefore concentrated predominantly in the conversion-onset/progression regime rather than distributed uniformly across the lithiation sequence.

The relaxation response provides a complementary distinction. Ball milling shortens the characteristic relaxation time, t63, in R1 but lengthens it throughout R2–R4. The pronounced central minimum observed in pristine HEO also develops into a broader, shallower trough after milling. Thus, the conversion-onset/progression regime accommodates a larger fraction of lithiation while exhibiting more persistent post-interruption voltage relaxation. These observations distinguish the accessible extent of conversion from the timescale of subsequent relaxation, which need not change in the same direction.

The enhanced R2 contribution is consistent with the microstructural changes expected from ball milling. Particle refinement, structural disorder, and increased interfacial accessibility may facilitate the participation of additional oxide regions in the onset and progression of conversion, where lattice destabilization and structural reconstruction become prominent. The longer relaxation response may reflect the continued equilibration of heterogeneous reconstructed environments, although its microscopic origin cannot be uniquely determined from GITT alone. The principal electrochemical effect of ball milling is therefore localized to the earlier conversion-onset/reconstruction part of the reaction, providing a stage-specific explanation for its capacity-enhancing effect.

## 2.5. Mg-induced capacity loss originates predominantly from late conversion

Mg incorporation decreases the first-lithiation capacity, with the stage-resolved GITT response identifying R3 as the principal region affected (Figure 6). The contribution of R3 decreases from approximately 48% of the total GITT capacity in pristine HEO to 31% in Mg-HEO. In contrast, R1 and R4 show relatively small changes, while R2 is only moderately affected. The capacity loss is therefore concentrated in the late-conversion regime, broadly associated with the continued evolution of reconstructed oxide-derived states toward metal/Li₂O conversion products.

The reduced R3 contribution is not accompanied by a comparably large change in its characteristic relaxation timescale. Although Mg incorporation decreases the relaxation magnitude and shifts the conversion-associated voltage response toward lower potentials, the median t63 within the remaining R3 response changes only modestly. This distinction indicates that Mg incorporation has a greater influence on the accessible extent of late conversion than on the characteristic timescale of the relaxation that remains observable. The smaller relaxation magnitude does not, by itself, establish faster reaction kinetics or a smaller fraction of any specific conversion product.

The preferential contraction of R3 is consistent with a change in the accessibility or energetics of subsequent conversion in the Mg-containing oxide. A reduced inventory of redox-active cations may also contribute to the lower total capacity, but uniform capacity dilution alone would not explain the pronounced decrease in the relative R3 contribution. Although the results do not establish a unique Mg coordination environment or bond-level stabilization mechanism, they identify late conversion as the principal electrochemical region affected by Mg incorporation.

## 2.6. Stage-resolved GITT links macroscopic capacity changes to their reaction origin

The combined material comparisons further establish the stage specificity of the capacity changes (Figure 6). Ball milling increases the contribution of R2 in both the Mg-free and Mg-containing electrodes, whereas Mg incorporation produces its largest regional capacity decrease in R3, regardless of whether ball milling is applied. Although the magnitude of each response depends on the material composition and processing history, the dominant region affected by each modification remains consistent. The opposite macroscopic capacity changes therefore reflect distinct redistributions of the accessible lithiation response rather than uniform changes across the entire conversion sequence.

The accompanying relaxation behavior provides an additional distinction between reaction accessibility and subsequent equilibration. Ball milling expands the conversion-onset/progression regime while prolonging post-interruption relaxation over R2–R4. Mg incorporation instead markedly reduces the contribution of late conversion, with comparatively modest changes in the characteristic relaxation time of the remaining R3 response. These contrasting responses indicate that the extent of accessible conversion and the timescale of voltage relaxation represent different aspects of the evolving electrochemical state.

The significance of this stage-resolved comparison extends beyond the overall capacity differences. By identifying where capacity is gained or lost within the conversion sequence, the analysis connects material modification to the specific reaction regions most strongly affected. This distinction provides a basis for evaluating conversion-anode modifications in terms of the accessibility of individual reaction regimes, rather than treating the multistep conversion process as a single uniform reaction.

# 3. Conclusion

The first-lithiation response of spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes was resolved into four electrochemically distinct regimes through GITT voltage relaxation and the rest-end voltage profile. The stage-resolved analysis revealed that the capacity gain induced by ball milling is concentrated predominantly in the conversion-onset/progression regime (R2), broadly associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction. In contrast, Mg incorporation reduces capacity mainly through contraction of the subsequent late-conversion regime (R3), associated with continued evolution toward metal/Li₂O conversion products. These distinct regional responses are maintained across the Mg-free and Mg-containing material comparisons, demonstrating that opposite macroscopic capacity changes can arise from selective changes in different parts of the same multistep conversion sequence. Identifying where capacity is gained or lost, rather than evaluating its magnitude alone, provides a more specific basis for understanding how materials processing and composition affect conversion reactions.
