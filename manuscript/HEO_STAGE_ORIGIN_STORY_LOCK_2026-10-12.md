## Adopted paper logic

1. Spinel HEO lithiation is already known to be a multistep conversion reaction.
2. Conventional capacity/voltage profiles show the macroscopic effect of a material modification but not its stage origin.
3. GITT relaxation separates first lithiation into four electrochemically distinct regimes.
4. Broad physical meaning is assigned only after the electrochemical segmentation, using relaxed-voltage behavior and prior conversion literature.
5. Ball milling and Mg incorporation affect different reaction stages.
6. The stage origin explains the opposite macroscopic capacity changes.

## Current headline

**BM:** capacity increase -> predominantly R2 expansion -> conversion onset/progression, oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction.

**Mg:** capacity decrease -> predominantly R3 suppression -> subsequent late conversion toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived states.

This is the Abstract headline.

Secondary but valid information:

- BM also increases R1/R4 and reduces the relative R3 share.
- BM increases post-onset relaxation persistence.
- Mg changes the remaining R3 t63 only weakly.
- accessibility and relaxation persistence are separable observables.

Do not let secondary findings displace the stage-origin headline in the Abstract.

## Physical sequence used consistently across the paper

Use one reader-facing sequence:

**early lithiation -> conversion onset/progression -> late conversion -> post-plateau deep lithiation**

Broad physical labels:

- early lithiation: initial reduction / pre-main-conversion processes;
- conversion onset/progression: oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction become prominent;
- late conversion: continued reconstructed-state evolution toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived states;
- post-plateau deep lithiation: low-voltage reaction after the broad relaxed-voltage plateau.

The list should remain grammatically parallel in the Abstract. Do not insert a long mechanistic clause into only one list item.

## Evidence boundary

GITT directly provides electrochemical relaxation/voltage behavior.

It does **not** directly identify:

- M–O bond breaking;
- a specific cation reduction sequence;
- a unique local reconstructed phase;
- exact Mg site occupancy;
- four discrete elementary reaction steps.

Therefore manuscript wording should follow:

**electrochemically defined regime -> broad physical assignment consistent with independent voltage behavior and established literature**

not:

**GITT directly detects a microscopic intermediate.**

## Why dominant-effect wording is required

Ball milling changes several regions, but listing all of them weakens memory and impact.

Current preferred hierarchy:

- **dominant:** R2 expansion
- secondary: R1/R4 increases, R3 relative contraction, longer post-onset relaxation

Mg:

- **dominant:** R3 suppression
- secondary: lower Delta Erest / relaxed-voltage shifts / weak change in remaining R3 t63

The Abstract should report the dominant stage responsible for the macroscopic capacity change. Full regional detail belongs in Results.

## Current Abstract

[ABSTRACT PROVISIONALLY LOCKED]

Spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes undergo multistep conversion during lithiation, involving progressive cation reduction, oxide destabilization and reconstruction, and the formation of reduced metal/Li2O-derived states. The GITT relaxation response during first lithiation resolves this process into four electrochemically distinct regimes: early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation. This stage-resolved response provides a basis for determining how material modifications alter the conversion sequence underlying the overall capacity. Ball milling and Mg incorporation produce opposite capacity changes, but the GITT analysis shows that they act on different reaction stages: the capacity increase induced by ball milling arises predominantly from expansion of the conversion-onset/progression regime associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction, whereas the capacity loss caused by Mg incorporation is concentrated in the subsequent late-conversion regime associated with evolution toward reduced metal/Li2O-derived states. These results show that macroscopic capacity changes can originate from selective expansion or suppression of specific parts of a multistep conversion sequence. By locating the reaction stage responsible for a performance change, GITT relaxation provides an electrochemical basis for connecting materials processing and composition directly to the conversion processes that should be promoted or suppressed.
