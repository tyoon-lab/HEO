## Frozen abstract

Faster conversion kinetics are generally expected to increase accessible capacity of conversion-type anodes at a given current within a fixed voltage window. However, conversion reactions couple multiple processes, including electron transfer, structural reconstruction, nucleation and phase growth, and cation/oxygen redistribution, such that greater accessible capacity does not necessarily imply faster overall conversion kinetics. Here, spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes are used as a model system, with ball milling and Mg incorporation providing complementary modifications that respectively increase and decrease accessible conversion capacity. GITT is used as the primary kinetic probe, and the relaxation voltage change and a characteristic relaxation time, $t_{63}$, are compared with accessible capacity. The two modifications reveal distinct capacity–relaxation relationships. Ball milling increases accessible conversion capacity but slows relaxation, contrary to the usual expectation that greater accessible capacity accompanies faster kinetics. Mg incorporation decreases accessible conversion and slows relaxation, as conventionally expected, yet the magnitude of the relaxation voltage change also decreases. Conventional GITT-derived apparent diffusivity fails to capture these trends; most notably, $D_{\mathrm{app}}$ indicates faster kinetics for BM-HEO even though its directly measured relaxation is slower. Microkinetic analysis shows that these apparently contradictory combinations of accessible capacity, relaxation magnitude, and relaxation timescale can arise within a multistep conversion network. The conversion response therefore cannot be reduced to a single fast–slow kinetic coordinate, and GITT-derived apparent diffusivity should not be used alone to infer overall conversion kinetics.

## Logic intentionally frozen

1. The abstract starts from the conventional expectation and identifies the scope as conversion-type anodes without fronting that phrase.
2. The second sentence states the microkinetic premise directly: conversion reactions couple multiple processes, so greater accessible capacity does not necessarily imply faster overall conversion kinetics.
3. BM and Mg are called **complementary modifications because they change accessible conversion capacity in opposite directions**:
   - BM: accessible conversion capacity increases.
   - Mg: accessible conversion capacity decreases.
4. GITT is introduced before the BM/Mg observations so the reader knows the source of relaxation voltage change and $t_{63}$.
5. “The two modifications reveal distinct capacity–relaxation relationships” is retained as the bridge sentence without prematurely forcing both modifications into the same type of mismatch.
6. BM is the primary capacity–rate contradiction:
   - capacity ↑
   - $t_{63}$ ↑ (slower relaxation)
7. Mg is deliberately described differently:
   - capacity ↓
   - $t_{63}$ ↑ (slower relaxation; directionally consistent with the capacity decrease)
   - relaxation voltage-change magnitude ↓
   The unusual Mg constraint is therefore magnitude versus timescale/rate, not a second capacity–rate contradiction.
8. The conventional $D_{\mathrm{app}}$ sentence states that the apparent diffusivity fails to capture the trends and highlights BM as the clean compositionally identical example.
9. “ranking” language is avoided in the abstract. Prefer “indicates faster kinetics,” “conflicting indication/trend,” or “fails to capture.”
10. “physically admissible” was replaced by the more direct “can arise within a multistep conversion network.”
11. The microkinetic sentence uses **accessible capacity, relaxation magnitude, and relaxation timescale** as the consistent three-way language.
12. Final implication remains bounded: apparent GITT diffusivity should not be used **alone** to infer overall conversion kinetics.

## Terminology notes

- “Observables” means experimentally obtained quantities, not arbitrary mathematical variables.
- In reader-facing prose, avoid repeating the word “observables” unnecessarily; use the specific quantities when clarity benefits.
- $t_{63}$ is a model-free effective relaxation timescale, not a microscopic forward conversion-rate constant.
- Relaxation voltage-change magnitude and relaxation timescale/rate are not equivalent measurements.
- Do not convert the Mg result into a stronger claim than the data support.
