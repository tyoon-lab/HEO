## Candidate revision for PI discussion

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) combine multiple transition-metal cations within a common oxide lattice and have attracted interest as high-capacity conversion-type anodes. Although their lithium storage can be summarized by an overall oxide-to-metal/Li₂O conversion reaction, state-resolved studies reveal a more intricate evolution during lithiation. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy has shown that Fe and Mn in spinel HEOs pass through intermediate oxide states during reduction to their metallic forms, whereas Ni and Cu exhibit more direct conversion pathways [7]. Ex situ X-ray absorption and diffraction studies have also identified Li uptake, cation migration, and spinel-to-rock-salt transformations during lithiation [Triolo 2023]. Related in situ observations in Fe₃O₄ reveal the coexistence of Li-containing intermediate oxide and metal/Li₂O conversion products [He 2016]. These findings indicate that reduction and structural transformation processes overlap during lithiation, with their relative prominence changing as the reaction progresses.
