# Figure 6 — Four-Step Homogeneous Microkinetic Analysis

**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** current main-text Figure 6 authority

## Scientific role

Figure 6 tests whether the experimentally observed higher-capacity/slower-relaxation ordering can arise within a homogeneous multistep conversion network without invoking spatial or population heterogeneity.

The literature-grounded effective sequence is described in the main text rather than occupying a figure panel:

\[
O\rightleftharpoons I\rightleftharpoons J\rightleftharpoons K\rightleftharpoons C
\]

- R1: initial electrochemical lithiation / first electron transfer
- R2: effective M–O dissociation / local structural reconstruction
- R3: effective Li2O-forming / product-side reconstruction
- R4: subsequent electron transfer / metal reduction

Primary literature basis: Ng et al., J. Mater. Chem. A 2021, DOI 10.1039/D0TA09683K.  
Reaction-network microkinetic precedent: Alsaç et al., ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2026, DOI 10.1021/acsami.5c20956.

## Panel architecture

### (a) Single-step limiting audit

Plot normalized cutoff capacity versus normalized t63 while R1, R2, R3, or R4 is varied independently from 0.1× to 10×.

Representative 0.1× results:
- R1: Q/Q0 = 0.893; t63/t63,0 = 0.959
- R2: Q/Q0 = 0.351; t63/t63,0 = 2.437
- R3: Q/Q0 = 0.495; t63/t63,0 = 1.573
- R4: Q/Q0 = 0.907; t63/t63,0 = 1.058

Interpretation:
- R2 and R3 provide the strongest internal control.
- Slowing either internal step alone gives the conventional lower-capacity/slower-relaxation response.
- The BM ordering is therefore not produced by simply slowing one step.

### (b) R2–R3 kinetic-regime map

Vary only R2 and R3 rate scales while holding all equilibrium parameters, R1, and R4 fixed.

Sweep:
- R2 scale: 0.12–1.20
- R3 scale: 0.8–50
- 396 total grid points

28/396 points (7.1%) satisfy both:
\[
Q_{\mathrm{cutoff}}/Q_0>1,
\qquad
t_{63}/t_{63,0}>1.
\]

The finite higher-capacity/slower-relaxation region appears when R2 is slowed while R3 is accelerated.

### (c) Observable-space projection

Plot the same model map in Q/Q0 versus t63/t63,0 space and overlay the experimental material ratios:

BM-HEO / HEO:
- Q ratio = 782.08 / 609.12 = 1.284
- t63 ratio = 11.57 / 8.68 = 1.333

Mg-HEO / HEO:
- Q ratio = 458.91 / 609.12 = 0.753
- t63 ratio = 11.01 / 8.68 = 1.268

Interpretation:
- Mg-HEO lies close to the conventional lower-capacity/slower-relaxation region of the model manifold.
- BM-HEO lies in the same higher-capacity/slower-relaxation quadrant generated by step-selective kinetics, but the experimental magnitude is larger than that reached by the minimal two-parameter model.
- Neither experimental point is a microscopic fit.

### (d) Current-off eigenmode explanation

Representative point:
- R2 scale = 0.465
- R3 scale = 50

Relative response:
- Q/Q0 ≈ 1.111
- t63/t63,0 ≈ 1.087
- slowest finite eigenmode: 15.35 → 17.68 min

Linearized current-off dynamics:
\[
\delta\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{J}\delta\mathbf{x},
\qquad
\delta E(t)=\sum_i B_i\exp(-t/\tau_i).
\]

Interpretation:
a slower internal eigenmode can lengthen current-off relaxation while a different downstream step sustains greater reaction throughput before cutoff.

## Claim boundary

Safe:
- homogeneous four-step kinetics can produce higher cutoff capacity together with slower relaxation;
- the ordering occupies a finite R2–R3 parameter region;
- different steps can dominate forward throughput and current-off relaxation;
- Mg is qualitatively compatible with a lower-capacity/slower-relaxation region of the same minimal network.

Do not claim:
- BM uniquely slows R2 or accelerates R3;
- Mg uniquely maps to one R2/R3 point;
- the model quantitatively fits BM-HEO;
- a unique HEO elementary RDS is identified;
- heterogeneous populations are required;
- the full experimental relaxation magnitude is generated solely by the modeled conversion sequence.

## Relaxation magnitude

Experimental ΔE_relax is not a quantitative model target. The measured 3 s-to-60 min voltage change can contain conversion polarization plus transport, interfacial, and state-dependent thermodynamic contributions. Use Li et al., JACS 2016, DOI 10.1021/jacs.6b00061 as the principal reference for this bounded interpretation.
