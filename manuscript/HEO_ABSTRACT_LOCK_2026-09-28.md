## Frozen abstract

Faster conversion kinetics are generally expected to raise accessible capacity in conversion-type anodes. However, conversion couples electron transfer, structural reconstruction, nucleation/phase growth, and cation/oxygen redistribution, complicating the link between capacity and overall kinetics. This relationship is examined in spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes modified by ball milling or Mg incorporation. Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) is used to characterize current-off relaxation in terms of the voltage relaxation magnitude and a characteristic relaxation time, $t_{63}$. Ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO) exhibits higher accessible conversion capacity but slower relaxation, contrary to the conventional expectation. Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO) shows lower accessible capacity and slower relaxation, as expected, while its voltage relaxation magnitude also decreases. Conventional GITT-derived apparent diffusivity, widely used to compare Li-ion transport kinetics, fails to preserve the observed kinetic ordering: $D_{\mathrm{app}}$ ranks BM-HEO faster even though its relaxation is slower. Microkinetic analysis recovers the conventional capacity–rate coupling under uniform kinetic acceleration but shows that a heterogeneous multistep conversion network can produce higher accessible capacity with slower relaxation. These results distinguish reaction accessibility from kinetic speed and show that neither capacity gains nor apparent diffusivity alone establishes improved overall conversion kinetics, providing a basis for evaluating materials modifications in multistep conversion systems.

## Current abstract logic

1. **Conventional expectation first.** Faster conversion kinetics are expected to raise accessible capacity.
2. **Do not state the paper's unexpected result as established background.** The background only establishes that multistep conversion complicates the capacity–kinetics relationship.
3. **Material system and perturbations are introduced without pre-announcing the result.** HEO is modified by ball milling or Mg incorporation.
4. **GITT is introduced before the result it supports.** Current-off relaxation is characterized through voltage relaxation magnitude and $t_{63}$.
5. **BM is the primary contradiction.** Accessible conversion capacity increases while relaxation becomes slower.
6. **Mg is complementary, not a second contradiction.** Lower capacity + slower relaxation is directionally conventional, while the relaxation magnitude also decreases.
7. **Conventional apparent diffusivity is explicitly contextualized as a common literature practice.** The HEO/BM result shows that $D_{\mathrm{app}}$ fails to preserve the relaxation-based kinetic ordering.
8. **Microkinetics first recovers the conventional limit, then explains physical possibility of the mismatch.** Uniform acceleration gives the usual capacity–rate coupling; heterogeneous multistep kinetics can give higher accessible capacity with slower relaxation.
9. **Impact is stated beyond the diagnostic warning.** Reaction accessibility and kinetic speed are linked but non-equivalent; material modifications should not be judged as kinetically improved from capacity gain or apparent diffusivity alone.
10. **AFM length constraint is treated as an architecture constraint, not a reason to delete scientific logic.** Compression removed repetition before removing claims.

- Keep **Faster conversion kinetics** rather than vague alternatives such as rapid conversion.
- Prefer an affirmative problem statement over prematurely writing the result as a negative background claim.

- Define acronyms at first use.

## Scientific boundaries

- $t_{63}$ remains a model-free effective relaxation timescale, not a microscopic forward rate constant.
- Relaxation magnitude is not itself a rate.
- BM-HEO is the clean same-composition apparent-$D$ audit.
- Mg-HEO is not presented as a second capacity–rate contradiction.
- The microkinetic model is an existence proof, not a fitted microscopic assignment.
- Do not claim that capacity and kinetics are independent; they are kinetically coupled but non-equivalent.
