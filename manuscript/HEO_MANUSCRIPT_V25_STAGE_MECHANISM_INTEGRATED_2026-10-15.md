## Abstract

Spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes undergo multistep conversion during lithiation. Yet the capacity changes produced by material modifications do not reveal which parts of this reaction sequence are altered. The galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) relaxation response during first lithiation resolves four electrochemically distinct regimes: early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation. Ball milling and Mg incorporation produce opposite capacity changes, but their dominant effects occur in different parts of the conversion sequence. Ball milling increases capacity primarily through expansion of the conversion-onset/progression regime, broadly associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction. In contrast, Mg incorporation reduces capacity mainly by suppressing the subsequent late-conversion regime, associated with further evolution toward metal/Li₂O conversion products. These findings demonstrate that macroscopic capacity changes can originate from selective expansion or suppression of distinct regions within the same multistep conversion reaction. Such stage specificity shifts the focus from how much capacity is gained or lost to where within the conversion sequence that change occurs, offering a more targeted perspective on the design of conversion-type anodes.

Keywords: high-entropy oxide; conversion anode; GITT; voltage relaxation; reaction-stage origin; ball milling; Mg incorporation

## 1. Introduction

Spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes undergo conversion during lithiation and can undergo reoxidation and reconversion during delithiation. Operando and ex situ studies of spinel and related multication oxide electrodes describe overlapping cation reduction, destabilization and reorganization of the parent oxide, and the development of metallic and Li₂O-containing conversion products during the reduction pathway. [3,6–10] The reverse electrochemical response may involve different oxide-derived states rather than a simple reversal of the original structures. Along the lithiation pathway, successive changes in the dominant reaction processes can coexist with substantial chemical and structural overlap. Identifying which part of this evolving response accounts for capacity changes after materials modification is therefore essential.

Ball milling and Mg incorporation provide contrasting materials perturbations within this problem. Processing can change particle dimensions, local disorder and interfacial accessibility, while compositional incorporation can change the inventory and local environment of redox-active cations. [1,3,4,17–20] The resulting changes in total capacity do not establish whether the modifications perturb the same portion of the conversion sequence or act predominantly at different stages. Identifying their dominant electrochemical regions would connect materials processing and composition more directly with the conversion processes being affected.

Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) tracks the electrochemical response across successive lithiation increments by recording voltage during current pulses and subsequent open-circuit relaxation. Changes in the pulse voltage, finite-window relaxation magnitude and characteristic recovery time contain information about how polarization and post-pulse equilibration evolve with the reaction state. In conversion electrodes, this recovery can reflect Li redistribution, evolving interfaces, structural re-equilibration and the local voltage–composition relation, making reduction to one apparent diffusion coefficient difficult to interpret. [11–15] The measured relaxation amplitude and characteristic time, considered alongside the voltage after a defined 60 min rest, therefore provide complementary landmarks for interpreting changes in the dominant electrochemical processes. The 60 min voltage remains an operational rest-end coordinate rather than a demonstrated equilibrium potential.

In this study, pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO) are compared using the first-lithiation GITT response. Combined voltage and relaxation features distinguish four electrochemically different regimes—early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation—and support interpretation of their dominant conversion processes. Ball milling produces its largest positive nominal GITT-capacity change in R2, associated with oxide destabilization and reconstruction, whereas Mg incorporation produces its largest negative change in R3, associated with further reduction and evolution toward metal/Li₂O-derived states. The regional comparisons therefore connect materials processing and composition to different parts of the conversion pathway.

### 2.2. Opposite capacity changes motivate a stage-resolved analysis of conversion

The first-cycle voltage profiles reveal contrasting capacity changes induced by ball milling and Mg incorporation (Figure 3). The initial lithiation/delithiation capacities are 901.25/609.12 mAh g⁻¹ for HEO, 1056.10/782.08 mAh g⁻¹ for BM-HEO, 731.15/458.91 mAh g⁻¹ for Mg-HEO, and 944.07/580.83 mAh g⁻¹ for BM-Mg-HEO. Ball milling increases first-lithiation capacity in both composition pairs, whereas Mg incorporation decreases it in both the pristine and ball-milled materials.

These contrasting responses establish the macroscopic effects of the two modifications but do not reveal their origins within the multistep conversion sequence. The additional capacity produced by ball milling extends over a substantial portion of lithiation rather than appearing as a single isolated feature. Likewise, the reduced capacity following Mg incorporation does not identify whether conversion onset or subsequent reaction progression is primarily affected. Determining which parts of the conversion sequence account for these changes therefore requires an electrochemical description that resolves the lithiation response beyond its total capacity.

### 2.3. GITT relaxation fingerprints resolve four lithiation regimes

The first-lithiation GITT response exhibits pronounced variations in the voltage recovery as lithiation progresses (Figure 4). Each GITT step comprises a 10 min galvanostatic pulse followed by a 60 min open-circuit rest. The relaxation amplitude, ΔErest, is the absolute voltage change between 3 s and 60 min after current interruption. The operational characteristic time t63 is the time required to complete 63.2% of that measured voltage change. These descriptors, together with the voltage at the end of the 60 min rest, follow the electrochemical response without imposing a single-exponential relaxation rate or treating the rest-end voltage as exact equilibrium.

Three prominent landmarks divide the response into four regions. L1 marks the transition from the initial lithiation response to the subsequent conversion-associated region. L3 corresponds to a pronounced minimum in t63, accompanied by a minimum in ΔErest and a nearby maximum in differential capacity derived from the rest-end voltage profile. L4 marks the end of the broad rest-end voltage plateau and the transition to a steeper low-voltage branch. These landmarks define R1–R4, while smaller extrema are retained as features within the corresponding regions rather than additional boundaries.

The three landmarks are informative because the measured voltage and relaxation responses probe complementary aspects of the evolving electrode. Across the extended rest-end voltage plateau, substantial lithiation charge is accommodated with comparatively small changes in voltage, consistent with successive reduction and structural reorganization occurring over overlapping potential ranges. The relaxation amplitude ΔErest reflects finite-window voltage recovery after interruption, while t63 describes its characteristic progression. Both depend on redistribution and interfacial equilibration as well as the local voltage–composition relation, so their variations combine several contributions rather than measuring the rate of a single elementary reaction. Nevertheless, their coupled evolution can identify transitions in the processes that most strongly influence the electrochemical response. In particular, the minima in ΔErest and t63 near L3, together with a nearby differential-capacity feature, distinguish the central conversion response from the later part of the plateau. This is evidence for a change in the dominant relaxation and charge-accommodation behavior, rather than direct proof that a specific solid phase has completed formation.

The electrochemical landmarks and known oxide-conversion chemistry support a stage-dominant physical interpretation. R1 covers initial Li uptake and the response preceding the extended conversion-associated plateau. R2 marks conversion onset and progression, where transition-metal reduction and destabilization/reconstruction of the parent oxide are expected to become particularly important. Such reorganization changes the available reaction sites and interfaces, allowing charge to be accommodated across a broad potential region while producing heterogeneous voltage recovery. R3 spans later conversion on the plateau and is associated with further reduction and evolution toward metal/Li₂O-derived products and interfaces. R4 follows the plateau end at L4 and represents post-plateau deep lithiation. These assignments identify the reactions expected to contribute most strongly within each measured regime, even though reduction, reconstruction, and product formation can overlap across neighboring regions. Consequently, the four regions have a mechanistic interpretation rather than representing four uniquely identified phases or independent microscopic steps.

This stage-resolved description provides a common basis for determining where ball milling and Mg incorporation alter the accessible lithiation response.

### 2.4. Ball-milling capacity gain originates predominantly from conversion onset and reconstruction

The region-resolved GITT partition identifies where ball milling contributes the additional lithiation capacity (Figure 5; Tables S1–S2). R2 increases from 29.2% of the nominal first-lithiation GITT charge for HEO to 38.2% for BM-HEO. On the original accepted pulse partition, the R2 increase is +166.7 mAh g⁻¹ of nominal GITT-protocol charge, the largest positive regional increment. R1 and R4 also contribute positively, while R3 decreases from 47.7% to 28.9% of the total GITT charge. These signed regional changes sum to the overall GITT-protocol difference; they must not be equated with independent first-cycle GCD capacities. The dominant positive BM contribution is thus localized to the conversion-onset/progression regime.

The relaxation response provides a complementary distinction. Ball milling shortens the characteristic relaxation time, t63, in R1 but lengthens it throughout R2–R4. The pronounced central minimum observed in pristine HEO also develops into a broader, shallower trough after milling. Thus, the conversion-onset/progression regime accommodates a larger fraction of lithiation while exhibiting more persistent post-interruption voltage relaxation. These observations distinguish the accessible extent of conversion from the timescale of subsequent relaxation, which need not change in the same direction.

The preferential expansion of R2 after ball milling suggests that additional oxide regions become accessible during the onset and progression of conversion. Mechanical processing can refine particles, introduce disorder and modify oxide–electrolyte interfaces; these are plausible routes by which a larger fraction of the parent oxide participates in early reduction and structural reorganization. Such changes could increase charge accommodation within R2 while different regions of the full conversion sequence may respond in different directions. Indeed, milling also decreases the R3 contribution and increases the R4 contribution, indicating redistribution rather than an isolated R2 effect. The longer observed t63 over R2–R4 shows that the enhanced accessible conversion response coexists with more persistent voltage equilibration after interruption. A heterogeneous ensemble of newly accessible or reconstructed oxide-derived environments may require longer for charge/Li redistribution and interfacial equilibration, even when the electrode accommodates more charge during the preceding pulse. These physical explanations are consistent with the electrochemical signatures but require direct verification of particle size, disorder and interfacial changes from the final microscopy, diffraction and surface-area datasets.

### 2.5. Mg-induced capacity loss originates predominantly from late conversion

Mg incorporation decreases the first-lithiation capacity, with the original GITT regional partition identifying R3 as the principal affected region (Figure 6; Tables S1–S2). Its fractional contribution falls from 47.7% in HEO to 31.2% in Mg-HEO, while the nominal GITT-protocol R3 charge decreases by 266.7 mAh g⁻¹—the largest negative regional change. In the same comparison, the nominal R1 and R4 regional charges remain unchanged and R2 decreases only modestly. The dominant Mg-associated capacity loss therefore occurs in the late-conversion regime, associated with continuing reduction and development of metal/Li₂O-derived states.

The reduced R3 contribution is not accompanied by a comparably large change in its characteristic relaxation timescale. Although Mg incorporation decreases the relaxation magnitude and shifts the conversion-associated voltage response toward lower potentials, the median t63 within the remaining R3 response changes only modestly. This distinction indicates that Mg incorporation has a greater influence on the accessible extent of late conversion than on the characteristic timescale of the relaxation that remains observable. The smaller relaxation magnitude does not, by itself, establish faster reaction kinetics or a smaller fraction of any specific conversion product.

The preferential loss of R3 points to a composition-dependent limitation in the accessible extent or energetics of later conversion. If Mg replaces an electrochemically reducible transition-metal species, dilution of the redox-active cation inventory can contribute to the overall capacity loss. Changes in local coordination and oxide stability may additionally modify how partially reconstructed oxide-derived states proceed toward further reduction and metal/Li₂O-derived products. The regional comparison provides a means of discriminating these explanations: a nearly uniform dilution of reducible sites would generally produce more uniform regional losses, whereas the measured decrease is concentrated in R3 and the R2 contribution changes comparatively little. Moreover, the comparatively modest change in median t63 of the remaining R3 response indicates that the loss of accessible late-conversion charge is larger than the change in the measured post-pulse relaxation timescale. Such behavior is consistent with modified participation in later conversion rather than an exclusively rate-controlled loss. Confirmation of a particular Mg site, bond-stabilization mechanism or change in an individual conversion product requires independent compositional, structural and state-resolved chemical evidence.

### 2.6. Stage-resolved GITT links macroscopic capacity changes to their reaction origin

The two matched materials comparisons support the same qualitative stage distinction (Figure 6; Tables S1–S2). Ball milling causes its largest positive regional GITT-capacity increment in R2 both without and with Mg (+166.7 and +133.3 mAh g⁻¹, respectively). Adding Mg causes the most negative change in R3 both without and with milling (−266.7 and −283.3 mAh g⁻¹, respectively). Because the increments are signed, some positive and negative regional changes compensate one another; no one percentage should be described as an independent fraction of the GCD capacity gain or loss. The consistency of the dominant regions supports a stage-specific, rather than uniform, modification of accessible lithiation.

The accompanying relaxation behavior provides an additional distinction between reaction accessibility and subsequent equilibration. Ball milling expands the conversion-onset/progression regime while prolonging post-interruption relaxation over R2–R4. Mg incorporation instead markedly reduces the contribution of late conversion, with comparatively modest changes in the characteristic relaxation time of the remaining R3 response. These contrasting responses indicate that the extent of accessible conversion and the timescale of voltage relaxation represent different aspects of the evolving electrochemical state.

The regional results also suggest different physical origins for the two modifications. Ball milling may preferentially increase the participation of oxide domains in R2 by altering their accessibility and the heterogeneity of reconstruction, while Mg incorporation may primarily alter the extent or energetic accessibility of further reduction after initial oxide reorganization. These interpretations are constrained by both the signed regional GITT charge changes and their accompanying relaxation trends: ball milling expands R2 despite longer subsequent t63, whereas Mg reduces R3 with a relatively modest change in the t63 of the remaining response. The resulting distinction between reaction participation, polarization and equilibration is more informative than treating capacity and relaxation rate as interchangeable measures. By locating the capacity gain or loss and identifying physically plausible causes for its localization, this comparison provides a stage-specific basis for evaluating processing and compositional strategies in conversion-type anodes. The microscopic origins of each localization remain open to verification by direct structural and chemical evidence.

## 3. Conclusion

GITT voltage and post-pulse relaxation distinguish four electrochemically different regimes of lithiation in spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes, enabling a physical interpretation of how the dominant conversion response evolves with charge accommodation. Ball milling produces its largest positive regional GITT-capacity change in R2, associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction, whereas Mg incorporation produces its largest negative change in the later R3 regime, associated with continued reduction and development of metal/Li₂O-derived products. The stage dependence is consistent across the Mg-free and Mg-containing material comparisons. The expanded R2 response following milling is consistent with greater accessibility of oxide domains undergoing early conversion, while the selective R3 loss after Mg incorporation suggests a stronger modification of later conversion participation or energetics. These interpretations remain conditional on independent structural evidence, but the localization of the electrochemical capacity changes is directly supported by the region-resolved measurements. Identifying where capacity changes arise and evaluating why different modifications influence different regions provides a more specific basis for conversion-anode design than overall capacity alone.

## 4. Experimental Section

### 4.1. Materials and electrode preparation

An original collaborator methods draft describes synthesis of the Mg-free parent HEO by coprecipitation followed by calcination. NiCl₂·6H₂O, FeCl₃·6H₂O, CoCl₂·6H₂O, MnCl₂·4H₂O and CrCl₃·6H₂O (1.4 mmol each) were dissolved in 20 mL of deionized water. NaOH (14 mmol) and Na₂CO₃·H₂O (7 mmol), dissolved in 10 mL of deionized water, were introduced into the metal precursor mixture with stirring at 800 rpm. Following precipitation for 2 h at room temperature, the solid was centrifuged, rinsed three times with water, dried overnight at 70 °C and calcined at 900 °C for 2 h with a heating rate of 5 °C min⁻¹. For the ball-milled Mg-free material, the source draft describes 2 g of parent HEO milled for 12 h at 500 rpm using an 80 mL stainless-steel jar and 5 mm ZrO₂ balls with a powder-to-ball mass ratio of 1:10. The same conditions are not assumed for Mg-containing specimens without independent collaborator verification.

### 4.2. Electrochemical measurements

Galvanostatic charge–discharge measurements were performed between 0.005 and 2.5 V. The principal low-rate comparison was conducted at 0.1 C, with rate-capability testing extending to 5 C followed by recovery at 0.1 C. The exact 1 C capacity basis and cycle sequence will be verified against the experimental record before submission.

GITT was performed at 100 mA g−1 using repeated 10 min current pulses followed by 60 min open-circuit relaxation. The Mg-free datasets were recorded at approximately 1 s time resolution and the Mg-containing datasets at approximately 3 s time resolution; a common 3 s post-interruption reference was therefore used for cross-sample comparison.

### 4.3. Relaxation fingerprint analysis

For each GITT rest, ΔErest = |Erest,60 min − Erest,3 s|. The operational t63 is the first post-interruption time at which 63.2% of this 3 s-to-60 min measured change has occurred. The first 3 s after current interruption are not resolved consistently across every sample, so the common reference avoids an instrumental sampling bias. t63 summarizes the measured voltage recovery rather than one fitted elementary reaction-rate constant. ΔErest includes transport, polarization, thermodynamic and reconstruction-related contributions and is not equated with a pure kinetic overpotential.

The 60 min rest-end potential is used as a consistent state coordinate; it is not asserted to be fully equilibrated. L1 identifies the transition out of the early response; L3 is the central relaxation-associated minimum corroborated by the relaxed-voltage and differential-capacity behavior; L4 marks the end of the broad relaxed-voltage plateau. These existing landmarks define R1–R4. The original accepted boundaries, rather than an exploratory alternative knee fit, define the present regional partition. The impact of independently shifting boundaries by one pulse is summarized as an internal sensitivity check (Table S3), without claiming a unique microscopic phase at each landmark.

Nominal region-resolved GITT charge was computed using the specified 100 mA g⁻¹ current and 600 s full-pulse duration, corresponding to 16.6667 mAh g⁻¹ per full pulse. Independent direct source-workbook extraction confirms 65, 76, 48 and 56 complete first-lithiation pulses for HEO, BM-HEO, Mg-HEO and BM-Mg-HEO, respectively, exactly matching the totals inferred from the previously accepted regional partition (Table S1). Signed regional contrasts are summarized in Table S2. The published-looking regional boundaries L1/L3/L4 still follow the original accepted analysis: their pulse indices were reconstructed from rounded archival regional capacities, not freshly selected by an alternate knee/minimum algorithm. Nominal protocol GITT totals are not interchangeable with separately measured galvanostatic first-cycle capacity (Figure 3).
