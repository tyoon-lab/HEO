# 3. Results and Discussion

## 3.6. Cycling behavior reflects the balance between accessible interface and interphase stability

The electrochemical consequences of ball milling are not limited to the first cycle. Under the common electrolyte containing 10 wt% FEC, BM-HEO maintains a higher absolute capacity than HEO throughout the 100-cycle test. The Mg-containing electrodes operate at lower capacity, with comparatively flatter capacity trajectories over the same interval. The four-sample comparison therefore preserves the same first-order ordering established in the initial voltage profiles: ball milling increases electrochemical utilization, whereas Mg incorporation decreases the amount of charge accessed from the oxide.

The control cells without FEC reveal the interfacial cost associated with this higher utilization. In the absence of FEC, BM-HEO exhibits a stronger capacity decay than HEO during extended cycling. This sensitivity is consistent with the 4.6-fold increase in BET surface area and the much larger capacitance-derived accessible interface after milling. A larger interface can expose a greater fraction of the active material to electrochemical conversion, but it also creates more area for electrolyte decomposition and repeated interphase reconstruction. FEC substantially mitigates this surface penalty, allowing the capacity benefit of BM-HEO to persist under the standardized electrolyte condition used for the principal comparison. FEC is therefore treated here as an interphase-control condition that stabilizes the synthesis-dependent response rather than as an independent mechanistic variable.

The post-cycle SEM images provide qualitative support for substantial surface reconstruction during cycling. Both HEO and BM-HEO differ visibly from their uncycled electrodes after 100 cycles at 0.1 C, confirming that the composite surface does not remain morphologically invariant during repeated conversion. The ball-milled electrode retains a rough, highly heterogeneous morphology, whereas the cycled HEO surface shows extensive film-like coverage and local rounded features. These images are not used to assign a unique interphase composition or degradation mechanism, but they support the conclusion that the enlarged interface created by milling changes not only initial accessibility but also the surface state developed during cycling.

The rate-capability response places an additional constraint on the interpretation of the GITT relaxation. BM-HEO remains at higher absolute capacity than HEO as the rate is increased from 0.1 C to 5 C and recovers the higher capacity when the rate returns to 0.1 C. The longer t63 measured for BM-HEO during GITT therefore does not represent a generic loss of practical rate capability. Instead, it describes a slower recovery of the distributed electrochemical/structural state after current interruption. The rate experiment probes charge utilization under continuous galvanostatic driving, whereas t63 probes the time required for the post-pulse nonequilibrium state to relax over tens of minutes. Their opposite trends show that these quantities capture different aspects of electrode dynamics.

This distinction is particularly relevant for a conversion-type HEO. Milling can simultaneously increase accessible interface, shorten some local transport distances, and expose additional reaction sites while broadening the structural transformation over a wider range of local environments. The first effects support higher capacity under load, whereas the latter can produce a longer post-pulse structural/electrochemical relaxation. Describing the complete response with a single diffusion coefficient would obscure this coexistence.

Differential-capacity profiles provide an additional connection between the galvanostatic and GITT responses. The strongest first-cycle reduction feature occurs in the low-voltage region around the pronounced conversion plateau and evolves substantially during subsequent cycles. This voltage range overlaps the region in which the GITT relaxation hump becomes prominent. The differential-capacity response is not used as independent proof of a specific phase fraction because electrolyte reduction and conversion reactions can overlap during the first cycle. Its voltage coincidence nevertheless supports the assignment of the late-stage GITT feature to conversion-associated structural evolution rather than to a state-independent resistance.

The EIS series acquired during cycling also show pronounced state and history dependence, but several spectra contain unstable or outlying responses and the present dataset has not yet been reduced to a uniquely defensible equivalent-circuit parameter series. EIS is therefore kept as supporting information rather than used to define the central mechanism. This separation avoids assigning physical meaning to a fitted resistance before the spectral reproducibility and state matching are fully established.

## 3.7. Ball milling and Mg incorporation regulate different coordinates of the same conversion reaction

The combined structural and electrochemical results resolve the distinct roles of processing and composition. Ball milling primarily changes **accessibility and distribution**. It increases the physical surface area from 3.94 to 18.159 m2 g−1 in the Mg-free material and strongly increases the capacitance-derived accessible interface. The larger interface is accompanied by higher first-cycle and sustained galvanostatic capacity. At the same time, milling broadens the late-stage transformation-associated GITT feature, lowers its local peak amplitude, and lengthens the subsequent relaxation. These changes are consistent with a mechanically disordered and heterogeneous oxide in which a broader population of local environments participates in conversion.

Mg incorporation primarily changes **transformation extent and structural stability**. Mg-HEO has a larger BET area than HEO yet stores less charge, excluding loss of external surface area as the origin of the lower capacity. Mg strongly suppresses the late-stage excess polarization and the associated capacity while leaving the characteristic relaxation time unchanged or longer. The reduced polarization therefore does not result from a uniformly faster kinetic state. Instead, the data and Mg-containing HEO literature support a spectator/stabilizing role in which Mg reduces the extent of the spinel-to-rock-salt/conversion reconstruction and preserves a larger fraction of the oxide-derived structure.

The BM-Mg-HEO sample provides an internal test of this interpretation. Milling again increases accessible capacity and interfacial area in the Mg-containing composition, but it does not restore the pronounced HEO-like late-stage transition hump. Processing can therefore increase the fraction of the Mg-containing electrode that is electrochemically accessed, but it does not reverse the stronger compositional constraint imposed by Mg on the conversion pathway. This result separates two effects that would be difficult to distinguish from conventional capacity or apparent-diffusion plots alone: the amount of material that can participate and the extent of the structural transformation available to that material.

The four-sample matrix can consequently be summarized along two independent coordinates. The first is **electrochemical accessibility**, which is increased strongly by ball milling through surface-area and interface enlargement. The second is **conversion extent**, which is reduced strongly by Mg incorporation through structural stabilization. The dynamic GITT response adds a third observable, **relaxation time**, which does not follow either capacity or polarization amplitude monotonically. Ball milling raises utilization while slowing the long-rest response; Mg lowers polarization while also slowing or preserving the relaxation time. These orthogonal trends explain why no single apparent kinetic parameter can represent the synthesis-dependent behavior of the HEO electrodes.

The resulting mechanistic picture is therefore not that ball milling accelerates Li diffusion while Mg slows it. Ball milling broadens and redistributes the phase-transforming reaction while increasing the accessible fraction of the electrode. Mg suppresses the extent of that transformation and sacrifices conversion capacity in exchange for structural stabilization. Their combination allows accessibility and phase-evolution extent to be tuned separately. This distinction provides the principal synthesis–electrochemistry connection of the present work.

# 4. Conclusions

Spinel Fe–Co–Ni–Cr–Mn high-entropy oxide anodes and their ball-milled and Mg-containing derivatives were compared using a 2 × 2 composition/process design to separate electrochemical accessibility from structural stabilization. Ball milling increased the BET surface area from 3.94 to 18.159 m2 g−1 in the Mg-free material and substantially increased the electrochemically accessible interface and specific capacity. This improvement did not coincide with uniformly faster current-off kinetics. BM-HEO showed nearly the same apparent fast current-off resistance as HEO over the main lithiation interval but a longer characteristic relaxation time, while the late-stage excess polarization became lower in peak amplitude and broader in capacity. Ball milling is therefore assigned primarily to increased reaction accessibility and a broader distribution of the conversion transition rather than to a simple increase in Li diffusivity.

Mg incorporation produced the opposite electrochemical trade-off. The accessible capacity and late-stage transition-associated polarization were both reduced, whereas the characteristic relaxation time did not become shorter. Combined with the larger BET area of Mg-HEO relative to HEO and literature evidence for the stabilizing/spectator role of Mg in conversion-type HEOs, this response is attributed predominantly to suppression of the extent of the spinel-to-rock-salt/conversion transformation. Ball milling of the Mg-containing material increased utilization but did not restore the pronounced Mg-free transition response, indicating that processing changes accessibility while composition sets a stronger constraint on transformation extent.

The combined results show that accessible capacity, polarization amplitude, practical rate utilization, and post-pulse relaxation time are distinct descriptors of a phase-evolving HEO electrode. Separating these quantities reveals how Mg incorporation and mechanical processing regulate different coordinates of the same conversion reaction and provides a more direct synthesis–electrochemistry connection than interpretation based on a single apparent GITT diffusion coefficient.
