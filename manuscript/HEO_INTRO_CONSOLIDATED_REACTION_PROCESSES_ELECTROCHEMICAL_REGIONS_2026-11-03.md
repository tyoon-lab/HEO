# 1. Introduction

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) combine multiple transition-metal cations within a common oxide lattice and have attracted interest as high-capacity conversion-type anodes. Although their lithium storage can be summarized by an overall oxide-to-metal/Li₂O conversion reaction, state-resolved studies reveal a more intricate evolution during lithiation. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy has shown that the constituent metal cations in spinel HEOs undergo reduction over different, partly overlapping ranges of lithiation [7]. Ex situ X-ray absorption and diffraction studies have identified Li uptake, cation migration, and intermediate oxide reconstruction before and during metal-forming conversion [23]. Related intermediate oxide transformations have also been observed in conventional spinel oxides such as Fe₃O₄ [22]. These observations indicate that cation-reduction and structural transformation processes overlap during HEO lithiation, while their relative contributions evolve with voltage and lithiation capacity. Particular processes may therefore become more prominent over certain parts of lithiation.

This progression raises an important question when HEO materials are modified: which part of lithiation accounts for the resulting change in capacity? Studies on particle size, cation composition, and Li₂O incorporation have documented changes in HEO capacity and conversion behavior [9,20,24]. Previous studies have examined changes in the structure and overall capacity of modified spinel HEOs, while the distribution of these capacity changes across the lithiation process remains less well understood. A modification may affect charge storage similarly across lithiation or exert unequal and even opposing effects on different portions of the reaction. Quantifying these contributions would reveal where processing and composition have their greatest effects and inform targeted materials design.

Changes in the dominant reaction processes during lithiation can alter both the thermodynamic and kinetic behavior of the electrode. Low-rate voltage profiles contain information about reaction thermodynamics, but broad conversion plateaus and overlapping reactions often produce similar voltages over substantial charge intervals and poorly separated differential-capacity features [7,23]. Kinetic responses may provide additional contrast where these voltage features overlap. Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) probes these responses by recording voltage relaxation after successive current pulses. The relaxation amplitude and characteristic relaxation time describe the magnitude and timescale of voltage recovery, respectively, providing complementary information on the evolving electrochemical response. The voltage at the end of each rest period additionally provides a quasi-open-circuit voltage (QOCV), offering a quasi-thermodynamic reference for interpreting the relaxation behavior. Changes in the relaxation descriptors, considered alongside the QOCV profile, provide a basis for defining electrochemical regions and comparing their charge contributions across modified HEOs.

Ball milling and Mg incorporation were selected to examine how processing and cation composition affect the lithiation response of spinel HEOs. Previous studies have shown that particle refinement and Mg substitution can influence their electrochemical behavior [4,27]. First-lithiation GITT was applied to pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO), allowing the effects of each modification to be compared with and without the other. Across these matched comparisons, ball milling increases first-lithiation capacity, whereas Mg incorporation decreases it. Region-resolved GITT charge shows that ball milling produces its largest positive increment in R2, associated with conversion onset and progression, although its R3 contribution decreases. Mg incorporation, in contrast, produces its largest negative increment in R3, associated with later conversion. These differences show how total capacity changes arise from unequal regional contributions and identify the parts of lithiation most responsive to processing and composition.

## 2026-11-03 PI clarification — P3 thermodynamics/kinetics and amplitude

- P3 now starts from changes in electrode **thermodynamic state** and **kinetic behavior**, rather than unnecessarily specifying "reaction potential" and "kinetics of voltage relaxation" in the framing sentence.
- A low-rate voltage curve contains thermodynamic information; broad conversion plateaus and overlapping processes can obscure distinct contributions (and finite-current curves are not identical to strict equilibrium curves). GITT adds kinetic contrast where voltage alone is subtle.
- **QOCV** = measured rest-end voltage after an explicitly finite relaxation protocol (60 min for this dataset). It provides quasi-thermodynamic information, **not certified equilibrium OCV**; review Kim et al., JECST 2022 DOI 10.33961/jecst.2021.00836 notes 20+ day Fe3O4 relaxation in prior study and finite-rest/overpotential conventions.
- **Characteristic relaxation time (t63)** preferred terminology. Actual protocol uses 63.2% of finite-window voltage recovery from 3 s to 3600 s; operational collective timescale, not single-component relaxation constant.
- **Relaxation amplitude ΔErest = |E(3600s)-E(3s)|** describes how much potential recovers in that window. It is neither the *amount of stored charge* nor *phase fraction*. Magnitude depends on kinetic polarizations, ΔU/Δx thermodynamic slope, the relative amplitudes of relaxation processes, and finite-time window. Same GITT pulse has fixed nominal charge yet amplitude varies.

## Editorial update — 2026-11-03: RC physics and rationale for BM versus Mg

- The PI challenged the earlier claim that high polarization could coexist with fast relaxation. A **single RC branch** with fixed effective C and long pulse has polarization ∼IR and tau=RC: higher R tends to increase both magnitude and time. Yet independent variation of R and C or multiple polarization paths permits high-amplitude fast recovery and low-amplitude slow recovery. Numerical illustrative pairs (not HEO fits) at I=10 mA: R=10 Ω, C=1 F ⇒ τ=10 s, long-pulse overpotential ~100 mV; R=2 Ω, C=100 F ⇒ τ=200 s, ~20 mV. In our measurement only t=3–3600s after interrupt is counted; processes substantially faster than 3s are omitted. ΔErest and t63 need not correlate or reveal unique process physics.
- PI approved exact wording in P3: “The relaxation amplitude and characteristic relaxation time describe the magnitude and timescale of voltage recovery, respectively, providing complementary information on the evolving electrochemical response.”
- Why BM and Mg: **BM** probes processing/particle morphology and experimentally yields BET area 3.94→18.159 m² g⁻¹ (pristine pair) and 6.49→16.64 m² g⁻¹ (Mg pair), but neither surface area nor earlier microscopy alone proves distinct intrinsic reduction kinetics. Literature [4] Zhai 2024 particle fragmentation/milling; [17] Chen 2020 processing with ball milling. **Mg** probes compositional substitution with possible balance between redox-active and structurally stabilizing cations, backed by [20] Xiao 2023 and [21] Liu et al. 2026. Mg is not universally electrochemically inactive in all high-entropy oxides (see e.g. ACS AMI 2022 DOI 10.1021/acsami.2c11038). Thus "ball milling increases capacity, whereas Mg reduces it" is a **grounded matched-sample result here**, not a universal prior-art fact. GCD first lithiation HEO 901.25, BM 1056.10, Mg 731.15, BM-Mg 944.07 mAh/g. Do not conflate GCD and nominal GITT regional charge.

Avoid a fifth paragraph or extended separate BM/Mg survey, unless PI later specifically wants more emphasis.
