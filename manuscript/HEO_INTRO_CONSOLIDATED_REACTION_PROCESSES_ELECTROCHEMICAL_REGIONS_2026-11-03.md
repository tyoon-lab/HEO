# 1. Introduction

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) combine multiple transition-metal cations within a common oxide lattice and have attracted interest as high-capacity conversion-type anodes. Although their lithium storage can be summarized by an overall oxide-to-metal/Li₂O conversion reaction, state-resolved studies reveal a more intricate evolution during lithiation. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy has shown that the constituent metal cations in spinel HEOs undergo reduction over different, partly overlapping ranges of lithiation [7]. Ex situ X-ray absorption and diffraction studies have identified Li uptake, cation migration, and intermediate oxide reconstruction before and during metal-forming conversion [23]. Related intermediate oxide transformations have also been observed in conventional spinel oxides such as Fe₃O₄ [22]. These observations establish that HEO lithiation involves overlapping cation-reduction and structural transformation processes whose relative contributions evolve as lithiation proceeds.

As the contributions of these processes change during lithiation, materials modification may affect them to different extents. Previous studies have shown that changes in particle size, cation composition, and Li₂O incorporation alter the capacity and conversion behavior of spinel HEOs [9,20,24]. These investigations have related materials characteristics and structural changes at selected lithiation states to electrochemical performance. Capacity changes, however, are commonly evaluated over the full lithiation range, leaving the contributions of individual parts of the conversion reaction less clearly resolved. A modification could increase charge storage during one part of lithiation while decreasing it during another. Quantifying these contributions would reveal where processing and compositional changes exert their greatest effects and provide a basis for directing materials design toward particular conversion processes.

Changes in the relative contributions of the reaction processes should also appear in the electrochemical response. In conversion electrodes, substantial charge can be stored across a broad voltage plateau, where overlapping reduction and structural changes produce only subtle variations in voltage and poorly separated differential-capacity features [7,23]. Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) adds information from the time-dependent voltage recovery after each current pulse. This relaxation reflects Li redistribution as well as interfacial and structural changes, complicating the interpretation of apparent chemical diffusion coefficients based on simplified transport assumptions [11–13]. GITT-derived electrochemical parameters have previously distinguished regions with different dominant reactions in a NiO conversion anode [26]. The measured relaxation amplitude and characteristic recovery time, examined alongside the rest-end voltage, provide complementary signatures for identifying electrochemical regions and comparing their charge contributions among modified materials.

In this study, first-lithiation GITT is used to compare pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO). Changes in rest-end voltage, relaxation amplitude, and characteristic recovery time distinguish four electrochemical regions. Established HEO conversion chemistry provides a basis for interpreting the dominant reaction processes associated with each region. The sequence of electrochemical responses remains recognizable across the four materials, while their regional charge contributions differ substantially. Ball milling produces its largest positive regional GITT-charge increment during conversion onset and progression, whereas Mg incorporation produces its largest negative increment during later conversion. These contrasting effects locate the principal capacity changes within different parts of lithiation and support the design of HEO anodes by targeting specific conversion processes.
