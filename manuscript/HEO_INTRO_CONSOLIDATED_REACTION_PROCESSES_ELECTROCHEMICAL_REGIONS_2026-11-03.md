# 1. Introduction

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) combine multiple transition-metal cations within a common oxide lattice and have attracted interest as high-capacity conversion-type anodes. Although their lithium storage can be summarized by an overall oxide-to-metal/Li₂O conversion reaction, state-resolved studies reveal a more intricate evolution during lithiation. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy has shown that the constituent metal cations in spinel HEOs undergo reduction over different, partly overlapping ranges of lithiation [7]. Ex situ X-ray absorption and diffraction studies have identified Li uptake, cation migration, and intermediate oxide reconstruction before and during metal-forming conversion [23]. Related intermediate oxide transformations have also been observed in conventional spinel oxides such as Fe₃O₄ [22]. These observations indicate that cation-reduction and structural transformation processes overlap during HEO lithiation, while their relative contributions evolve with voltage and lithiation capacity. Particular processes may therefore become more prominent over certain parts of lithiation.

This progression raises an important question when HEO materials are modified: which part of lithiation accounts for the resulting change in capacity? Studies on particle size, cation composition, and Li₂O incorporation have documented changes in HEO capacity and conversion behavior [9,20,24]. Previous studies have examined changes in the structure and overall capacity of modified spinel HEOs, while the distribution of these capacity changes across the lithiation process remains less well understood. A modification may affect charge storage similarly across lithiation or exert unequal and even opposing effects on different portions of the reaction. Quantifying these contributions would reveal where processing and composition have their greatest effects and inform targeted materials design.

Changes in the dominant reaction processes can affect both the reaction potential and the kinetics of voltage relaxation. The broad voltage plateau characteristic of conversion electrodes can accommodate substantial charge while overlapping reduction and structural changes produce subtle variations in voltage and poorly separated differential-capacity features [7,23]. Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) adds time-resolved information by measuring voltage relaxation after successive current pulses. The characteristic recovery time describes how quickly the coupled transport, interfacial, and structural processes relax, while the relaxation amplitude measures the associated voltage recovery. Conventional estimates of apparent chemical diffusion coefficients from GITT rely on simplified transport assumptions that become challenging for conversion electrodes undergoing structural and interfacial changes [11–13]. In NiO, GITT-derived apparent diffusivity, apparent exchange current, and open-circuit potential trends have been used to identify four electrochemical regions; complementary voltammetric and physical analyses distinguished regions dominated by conversion from those strongly affected by SEI formation [26]. For HEOs, the measured relaxation amplitude and characteristic recovery time, considered alongside the rest-end voltage, provide electrochemical signatures for defining lithiation regions and comparing their charge contributions across modified materials.

In this study, first-lithiation GITT is used to compare pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO). Changes in rest-end voltage, relaxation amplitude, and characteristic recovery time distinguish four electrochemical regions. Established HEO conversion chemistry provides a basis for interpreting the dominant reaction processes associated with each region. The sequence of electrochemical responses remains recognizable across the four materials, while their regional charge contributions differ substantially. Ball milling produces its largest positive regional GITT-charge increment during conversion onset and progression, whereas Mg incorporation produces its largest negative increment during later conversion. These contrasting effects locate the principal capacity changes within different parts of lithiation and support the design of HEO anodes by targeting specific conversion processes.
