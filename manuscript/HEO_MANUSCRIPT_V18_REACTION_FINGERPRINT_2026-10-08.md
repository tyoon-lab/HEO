# GITT Relaxation Fingerprints Resolve Material-Specific Perturbations of Conversion Lithiation in Spinel High-Entropy Oxide Anodes

**Working manuscript v18 — reaction-fingerprint story**

**Target under consideration:** Advanced Functional Materials

# Abstract

Conversion-type anodes undergo coupled electron transfer, structural reconstruction, phase evolution, and cation redistribution, making it difficult to determine which part of the reaction is altered by a material modification. Here, spinel high-entropy oxide anodes modified by ball milling and Mg incorporation are examined using galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) relaxation as a state-dependent reaction fingerprint. The first-lithiation response separates into four electrochemically distinct regimes based on the evolution of the characteristic relaxation time, (t_{63}), the relaxation voltage change, (Delta E_{mathrm{rest}}), and the relaxed voltage profile. Comparison with established conversion chemistry assigns these regimes to early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion-associated reconstructed-state evolution, and post-plateau deep lithiation. Ball milling redistributes accessible reaction extent from the late conversion-associated regime toward the earlier and post-plateau regimes while increasing relaxation persistence after conversion onset. Mg incorporation produces a distinct response, selectively suppressing the late conversion-associated reaction extent with only a weak change in its remaining relaxation timescale. The same trends persist across the Mg-free and Mg-containing comparisons. These results show that material modifications can alter where conversion proceeds independently of how persistently the resulting nonequilibrium states relax, and establish GITT relaxation fingerprints as a means of resolving material-specific perturbations in complex conversion reactions.

**Keywords:** high-entropy oxide; conversion anode; GITT; voltage relaxation; reaction fingerprint; ball milling; Mg incorporation

# 1. Introduction

Conversion-type electrode materials can store multiple Li ions through redox reactions that transform the parent oxide into chemically and structurally distinct products. Unlike topotactic insertion, conversion requires extensive rearrangement of the host structure and can involve concurrent electron transfer, M–O bond breaking, nucleation and growth of new phases, cation redistribution, and formation of metal/Li2O domains. The electrochemical response therefore reflects a sequence of coupled processes rather than a single transport or charge-transfer event. Material modifications that change particle structure, disorder, composition, or interfacial area can consequently alter not only the total reaction extent but also the stage of conversion at which charge is accessed and the manner in which the resulting nonequilibrium state relaxes. [1–5]

This complexity is particularly relevant to high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes, in which several redox-active cations participate within a common oxide structure. Operando and ex situ studies of spinel HEOs have shown that lithiation proceeds through progressive multication reduction, loss or reconstruction of the parent oxide structure, and formation of reduced or metallic species together with Li2O. [6–10] These studies establish that HEO conversion is multistage and reconstructive, but they do not by themselves provide a simple electrochemical coordinate for identifying which part of this reaction sequence is altered by a given material modification. Conventional galvanostatic voltage profiles and differential-capacity curves indicate where charge is transferred, but they provide limited information on the persistence of the nonequilibrium states generated at each stage.

GITT provides an additional observable because each current pulse is followed by a voltage relaxation at a defined lithiation state. The technique is commonly reduced to an apparent diffusion coefficient, although such estimates become difficult to interpret when diffusion is coupled to phase transformation, reaction kinetics, finite-size effects, and state-dependent thermodynamics. [11–15] The current-off trajectory itself contains complementary information. A relaxation magnitude describes how far the post-pulse state evolves during rest, whereas a characteristic relaxation time describes how long that evolution persists. Tracking these quantities over lithiation can therefore provide a state-dependent fingerprint without requiring the transient to be represented by a single diffusion coefficient or a single-exponential process. State-dependent voltage relaxation has been examined in insertion electrodes, including direct time-domain mapping of fast and slow relaxation times across state of charge, but its use to determine where material modifications perturb a multistage conversion reaction remains comparatively unexplored. [16]

Spinel (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4-based HEOs provide a suitable system for this comparison because ball milling and Mg incorporation perturb the material in different ways. Ball milling modifies particle size, disorder, and interfacial structure and increases the electrochemically accessible capacity of the present HEO. Related HEO studies have likewise associated milling or particle refinement with altered conversion reversibility and electrochemical utilization. [1,4,17] Mg incorporation instead lowers the accessible capacity and has been associated in related HEO systems with stabilization of oxide-derived structures by an electrochemically less active component. [3,18–20] Comparing these modifications in both Mg-free and Mg-containing materials therefore provides a 2 × 2 experimental design for distinguishing changes in reaction accessibility from changes in relaxation behavior.

Pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO) are examined using first-lithiation GITT relaxation. The relaxation response is characterized by (t_{63}), (Delta E_{mathrm{rest}}), and the relaxed voltage profile, and reproducible landmarks in these observables are used to identify four electrochemically distinct lithiation regimes. Established conversion-reaction studies are then used to assign broad physical meaning to the fingerprint-defined regions rather than to impose reaction boundaries a priori. Ball milling redistributes accessible reaction extent across the lithiation sequence and increases relaxation persistence after conversion begins, whereas Mg incorporation selectively suppresses access to the late conversion-associated regime. The combined comparison shows that where conversion proceeds and how persistently its nonequilibrium states relax are distinct material-dependent properties.

# 2. Results and Discussion

## 2.1. Structural and morphological perturbations introduced by ball milling and Mg incorporation

The four materials were designed to separate the effects of mechanical processing and Mg incorporation within the same spinel HEO family (Figures 1 and 2). All samples retain the principal spinel-type structural characteristics, whereas ball milling and Mg incorporation produce distinguishable changes in microstructure and composition. Ball milling decreases the characteristic particle/domain dimensions and increases the accessible surface area, providing a larger population of interfaces and locally perturbed environments. Mg incorporation introduces a compositional perturbation without the same milling-induced morphological change. These differences establish two experimentally distinct routes for modifying the subsequent conversion response.

## 2.2. Ball milling and Mg incorporation alter accessible lithiation in opposite ways

The conventional electrochemical response first establishes how the four material modifications change the extent of lithiation that is accessible within the applied voltage window (Figure 3). The first-cycle lithiation/delithiation capacities are 901.25/609.12 mAh g−1 for HEO, 1056.10/782.08 mAh g−1 for BM-HEO, 731.15/458.91 mAh g−1 for Mg-HEO, and 944.07/580.83 mAh g−1 for BM-Mg-HEO. Ball milling therefore increases the accessible capacity in both the Mg-free and Mg-containing materials, whereas Mg incorporation decreases it relative to the corresponding Mg-free composition.

The additional capacity introduced by ball milling is not confined to a single narrow voltage interval. For the HEO/BM-HEO pair, only a small fraction of the first-lithiation capacity increase occurs within the central portion of the broad conversion plateau, whereas appreciable differences also appear before and after this interval. The capacity change therefore reflects a redistribution of accessible reaction over the lithiation trajectory rather than a simple enlargement of one conversion peak. Mg incorporation produces the opposite overall capacity response and shifts the major cathodic feature toward lower voltage.

These voltage profiles establish how much reaction becomes accessible but do not determine which stages of the reconstructive conversion are responsible for the differences. GITT relaxation was therefore examined as a state-dependent fingerprint of the evolving reaction.

## 2.3. GITT relaxation fingerprints resolve four lithiation regimes

The first-lithiation GITT response of pristine HEO is not kinetically uniform but evolves through a reproducible sequence of relaxation features (Figure 4). Each GITT step consists of a 10 min galvanostatic pulse followed by a 60 min open-circuit rest. The characteristic time (t_{63}) is defined as the time required to complete 63.2% of the measured voltage relaxation between 3 s and 60 min after current interruption. This descriptor is extracted directly from the voltage trajectory and does not require a single-exponential fit. (Delta E_{mathrm{rest}} = |E_{mathrm{rest,60,min}}-E_{mathrm{rest,3,s}}|) describes the magnitude of the same current-off relaxation, while the 60 min rest-end voltage provides the corresponding relaxed electrochemical coordinate.

Three major landmarks organize the response into four regions. The first boundary, L1, marks the end of the initial rapid evolution of the relaxation response. L3 occurs near the pronounced central minimum in (t_{63}), accompanied by a minimum in (Delta E_{mathrm{rest}}) and a nearby maximum in the differential capacity derived from the relaxed voltage profile. L4 marks the termination of the broad relaxed-voltage plateau and the transition to the steeper low-voltage branch. These landmarks define R1–R4. Intermediate (t_{63}) extrema remain useful kinetic features but are not required as separate reaction boundaries.

The regions are assigned only after their electrochemical definition. R1 precedes the main conversion response and is therefore assigned broadly to early lithiation and pre-main-conversion processes. R2 spans the onset and progression of the principal conversion response. R3 extends from the conversion-centered state toward the end of the relaxed-voltage plateau and is assigned to continued conversion and evolution of reconstructed states. R4 begins after this plateau and represents post-main-conversion or deep-lithiation processes. These assignments are consistent with operando studies of related spinel HEOs showing progressive multication reduction, oxide reconstruction, and subsequent formation of reduced or metallic products, but no one-to-one assignment of individual cations to the present R1–R4 boundaries is implied. [6–10]

The four regions therefore provide an experimentally defined reaction coordinate on which the effects of material modification can be compared.

## 2.4. Ball milling redistributes the conversion response and prolongs post-onset relaxation

Ball milling preserves the broad sequence of relaxation regimes but changes both their relative extent and their characteristic timescales (Figure 5). Relative to HEO, BM-HEO shows an expansion of R1, R2, and R4 and a contraction of R3. The additional accessible reaction after milling therefore does not arise from a uniform increase in the original late-conversion response. Instead, reaction extent is redistributed over a broader portion of the lithiation sequence.

The timescale response changes independently of this capacity redistribution. (t_{63}) decreases in R1 but increases throughout R2–R4 after ball milling. The central HEO minimum evolves into a broader shallow trough in BM-HEO, and the subsequent relaxation remains slower over a larger fraction of the conversion and post-plateau response. Thus, once the principal conversion process is engaged, the ball-milled material returns toward its relaxed state more persistently even though more reaction becomes electrochemically accessible.

Combined with the milling-induced microstructural changes, this response is consistent with a broader population of accessible conversion environments. Increased disorder and interfacial diversity can allow lithiation to proceed through a wider range of local reconstructed states rather than concentrating reaction extent within the R3 response of the pristine material. The same heterogeneity can produce a more persistent recovery after current interruption. The data therefore support a specific role for ball milling: it broadens and redistributes the accessible conversion response while prolonging the relaxation of conversion-associated reconstructed states. This interpretation remains phenomenological until the final structural characterization is used to constrain the microscopic origin.

## 2.5. Mg incorporation selectively limits the late conversion-associated response

Mg incorporation produces a qualitatively different modification of the fingerprint (Figure 6). The largest change in accessible reaction extent occurs in R3, whereas the absolute extents of R1 and R4 change little and R2 is only moderately affected. The reduction in total lithiation capacity is therefore concentrated in the late conversion-associated regime rather than distributed uniformly over the complete lithiation sequence.

This loss of reaction extent is not accompanied by a comparable slowing of the remaining R3 response. The median (t_{63}) in R3 changes only weakly between HEO and Mg-HEO, while (Delta E_{mathrm{rest}}) decreases and the relaxed voltage response shifts toward lower voltage. Mg incorporation therefore primarily changes whether the system accesses the R3-type state, rather than strongly changing the relaxation timescale after that state has been reached.

This behavior is consistent with the established role of Mg as an electrochemically less active or oxide-stabilizing component in related HEO conversion electrodes. [3,18–20] In the present system, the consequence can be stated more specifically: Mg suppresses participation in the late conversion-associated reconstructed state. The data do not establish a unique Mg site or a single microscopic bond-level origin, and the interpretation is therefore restricted to this experimentally resolved change in conversion accessibility.

## 2.6. The 2 × 2 comparison separates reaction accessibility from relaxation persistence

The Mg-containing pair provides an independent test of the ball-milling response. Applying ball milling to Mg-HEO again expands R1, R2, and R4 while further reducing the fraction assigned to R3. The same modification also increases (t_{63}) over R2–R4. Ball milling therefore produces the same qualitative redistribution and relaxation-persistence trends whether Mg is absent or present.

Conversely, Mg incorporation suppresses R3 in both the unmilled and ball-milled comparisons. The repeated R3 loss shows that the dominant Mg effect is not specific to the pristine morphology. The two perturbations are not fully independent—particularly in the relaxation magnitude—but they act on distinguishable aspects of the conversion response.

Ball milling primarily changes where along the lithiation sequence reaction extent is accessed and how persistently the resulting reconstructed states relax, whereas Mg incorporation primarily changes whether the late conversion-associated state is accessed. Accessible reaction extent and relaxation persistence are therefore related but non-equivalent material properties of reconstructive conversion.

More generally, the result illustrates the value of treating GITT relaxation as a reaction-state fingerprint rather than compressing the transient into a single apparent transport parameter. The physical interpretation does not come from relaxation alone: the fingerprint defines the electrochemical regions, while the relaxed voltage response and established conversion chemistry provide the physical assignment.

# 3. Conclusion

GITT relaxation fingerprints were used to resolve how ball milling and Mg incorporation perturb first-lithiation conversion in spinel HEO anodes. The evolution of (t_{63}), (Delta E_{mathrm{rest}}), and the relaxed voltage profile separates lithiation into four electrochemically distinct regimes that can be associated broadly with early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion-associated reconstructed-state evolution, and post-plateau deep lithiation. Ball milling expands the early, progressive-conversion, and post-plateau regimes at the expense of the late conversion-associated R3 response and increases relaxation persistence after conversion onset. Mg incorporation produces a different response: the loss of accessible reaction is concentrated in R3, whereas the relaxation timescale of the remaining R3 response changes only weakly. The same trends are reproduced across the Mg-free and Mg-containing comparisons. These results show that material modifications can alter the accessibility of different conversion states separately from the persistence of their relaxation. GITT relaxation fingerprints therefore provide an electrochemical route for mapping where complex conversion reactions are perturbed, when interpreted together with relaxed-voltage behavior and independently established reaction chemistry.

## 4.2. Electrochemical measurements

Galvanostatic charge–discharge measurements were performed between 0.005 and 2.5 V. The principal low-rate comparison was conducted at 0.1 C, with rate-capability testing extending to 5 C followed by recovery at 0.1 C. The exact 1 C capacity basis and cycle sequence will be verified against the experimental record before submission.

GITT was performed at 100 mA g−1 using repeated 10 min current pulses followed by 60 min open-circuit relaxation. The Mg-free datasets were recorded at approximately 1 s time resolution and the Mg-containing datasets at approximately 3 s time resolution; a common 3 s post-interruption reference was therefore used for cross-sample comparison.

## 4.3. Relaxation fingerprint analysis

For each GITT step, (Delta E_{mathrm{rest}}) was defined as (|E_{mathrm{rest,60,min}}-E_{mathrm{rest,3,s}}|). The characteristic time (t_{63}) was defined as the first time required to complete 63.2% of the measured 3 s-to-60 min voltage relaxation. The descriptor is operational and model-light: it avoids a single-exponential fit but is not interpreted as a microscopic elementary-rate constant.

The 60 min rest-end voltage was used as the relaxed voltage coordinate for the state-resolved comparison. Reaction-region boundaries were identified from reproducible landmarks in (t_{63}) together with independent changes in (Delta E_{mathrm{rest}}) and the relaxed voltage profile. L1 denotes the early transition out of the initial response, L3 the central conversion-associated landmark, and L4 the end of the broad relaxed-voltage plateau. These boundaries define R1–R4. Intermediate extrema are treated as within-region kinetic landmarks rather than additional reaction boundaries.

Region-resolved capacity spans were calculated from the GITT pulse protocol for within-experiment comparison and are reported primarily as fractions of the total first-lithiation GITT capacity. Because the protocol-derived GITT capacity scale and the separately measured first-cycle galvanostatic capacity do not share an identical absolute normalization, the regional percentages are used for mechanistic partitioning and are not presented as exact replacements for the galvanostatic capacities in Figure 3.

## 4.4. Supporting analyses

Conventional GITT apparent-diffusivity calculations, alternate relaxation percentiles, boundary-sensitivity calculations, and earlier coarse-grained microkinetic tests are retained as supporting or internal robustness analyses. They are not used to define the main reaction-regime assignments or the material-specific physical interpretation in the present manuscript.
