# Working title

**Mg Incorporation and Ball Milling Regulate Phase-Transformation Polarization and Lithium Storage in Spinel High-Entropy Oxide Anodes**

Alternative title to reconsider after the structural section is finalized:

**Contrasting Roles of Mg Incorporation and Ball Milling in Spinel High-Entropy Oxide Anodes: Phase Evolution, Polarization, and Electrochemical Utilization**

---

# Abstract

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) provide a compositionally flexible platform for conversion-type lithium storage, but the effects of compositional modification and mechanical processing on their reaction pathway remain difficult to separate from conventional transport descriptors. Here, spinel (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4 HEO and its ball-milled and Mg-containing derivatives are compared to determine how Mg incorporation and ball milling alter electrochemical utilization and the polarization associated with lithiation-induced phase evolution. Ball milling increases the accessible low-rate capacity but does not produce uniformly lower polarization or faster relaxation. Current-interruption analysis of galvanostatic intermittent titration data instead shows a large early polarization during the initial lithiation stage and a broader, slower relaxation response after milling. A pronounced late-stage polarization feature in the pristine HEO is assigned primarily to the spinel-to-rock-salt/conversion transition based on its voltage range and phase-evolution pathways established for closely related spinel HEO anodes. Ball milling lowers the peak amplitude of this feature while distributing it over a wider capacity interval. Mg incorporation produces a different response: the transition-associated polarization and accessible capacity are both strongly reduced, whereas the characteristic relaxation times do not become shorter. The reduced polarization therefore cannot be explained simply by accelerated Li transport and is instead attributed predominantly to suppression of the late-stage conversion-type phase transformation. These results show that Mg incorporation and ball milling regulate lithium storage through distinct changes in phase-transformation extent, polarization, and relaxation rather than through a single monotonic change in apparent diffusivity.

# 1. Introduction

High-entropy oxides (HEOs) extend the high-entropy concept from metallic alloys to multication ceramic compounds, where configurational disorder can stabilize compositionally complex single-phase structures and create properties that are difficult to obtain from conventional binary or ternary oxides.[1] Since the initial demonstration of entropy-stabilized oxides, HEOs have been investigated in rock-salt, spinel, perovskite, fluorite, and related structures for ionic conduction, catalysis, and electrochemical energy storage.[1–3] Their appeal as battery electrodes arises from the simultaneous presence of multiple redox-active cations, broad compositional tunability, and the possibility that configurational disorder can alter phase stability and structural evolution during repeated electrochemical reaction.[2]

Spinel (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4 is one of the most widely studied transition-metal HEO anodes for lithium-ion batteries. Single-phase spinel materials with this composition have shown high first-cycle capacity and stable cycling when synthesized through solid-state reaction or oxidation-based routes.[4,5] The lithium-storage reaction, however, is not a simple solid-solution insertion process. Structural studies on spinel HEOs and related multication oxides have shown progressive loss or reorganization of the parent spinel structure during lithiation, including spinel-to-rock-salt-like transformations, amorphization, reduction of transition-metal species, and conversion-type reactions.[4–6] Recent in situ and ex situ studies on (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4 further resolve a spinel → mixed spinel/rock-salt → rock-salt pathway during lithiation.[9] These transformations provide substantial charge storage but also introduce nucleation, phase-boundary propagation, structural rearrangement, and interfacial reconstruction. The associated electrochemical response therefore reflects more than Li transport within an unchanged host lattice.

Particle size, mechanical processing, and the presence of electrochemically inactive or weakly active cations can strongly modify this reaction pathway. Ball milling increases surface area and reduces characteristic particle dimensions, but it also introduces disorder, fresh interfaces, and mechanically perturbed local structures. In (FeCoNiCrMn)3O4 HEOs, long-term particle fragmentation has been linked to increased conversion reversibility and additional interfacial capacity, while excessive interfacial growth eventually contributes to degradation.[8] Related Mg-containing spinel HEOs show that Mg can act as a comparatively redox-inactive structural component during conversion and that high-energy milling itself can alter the balance between spinel and rock-salt-like structures.[6,7] Consequently, an increase or decrease in capacity after ball milling or Mg incorporation cannot be interpreted reliably from particle size or nominal diffusion length alone. The central question is how these synthesis variables change the extent and distribution of the electrochemical phase transformation that produces the stored charge.

Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) is commonly used to characterize the kinetics of HEO electrodes, typically by reducing each pulse to an apparent Li-ion diffusion coefficient.[5] This treatment is useful as a comparative descriptor when the underlying assumptions are satisfied, but conventional GITT analysis assumes a sufficiently simple diffusion geometry, an appropriate short-time regime, and a relation between transient voltage and composition that can become problematic during large structural changes or multiphase reactions.[10] Current interruption provides additional information that does not require the entire response to be represented by one diffusion coefficient. At short times, the voltage during a current pause can be separated into a time-independent intercept and a component that is linear with the square root of time under the semi-infinite diffusion approximation.[11] At longer times, the magnitude and characteristic time of the relaxation can be analyzed directly. Phase-transforming electrodes are particularly relevant in this context because nucleation and phase-boundary processes can themselves contribute appreciably to electrochemical overpotential.[12]

This study examines four related spinel HEO electrodes—pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-containing HEO (BM-Mg-HEO)—to separate the effects of mechanical processing and Mg incorporation. Structural and surface characterization establishes how the two synthesis variables modify the HEO before electrochemical testing, while galvanostatic cycling determines their effects on accessible capacity, rate response, and cycling stability. The current-off portions of 10 min GITT pulses followed by 60 min relaxation are then analyzed directly to resolve fast polarization, the finite-window relaxation amplitude, and model-free characteristic relaxation times. Ball milling increases low-rate electrochemical utilization while broadening the late-stage transformation-associated polarization and slowing the residual relaxation. Mg incorporation instead suppresses both the pronounced late-stage polarization feature and the associated conversion capacity without shortening the relaxation time. The combined response supports distinct mechanistic roles for the two synthesis variables: ball milling distributes the conversion transition over a broader capacity interval, whereas Mg predominantly suppresses its extent. This distinction provides a synthesis–electrochemistry connection that is not captured by a single apparent GITT diffusivity.

## 2.1. Synthesis of HEO and BM-HEO

Use the Yoo-group draft as the authoritative source:

- NiCl2·6H2O, FeCl3·6H2O, CoCl2·6H2O, MnCl2·4H2O, and CrCl3·6H2O as metal precursors.
- 1.4 mmol of each metal chloride in 20 mL deionized water.
- 14 mmol NaOH and 7 mmol Na2CO3·H2O in 10 mL deionized water as alkaline solution.
- slow addition under 800 rpm stirring.
- precipitation for 2 h at room temperature.
- centrifugation and three washing steps.
- drying overnight at 70 °C.
- calcination at 900 °C for 2 h with 5 °C min−1 ramp to convert HEH to HEO.
- ball milling of 2 g HEO for 12 h at 500 rpm in an 80 mL stainless-steel jar with 5 mm ZrO2 balls and powder:ball mass ratio of 1:10.

## 2.5. GITT current-interruption and relaxation analysis

Planned final Methods content:

1. use cumulative specific capacity as the state coordinate;
2. analyze current-off/rest rather than current-on polarization as the principal mechanistic observable;
3. obtain an apparent instantaneous current-off jump by fitting the early rest response as E(t) = a + b sqrt(t) and extrapolating to t → 0;
4. define the finite-window relaxation amplitude from the early current-off reference to the 60 min endpoint;
5. define t50, t63, and t90 as the times required to reach 50%, 63.2%, and 90% of the observed 60 min relaxation amplitude;
6. use t63 as the principal main-text characteristic-time descriptor while retaining t50/t90 and early/terminal slopes in the Supporting Information;
7. quantify the late-stage transition-associated excess polarization using peak amplitude, capacity width, and capacity-weighted excess polarization area after a declared background subtraction.

The integrated excess area should not be called a rigorous dissipated energy because the relaxation amplitude is sampled at discrete GITT states rather than measured as the continuous operating overpotential.

## 3.4. Ball milling broadens the phase-transformation polarization

Working assignment:

The pronounced late-stage relaxation hump in HEO is assigned primarily to the spinel-to-rock-salt/conversion phase transformation based on the observed voltage range and established phase evolution in closely related spinel HEO anodes.

Exploratory excess-feature metrics:

| Sample | Excess peak | FWHM-like capacity width | Normalized excess area | Capacity-weighted excess area |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HEO | ~71 mV | ~350 mAh g−1 | ~22.3 mV | ~24.1 mWh g−1-equivalent |
| BM-HEO | ~46 mV | ~417 mAh g−1 | ~15.0 mV | ~19.0 |
| Mg-HEO | ~15.9 mV | ~233 mAh g−1 | ~4.95 mV | ~4.0 |
| BM-Mg-HEO | ~21.1 mV | ~367 mAh g−1 | ~7.58 mV | ~7.1 |

Working interpretation:

Ball milling lowers the local maximum of the transformation-associated polarization while distributing the response over a wider capacity interval. The larger accessible low-rate capacity can therefore coexist with slower residual relaxation. At higher rates, the accumulated polarization can drive the electrode to cutoff before the additional low-rate capacity is fully accessed.

## 3.5. Mg suppresses the late-stage conversion transition rather than simply accelerating transport

Preferred mechanistic sequence:

Mg incorporation → stabilization of the oxide/spinel-derived structure → reduced extent of late-stage spinel-to-rock-salt/conversion transformation → smaller transformation-associated polarization + lower accessible conversion capacity.

Evidence to present in this order:

1. late-stage polarization hump strongly decreases;
2. capacity decreases;
3. fast current-off polarization also decreases;
4. t63/t90 do not become shorter and are often longer;
5. therefore smaller polarization is not explained by uniformly faster relaxation/Li transport.

Alternative explanations should be discussed only after the preferred interpretation:

- faster Li diffusion alone is weakened by the longer, not shorter, relaxation times;
- lower ohmic/electronic resistance can explain the fast jump but not the selective suppression of the late-stage hump and capacity;
- reduced surface accessibility alone does not naturally explain the state-specific suppression, particularly because measured surface-accessibility indicators do not simply track capacity;
- SEI differences can contribute to the early first-cycle response but are less consistent with the pronounced low-voltage state dependence of the late-stage feature.

## 3.6. Synthesis–electrochemistry map

Final discussion should integrate rather than repeat the preceding sections:

- pristine HEO: concentrated conversion transition and pronounced late-stage polarization;
- BM-HEO: broader transition interval, lower local peak polarization, increased low-rate utilization, slower residual relaxation, weaker rate retention;
- Mg-HEO: suppressed transition extent, reduced polarization and capacity, but not faster relaxation;
- BM-Mg-HEO: increased slow polarization after milling without restoration of the strong Mg-free transition feature.

The paper should end this section with the conclusion that accessible capacity, polarization magnitude, and relaxation time are distinct electrochemical observables that respond differently to composition and processing.
