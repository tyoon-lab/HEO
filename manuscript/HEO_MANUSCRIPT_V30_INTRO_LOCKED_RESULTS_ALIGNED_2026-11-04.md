# Reaction-Region-Dependent Lithiation Response to Ball Milling and Mg Incorporation in Spinel High-Entropy Oxide Anodes

PI review manuscript | 04 November 2026 | locked Introduction and Introduction-aligned Results; preliminary XPS excluded pending verification.

Abstract below preserved from earlier PI working draft; final Abstract to be drafted after full-manuscript review.

## Abstract (working text — not finalized)

Ball milling and Mg incorporation produce opposing changes in the lithiation capacity of spinel high-entropy oxide (HEO) anodes. These macroscopic changes, however, provide limited insight into how the electrochemical response is altered as lithiation proceeds. Analysis of galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) voltage and post-pulse relaxation distinguishes four electrochemically distinct regimes: early lithiation, conversion onset and progression, late conversion, and post-plateau deep lithiation. Ball milling produces its largest regional GITT charge increase during conversion onset and progression, associated with oxide destabilization and structural reconstruction. In contrast, Mg incorporation produces its largest regional GITT charge decrease during late conversion, associated with continued reduction and further development of metal/Li₂O-derived products. Increased BET surface area after milling is consistent with improved accessibility of reactive oxide regions, whereas the pronounced Mg-associated late-conversion loss suggests restricted access to further reduction, potentially involving persistent Mg-containing oxide environments. These findings demonstrate that opposing changes in overall capacity arise from different regions of the lithiation pathway, providing a basis for understanding how materials processing and composition influence conversion-type electrodes.

Keywords: spinel high-entropy oxide; conversion anode; ball milling; magnesium incorporation; GITT relaxation; reaction-region capacity

## 1. Introduction

Spinel high-entropy oxides (HEOs) combine multiple transition-metal cations within a common oxide lattice and have attracted interest as high-capacity conversion-type anodes. Although their lithium storage can be summarized by an overall oxide-to-metal/Li₂O conversion reaction, state-resolved studies reveal a more intricate evolution during lithiation. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy has shown that Fe and Mn in spinel HEOs pass through intermediate oxide states during reduction to their metallic forms, whereas Ni and Cu exhibit more direct conversion pathways [7]. Ex situ X-ray absorption and diffraction studies have also identified Li uptake, cation migration, and spinel-to-rock-salt transformations during lithiation [23]. Related in situ observations in Fe₃O₄ reveal the coexistence of Li-containing intermediate oxide and metal/Li₂O conversion products [22]. These findings indicate that reduction and structural transformation processes overlap during lithiation, with their relative prominence changing as the reaction progresses.

This progression raises an important question when HEO materials are modified: where along lithiation does the resulting capacity change arise? Particle-size control, cation substitution, and Li₂O incorporation have been shown to alter spinel HEO capacity and conversion behavior [9,20,24]. These studies have examined intermediate oxide conversion, ion transport, and voltage features associated with insertion and conversion. The charge gained or lost over different portions of lithiation, however, remains less well quantified. A modification may influence charge storage over the full lithiation range or preferentially affect particular portions, potentially with changes of opposite sign. Locating and quantifying these contributions would clarify how processing and composition alter the conversion response and support targeted materials design.

Changes in the dominant reaction processes during lithiation can alter both the thermodynamic and kinetic behavior of the electrode. Low-rate voltage profiles contain information about reaction thermodynamics, but broad conversion plateaus and overlapping reactions often produce similar voltages over substantial charge intervals and poorly separated differential-capacity features [7,23]. Kinetic responses may provide additional contrast where these voltage features overlap. Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) probes these responses by recording voltage relaxation after successive current pulses. The relaxation amplitude and characteristic relaxation time describe the magnitude and timescale of voltage recovery, respectively, providing complementary information on the evolving electrochemical response. The voltage at the end of each rest period additionally provides a quasi-open-circuit voltage (QOCV), offering a quasi-thermodynamic reference for interpreting the relaxation behavior. Changes in the relaxation descriptors, considered alongside the QOCV profile, provide a basis for defining electrochemical regions and comparing their charge contributions across modified HEOs.

Ball milling and Mg incorporation were selected to examine how processing and cation composition affect the lithiation response of spinel HEOs. Previous studies have shown that particle refinement and Mg substitution can influence their electrochemical behavior [4,27]. First-lithiation GITT was applied to pristine HEO, ball-milled HEO (BM-HEO), Mg-containing HEO (Mg-HEO), and ball-milled Mg-HEO (BM-Mg-HEO), allowing the effects of each modification to be compared with and without the other. Across these matched comparisons, ball milling increases first-lithiation capacity, whereas Mg incorporation decreases it. Region-resolved GITT charge shows that ball milling produces its largest positive increment in R2, associated with conversion onset and progression, although its R3 contribution decreases. Mg incorporation, in contrast, produces its largest negative increment in R3, associated with later conversion. These differences show how total capacity changes arise from unequal regional contributions and identify the parts of lithiation most responsive to processing and composition.

## 2. Results and Discussion

### 2.1. Structural and surface changes induced by ball milling and Mg incorporation

The four related HEO materials provide two matched comparisons for ball milling and two for Mg incorporation (Figure 1). Diffraction and electron microscopy indicate a spinel-type parent material and distinguish the particle and aggregate morphologies. Elemental mapping detects the expected transition-metal constituents and a Mg signal in the nominally Mg-containing samples. These structural and compositional observations establish the material series used to compare processing and composition, while detailed phase indexing and Mg lattice-site assignment require agreement between the final diffraction and microscopy analyses.

The most prominent measured physical effect of milling is an increase in gas-accessible surface area (Figure 2). The reported BET area rises from 3.94 to 18.159 m² g⁻¹ in the Mg-free pair and from 6.49 to 16.64 m² g⁻¹ in the Mg-containing pair. These increases establish that mechanical processing modifies the exposed powder surface and suggest a possible increase in the accessibility of reactive oxide domains during lithiation. Mg incorporation also raises the BET area of the unmilled sample, from 3.94 to 6.49 m² g⁻¹, while its first-lithiation capacity decreases. The Mg-associated capacity loss therefore cannot be attributed simply to a lower measured BET area; the gas-accessible area does not directly measure the electrochemically active area.

The four-material comparison thus separates changes in powder surface characteristics associated with processing from changes in cation composition. Milling may modify the interfaces and oxide domains that participate in conversion, whereas introducing Mg changes the inventory and local environment of transition-metal cations. The resulting first-lithiation responses provide a means of identifying which electrochemical portions of conversion are most sensitive to each modification.

### 2.2. Electrochemical performance establishes opposing modification effects

The voltage profiles at 0.1 C display the pronounced influence of processing and composition on lithium storage (Figure 3a–d). The first-lithiation/delithiation capacities are 901.25/609.12 mAh g⁻¹ for HEO, 1056.10/782.08 mAh g⁻¹ for BM-HEO, 731.15/458.91 mAh g⁻¹ for Mg-HEO, and 944.07/580.83 mAh g⁻¹ for BM-Mg-HEO. Ball milling increases the first-lithiation capacity in either compositional background, whereas introducing Mg lowers it in both unmilled and milled materials. The same crossed comparison therefore distinguishes a processing-related capacity gain from a composition-related loss without relying on a single reference material.

Cycling stability and rate capability provide complementary electrochemical context for these contrasting first-lithiation responses (Figure 3e,f). The cycling comparison evaluates whether changes in the initial capacity are sustained as the electrodes undergo repeated conversion and reconversion. The rate sequence tests the accessibility of stored charge under increasing current and its recovery upon returning to 0.1 C. These measurements establish the practical performance context, while the first-lithiation voltage data provide the common starting point for identifying the origins of the initial capacity differences.

The opposing total-capacity trends do not establish which portions of the conversion sequence account for the changes. Previous operando and state-resolved studies reveal overlapping cation reduction, oxide reconstruction, and metal-forming conversion during lithiation [7–9,22,23]. The present analysis therefore focuses on locating the signed charge differences within the observed electrochemical response rather than assigning each capacity change to an isolated chemical reaction.

### 2.3. Electrochemical signatures of the progressive conversion reaction

The GITT response evolves as the oxide accommodates lithium (Figure 4). Each first-lithiation step comprises a 600 s constant-current pulse followed by 3600 s of open-circuit relaxation. Two operational descriptors characterize voltage recovery: the relaxation amplitude, ΔErest, measured between 3 s and 60 min after interruption, and the characteristic relaxation time, t63, required to complete 63.2% of that finite-window recovery. The voltage at the end of each rest period provides a protocol-defined quasi-open-circuit voltage (QOCV) profile that complements these relaxation descriptors. The finite rest time is treated consistently across the four materials rather than assumed to establish full equilibrium.

The pristine HEO exhibits an initial pronounced decay in relaxation amplitude and time, a broad lower-potential region with changing relaxation behavior, and a final transition to a steeper deep-lithiation branch. Three landmarks, L1, L3, and L4, define four electrochemical regions (R1–R4). L1 marks the transition out of the early lithiation response; L3 identifies a characteristic change in the central amplitude and time trends; and L4 marks termination of the broad rest-end-voltage plateau. The relevant criterion is the coherent evolution of the observed trends, not exact coincidence of their numerical extrema. Similar sequences remain recognizable across BM-HEO, Mg-HEO and BM-Mg-HEO, although the landmark locations and detailed relaxation profiles shift with modification (Figure 4b).

The chemical interpretation draws on established conversion studies while retaining the experimentally defined nature of the four regions. Operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy shows that different cations in spinel HEOs follow partly overlapping reduction pathways [7]. Other state-resolved investigations identify Li uptake, cation redistribution, and spinel-to-rock-salt intermediate transformation accompanying metal-forming conversion [9,23]. In conventional spinel oxides, lithiated intermediate domains and metal/Li₂O conversion products may coexist [22]. These observations support the following region associations: R1, early Li uptake and oxide evolution; R2, conversion onset and progression with substantial oxide destabilization and reconstruction; R3, later conversion with continuing reduction and further development of metal/Li₂O-derived products and interfaces; and R4, post-plateau deep lithiation, which may also include interfacial storage. The regions describe shifts in the dominant electrochemical response of overlapping processes, not discrete or uniquely identified phase boundaries.

The relaxation response adds contrast where different reaction contributions produce overlapping voltage features. ΔErest measures the magnitude of finite-window voltage recovery, while t63 describes its characteristic progression. Both depend on the evolving voltage–composition relation and the redistribution and interfacial equilibration following each pulse; a change in either descriptor does not uniquely identify a chemical phase or a single elementary rate. Distinct changes in these observables, evaluated with the QOCV profile, provide electrochemical landmarks for comparing the charge delivered in different portions of lithiation.

### 2.4. Localization of modification-induced charge changes across the four regions

The four-region partition reveals that the two modifications act on different portions of the lithiation response (Figure 5; Tables S1 and S2). Each region is quantified using the nominal charge delivered by the corresponding complete GITT pulses. In pristine HEO, the largest contribution is R3 (516.7 mAh g⁻¹), followed by R2 (316.7 mAh g⁻¹). After ball milling, the R2 contribution rises to 483.3 mAh g⁻¹ while R3 decreases to 366.7 mAh g⁻¹. Thus, milling does more than increase the total accessible charge: it redistributes charge toward the earlier conversion-onset/progression region.

The same pattern holds in the Mg-containing pair. Relative to Mg-HEO, BM-Mg-HEO gains 133.3 mAh g⁻¹ in R2 while losing 166.7 mAh g⁻¹ in R3. The gain in R2 is the largest positive regional change caused by milling in both compositional backgrounds (+166.7 and +133.3 mAh g⁻¹). Positive contributions from R1 and R4 partly offset the simultaneous R3 decrease. The net milling-induced charge gain is therefore a sum of competing changes, not the exclusive growth of one conversion process.

Mg incorporation produces a different, more concentrated signature. The nominal R3 charge decreases from 516.7 to 250.0 mAh g⁻¹ upon introducing Mg into pristine HEO, and from 366.7 to 83.3 mAh g⁻¹ upon introducing Mg into BM-HEO. The corresponding R3 losses (−266.7 and −283.3 mAh g⁻¹) are by far the largest negative regional changes in the respective pairs. Changes in R1 and R4 are negligible in the accepted first-lithiation partition, whereas R2 is reduced much less strongly. Mg therefore acts predominantly on the later portion of the progressive conversion response.

A quantitative comparison of the signed changes is essential because percentage contributions alone can obscure the redistribution when total charge changes. The GITT values reported here are protocol-derived nominal charges for the fixed 100 mA g⁻¹ and 600 s pulses; they are not substituted for the independent galvanostatic capacities reported in Figure 3. Nevertheless, both measurements establish the same direction of the overall BM and Mg effects, and the region-resolved GITT analysis identifies their distinct electrochemical locations.

### 2.5. Physical origins of the contrasting material responses

Ball milling produces its largest positive GITT-charge increment in R2, associated with conversion onset and progression. The substantial BET-area increase in both milled compositions provides a plausible physical correlate: exposing additional oxide surface may improve the accessibility of domains that enter overlapping reduction and structural-reorganization processes. Prior work also demonstrates that particle-size control can influence the completeness of spinel HEO conversion [9]. Nevertheless, BET area alone cannot establish the participating interfacial area during lithiation or isolate the effects of particle size, disorder, and electrical connectivity. The simultaneous decrease in R3 charge indicates that milling redistributes charge among electrochemical regions rather than uniformly accelerating or enhancing every part of conversion.

The relaxation times provide another distinction. Milling shortens t63 in the early R1 region but prolongs characteristic recovery across much of R2–R4. The enlarged R2 charge contribution therefore coexists with more persistent voltage relaxation after the current is interrupted. The measured t63 characterizes post-pulse voltage recovery, not the rate of a unique elementary conversion reaction during the pulse. These observations distinguish the charge accessible during lithiation from the timescale of subsequent equilibration.

Mg incorporation produces a different regional response. The pronounced R3 charge decrease shows that less charge is accessed during later conversion within the applied voltage window, despite the larger measured BET area of Mg-HEO relative to pristine HEO. One plausible explanation is that Mg-containing oxide environments persist during reduction and alter the accessibility or energetics of subsequent conversion. Studies of compositionally related HEOs support a structural role for Mg and the persistence of oxide-derived components after lithiation [10,17–21]. The present electrochemical data, however, do not determine Mg site occupancy or directly identify a Mg-rich residual phase. Dilution or selective replacement of reducible transition-metal centers, as well as changes in polarization or in the potential at which further conversion is accessed, may contribute concurrently. The strongest direct finding is the concentration of the Mg-associated charge decrease in R3, rather than a uniform decrease throughout lithiation.

The characteristic relaxation time of the remaining R3 response changes much less than its charge contribution after Mg incorporation. This difference indicates that the amount of charge accessed during later conversion and the timescale of its subsequent voltage recovery respond differently to compositional modification. A relatively small change in post-pulse t63 does not establish that the conversion kinetics during current flow are unchanged. The paired R2/R3 contrasts are consistent with a composition-sensitive modification of later conversion accessibility, while the specific chemical contribution remains to be resolved by state-sensitive structural measurements.

### 2.6. Implications for electrochemical-region-guided materials design

The matched comparisons illustrate how electrochemical-region-resolved analysis can connect known conversion chemistry to a materials-design question. Milling increases the nominal charge delivered in R2 while reducing its R3 contribution, whereas Mg incorporation produces a substantially larger charge loss in R3 than elsewhere. Opposite changes in total capacity thus arise from distinct patterns of regional gains and losses. The analysis identifies where material modifications affect the lithiation response, providing a basis for investigating whether changes originate from accessible oxide domains, reducible cation populations, or shifts in conversion energetics.

The results also distinguish measured BET area, post-interruption relaxation timescale, and first-lithiation capacity. A higher gas-accessible surface area does not necessarily correspond to a greater total lithiation capacity, and a larger regional charge can coexist with slower voltage recovery. Long-term cycling stability is a separate performance characteristic and should be interpreted independently of initial lithiation capacity. Material optimization can therefore be guided by the desired balance of regional charge contributions while considering the potential window and subsequent cycling behavior.

The present four-material comparison establishes a common electrochemical basis for locating processing- and composition-induced differences along an overlapping conversion pathway. Chemical assignments to R1–R4 remain informed by independent HEO and spinel-oxide studies rather than by exclusive phase detection. These region-resolved observations can direct future characterization and processing choices toward the portions of lithiation that contribute most strongly to capacity gain or loss.

## 3. Conclusion

Spinel HEO lithiation comprises electrochemically distinguishable regions associated with changing contributions from Li uptake, oxide reconstruction, and conversion-product development. GITT relaxation amplitude and characteristic relaxation time, interpreted with the finite-rest QOCV profile, define four regions along the first-lithiation pathway. In the matched four-material comparisons, ball milling yields its largest positive nominal GITT-charge increment during conversion onset and progression (R2) while decreasing the subsequent R3 contribution. Mg incorporation instead produces its largest charge loss during later conversion (R3). These region-specific trends are consistent across both relevant matched comparisons. The increase in BET area after milling suggests a possible contribution from greater exposed oxide surface, whereas the Mg-associated R3 loss points toward a composition-sensitive change in the charge accessed during later conversion. The mechanisms may involve persistent oxide environments, changes in reducible-cation content, and shifts in conversion accessibility; direct state-sensitive measurements are needed to distinguish them. Region-resolved charge comparisons thus locate how processing and composition alter the lithiation response of multication conversion electrodes.

### 4.4. GITT relaxation and regional-charge analysis

The finite-window relaxation amplitude was defined for each GITT step as ΔErest = |Erest(3600 s) − Erest(3 s)|. The characteristic recovery time, t63, was defined as the first elapsed time required to complete 63.2% of the signed voltage change between the same two endpoints. These operational descriptors do not assume that the relaxation is single exponential. The potential at the end of the 3600 s rest was taken as a reproducible rest-end voltage coordinate rather than a strictly equilibrated open-circuit potential.

Electrochemical boundaries were identified from characteristic changes in the trends of ΔErest and t63, supported by the rested-voltage profile. L1 delineates the departure from early lithiation, L3 denotes the central change in conversion-associated relaxation behavior, and L4 follows the termination of the broad rest-end-voltage plateau. The resulting intervals define R1 (start–L1), R2 (L1–L3), R3 (L3–L4) and R4 (L4–end). Descriptor extrema were not required to occur at the same pulse, because the selected boundaries identify changes in the dominant response rather than sharp microscopic phase boundaries. The perturbation sensitivity to one-pulse shifts was assessed for the accepted region partition (Table S3).

Nominal regional GITT charge was determined from the applied mass-normalized current multiplied by the total duration of complete lithiation pulses assigned to each region. One 600 s pulse at 100 mA g⁻¹ contributes 16.6667 mAh g⁻¹. The four datasets contain 65, 76, 48 and 56 complete pulses for HEO, BM-HEO, Mg-HEO and BM-Mg-HEO, respectively. Signed differences between matched material pairs were calculated from these regional charges (Tables S1–S2). These nominal GITT values describe the dedicated titration protocol and are not equated to the capacities measured under conventional galvanostatic cycling.
