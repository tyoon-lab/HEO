Region-Resolved Charge Redistribution and Supporting GITT Relaxation Signatures in Spinel High-Entropy Oxide Anodes

The four original three-cycle GITT workbooks were processed at pulse resolution using a common 3 s-to-rest-end relaxation definition. Changes in the characteristic relaxation time t₆₃ identify the electrochemical-region landmarks, while ΔErest and QOCV describe the magnitude and voltage dependence of the response. Region-resolved nominal pulse charge is used to compare the effects of milling and Mg incorporation.

## S1. Material characterization

The N₂ BET surface areas are 3.94 (HEO), 18.159 (BM-HEO), 6.49 (Mg-HEO) and 16.64 m² g⁻¹ (BM-Mg-HEO). Milling increases the measured area in both composition pairs. The higher BET area of Mg-HEO relative to HEO contrasts with its lower lithiation capacity and reduced late-region GITT charge. Mg effects therefore extend beyond the geometric surface-area change. The Cr 2p spectra and originally fitted envelopes are compared in Figure 2; the high-binding-energy feature near 579 eV is characterized as an observed spectral component pending independent energy calibration and oxidation-state verification.

## S2. GITT measurement definitions and source provenance

GITT was conducted at a nominal 100 mA g⁻¹ with 600 s current pulses and 3600 s open-circuit rest periods. Mg-free data were recorded at approximately 1 s intervals and Mg-containing datasets at approximately 3 s intervals. For cycles C1–C3, current sign defined lithiation (negative) and delithiation (positive). The entire current-on duration, including the terminal partial pulse, was used for whole-half-cycle nominal charge. Complete pulse analysis required a 600 s pulse and a subsequent 3600 s rest. For each complete pulse, E(3 s) after current interruption serves as the initial voltage and the last recorded rest potential before the next pulse defines QOCV. The signed E(3 s)-to-E(3600 s) recovery defines t₆₃ as the elapsed time from the 3 s reference to the first crossing of 1−exp(−1) of that finite-window voltage change; ΔErest is its absolute magnitude. Crossing times were linearly interpolated between adjacent measured time stamps. QOCV is a finite-rest potential, not an independently verified equilibrium OCV. The characteristic time is operational, rather than a fitted single-exponential rate constant. Separate model-sensitivity analyses using 3–3500 s and 10–3500 s definitions are explicitly identified below.

All boundaries are selected from the t₆₃ sequence. L1 marks the early post-transient minimum preceding renewed growth, L3 marks the central minimum followed by the next rise, and L4 marks the late kinetic change. HEO and BM-HEO exhibit a late maximum and subsequent t₆₃ downturn; Mg-HEO and BM-Mg-HEO exhibit a late change in slope followed by renewed t₆₃ increase. The cumulative after-pulse boundaries are HEO 6/25/55, BM-HEO 9/38/60, Mg-HEO 7/24/42 and BM-Mg-HEO 9/35/46. In the Mg-containing files, t₆₃ at pulse 42 versus 43 changes by −0.021 min (Mg-HEO), and pulse 46 versus 47 by −0.029 min (BM-Mg-HEO), amounts below their nominal 3 s sampling interval. The slope change assignment is therefore operational and the ±1-pulse sensitivity in Table S3 is important. QOCV and ΔErest remain supporting observables and are excluded from the boundary criterion.

## S5. Interpretation and boundary sensitivity

Electrochemical-region charge accounting localizes material-dependent changes across first lithiation. The measured t₆₃ and ΔErest values describe the 3 s-to-60 min voltage recovery and contain coupled transport, interfacial and structural contributions. Their relation to individual reaction rates or solid-state diffusivity depends on the relevant model and state. The late Mg-associated t₆₃ slope change describes a change in the measured relaxation trajectory, while microscopic assignment of persistent Mg-rich phases remains a materials hypothesis supported by related literature.

Four original GITT workbooks contain 65, 76, 48 and 56 complete pulses. Mg-HEO t₆₃ changes by −0.021 min between pulses 42 and 43, and BM-Mg-HEO by −0.029 min between 46 and 47. These gradual late changes mark operational endpoints; Table S3 provides ±1-pulse sensitivity.

## S6. Time-resolved analysis of the R3 relaxation-amplitude hump

The same original four GITT measurement workbooks used for Figure 4 were analyzed pulse by pulse over each accepted R3 interval: HEO pulses 26–55, BM-HEO 39–60, Mg-HEO 25–42, and BM-Mg-HEO 36–46. Rest-voltage recovery was partitioned at 3 s, 60 s, 600 s, and the approximately 3600 s rest endpoint following current interruption. Linear interpolation between instrument time stamps provides the boundary voltages, and the three incremental changes sum exactly to ΔErest for each pulse. The 3 s initial reference retains the agreed voltage descriptor definition, excluding the initial interruption jump. This time-window decomposition describes voltage-recovery contributions rather than unique relaxation modes or distinct redox reactions.

The pristine-HEO R3 amplitude rises by 44.2 mV from pulse 26 to 50, composed of −9.8 mV (3–60 s), +16.5 mV (1–10 min), and +37.5 mV (10–60 min). Late recovery thus accounts for most of the observed positive hump increment, while the earliest contribution declines. Mg-HEO has a smaller within-R3 maximum (+8.6 mV from onset). BM-HEO and BM-Mg-HEO show decreasing total ΔErest within R3, even though the late recovery fraction increases in all four samples. The total-amplitude maximum and the trend toward relatively slower normalized relaxation therefore encode related but distinct changes in the voltage response (Figures S4–S6).

## S7. Cross-material evidence for delayed voltage recovery during conversion and phase transformation

The R3 hump in Figure 4 is a local maximum of the finite-window rest-voltage recovery amplitude across successive lithiation states. This quantity differs from a nonmonotonic voltage trace within one rest, from a voltage bump during subsequent cycling, and from persistent charge–discharge hysteresis after relaxation. The most direct comparisons are state-dependent GITT relaxation measurements in FeF₃, where the spatial distribution of partially converted domains affects voltage recovery and reaction pathway changes can affect persistent hysteresis independently of transient recovery (Table S6).

Complementary diffraction studies show Li redistribution between graphite and SiO during open-circuit rest and structural rearrangement in graphite and LiFePO₄ after current interruption. Silicon nanoparticle studies additionally demonstrate mechanical/interphase stress relaxation as an alternative source of slow voltage evolution. These precedents motivate a hypothesis of delayed re-equilibration among heterogeneous conversion states in HEO R3, while local QOCV response and interfacial contributions remain additional possibilities. No individual intermediate or microscopic process is assigned solely from the amplitude hump.

## S9. Conditional C2-to-C3 charge-distribution comparison

A restricted empirical comparison quantified whether the observed delithiation distribution can be described by only an accessible-charge scaling and a common potential offset. Let u(V) denote the interpolated cumulative charge assigned to fully rested pulses, normalized either by the whole-half-cycle charge (the original analysis) or by the charge of fully rested pulses alone (the Figure 6 normalization). The shift-only null is u_treat(V) = u_ref(V − δ). The redistribution alternative adds β[Φ((V − 1.18)/0.13) − Φ((V − 1.56)/0.13)], where Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. These broad potential terms are phenomenological; β is not an experimentally measured phase fraction. Model parameters are fitted to C2 only, then evaluated against the measured treatment C3 curve conditional on the same-cycle reference C3 curve. The common delithiation voltage grid is 0.35–1.65 V (100 points); endpoint extrapolation is excluded. These are comparisons of observed curve shape, not independent absolute-cycle forecasts.

## S10. Potential-matched voltage relaxation and conditional waveform shape

The primary operational t₆₃ uses the first crossing of 63.212% of the signed 3–3600 s recovery measured from the 3 s reference. A separate prior contrast used a 3–3500 s grid (3 s followed by 10 s increments through 3500 s). To distinguish the two definitions, both were recomputed. Using the same C2-calibrated charge-distribution shift and shape-preserving interpolation of the t₆₃–QOCV trajectories over 0.35–1.65 V, milling increases the mean C3 delithiation t₆₃ contrast under either window (Table S10).
