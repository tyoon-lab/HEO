# Future Direction — Extending GITT / Phase-Transition Diagnostics from Oxides to Sulfides

**Date:** 2026-09-18
**Status:** Future collaborative research direction; not part of the current oxide manuscript.

## Core idea

The current HEO work is being developed around:
GITT current interruption -> separate polarization amplitude and relaxation time -> identify state-localized transition-associated polarization -> use a spatial transformation model to visualize internal reaction state.

A natural next collaborative extension is to sulfide-based electrode materials.

The broader question is:
> Can state-resolved GITT relaxation plus internal-state modeling distinguish transport limitation, phase transformation, and conversion/reconstruction kinetics across chemically different solid-state hosts?

## Why sulfides are attractive

Sulfides provide a useful contrast to oxides because metal-sulfur bonding, conversion pathways, phase-boundary mobility, interfacial energy, electronic/ionic transport, and structural reconstruction can differ substantially from metal-oxygen systems.

This creates a direct opportunity to test whether the diagnostic concepts developed in HEOs are material-specific or general.

## Candidate comparative observables

Retain the same experimental descriptors where appropriate:
- fast current-off descriptor
- finite-window relaxation amplitude
- t50 / t63 / t90
- state-localized excess polarization
- transition peak amplitude
- transition width in capacity/state
- modeled transformed-state/internal-state visualization

Do not assume oxide parameter values transfer to sulfides.

## Modeling extension

The current spatial architecture can serve as a starting point:
- conserved state variable c(r,t)
- nonconserved structural order parameter phi(r,t)
- stabilization/free-energy term
- gradient/interfacial term
- structural mobility
- distributed local transition conditions

For sulfides, the free-energy and reaction terms must be re-derived around the actual sulfide reaction pathway rather than copied mechanically from the oxide model.

Candidate questions:
1. Is phase-transition polarization more or less coupled to transport relaxation in sulfides?
2. Does transformed-state mobility differ substantially from oxide HEOs?
3. Is local transition heterogeneity broader or narrower?
4. Can GITT-derived visual simulation distinguish conversion-dominated and insertion-dominated sulfides?
5. Can the same diagnostics become a cross-chemistry electrochemical method?

## Strategic positioning

If successful, the sulfide collaboration could turn the HEO-specific analysis into a broader methodological program:

**GITT as an internal-state diagnostic for phase-evolving electrodes, not merely a diffusivity measurement.**

This broader program should be developed after the oxide manuscript is stabilized so the oxide study provides the first well-defined demonstration and the sulfide system becomes a cross-chemistry validation/extension.
