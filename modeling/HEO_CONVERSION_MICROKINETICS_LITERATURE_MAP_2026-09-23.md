# Conversion Microkinetics — Literature Map, 2026-09-23

## Purpose

Track prior work relevant to treating conversion reactions as a multi-step electrochemical kinetic network.

This is a working literature map, not yet the final manuscript reference list. Verify full citation metadata before manuscript insertion.

## 1. Closest direct conversion-microkinetic precedent

Alsaç et al., ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces (2026)  
DOI: **10.1021/acsami.5c20956**

Why relevant:
- explicit microkinetic modeling of conversion cathodes including S, FeS2, and FeF3;
- reaction networks + Butler–Volmer kinetics;
- species evolution, transport/passivation;
- distinguishes initial and subsequent reaction pathways.

Difference from current HEO direction:
- their emphasis is species/voltage/pressure evolution;
- the present HEO idea specifically links multi-step conversion microkinetics to **current-interruption relaxation amplitude, timescale, and cycle evolution**.

## 2. FeF3 GITT / conversion overpotential

JACS (2016)  
DOI: **10.1021/jacs.6b00061**

Why relevant:
- GITT and conversion overpotential;
- discusses contributions from new-phase nucleation/growth, mass transport, spatial heterogeneity, and nanophase interfacial penalties;
- useful precedent that conversion overpotential/relaxation cannot be reduced automatically to one charge-transfer resistance.

## 3. NiO nucleation / conversion

ACS Nano (2019)  
DOI: **10.1021/acsnano.9b02007**

Why relevant:
- potential-dependent nucleation barrier;
- theoretical conversion potential vs much lower potential needed for bulk conversion;
- supports an activation/nucleation/growth decomposition.

## 4. NiO heterogeneous nucleation / phase-front evolution

Nature Communications article: **ncomms4358**

Why relevant:
- first-cycle vs subsequent-cycle reaction behavior differs;
- heterogeneous nucleation and evolving phase fronts;
- useful for interpreting why first-cycle conversion-associated relaxation need not be stationary.

## 5. FeS2 continuum / P2D conversion modeling

Journal of Power Sources (2022)  
PII: **S0378775322008783**

Why relevant:
- conversion with changing active area, product shell resistance, transport, and rate limitation;
- shows why a single scalar kinetic constant is generally insufficient for conversion electrodes.

## 6. Literature gap to keep investigating

Potentially underdeveloped combination:

**elementary-step/RDS-style electrochemical microkinetics**
+
**current-interruption/open-circuit internal-state relaxation**
+
**cycle-dependent amplitude/timescale evolution**

Do not make a “first” claim yet.

If expanded experimentally:

- use current-on polarization + current-off relaxation jointly to narrow mechanism space.

Potential broad question:

## 8. First-cycle vs subsequent-cycle conversion: working precedents

These references are especially relevant to the Figure 6 history-dependence interpretation. Full bibliographic metadata should be verified before manuscript insertion.

### NiO — first vs later conversion polarization / heterogeneous nucleation

Nature Communications article **ncomms4358**

Working relevance:
- first-cycle reduction occurs at substantially lower potential than later cycles;
- heterogeneous nucleation and phase-front evolution are directly relevant to the idea that the first conversion state differs from later cycling;
- supports caution against interpreting a first-cycle hump as a stationary kinetic fingerprint.

### Co3O4 — first vs subsequent discharge plateau shift

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces (2023)  
DOI: **10.1021/acsami.3c11795**

Working relevance:
- first-cycle and subsequent-cycle conversion plateaus shift;
- structural/irreversible transformation is discussed as part of the cycle dependence.

### Fe2O3 — first vs later reduction peak shift

Scientific Reports article **srep07452**

Working relevance:
- first-cycle reduction peak shifts in subsequent cycling;
- another example that initial conversion and later repeated conversion do not share one fixed voltage signature.

### Mg-containing HEO conversion pathway

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces (2020)  
DOI: **10.1021/acsami.0c13161**

Working relevance:
- Mg-containing HEO conversion is reported as incomplete/irreversible in the first cycle;
- later-cycle voltage behavior differs from the initial conversion pathway;
- directly relevant to the present observation that strong first-cycle Mg relaxation suppression does not persist as an equally strong later-cycle t63 difference.

### HEO first-conversion structural history

Nature Communications (2023)  
DOI/Article: **10.1038/s41467-023-37034-6**

Working relevance:
- original HEO structure is strongly altered by the first conversion;
- subsequent cycling occurs through a metal/oxide-derived nanoscale state rather than a simple restoration of the pristine starting material;
- supports the generic term `cycled state` / `post-first-cycle state`, while the present manuscript should avoid claiming a specifically resolved reconstructed structure without direct evidence.

## 9. Interpretation rule for these cycle-dependent references

Use them to support the general proposition that:

**initial conversion and subsequent repeated conversion can follow different structural/kinetic pathways and polarization landscapes.**

Do not use literature voltage shifts as direct proof that the entire shift is a kinetic overpotential change; equilibrium potentials, phases, interfaces, and reaction pathways can also change after the first conversion.
